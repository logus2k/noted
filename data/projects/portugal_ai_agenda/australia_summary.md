# Australia — Accelerating Australia's AI Agenda (BCA, Jun 2025) + National AI Plan (Nov 2025) + AI Plan for the Australian Public Service (Nov 2025)

This summary covers **three complementary Australian documents** that together form a strategic-plus-operational stack analogous to (but more layered than) the UK's:

| Document | Date | Role | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Accelerating Australia's AI Agenda** | June 2025 | **Industry-side strategic agenda** with 16 Actions (Business Council of Australia, non-government) | [australia_accelerating_australia_ai_agenda.pdf](../../domains/national_ai_agendas/sources/australia_accelerating_australia_ai_agenda.pdf), 44 pages |
| **National AI Plan** | November 2025 | **Government-of-Australia strategic plan**, 3 goals × 9 Actions (Dept. of Industry, Science and Resources) | [australia_national_ai_plan.pdf](../../domains/national_ai_agendas/sources/australia_national_ai_plan.pdf), 37 pages |
| **AI Plan for the Australian Public Service** | November 2025 | **Operational APS playbook**, 3 pillars × 11 deliverables (Dept. of Finance + DTA + APSC) | [australia_ai_plan_for_public_service.pdf](../../domains/national_ai_agendas/sources/australia_ai_plan_for_public_service.pdf), 30 pages |

**BCA (Accelerating):** Published June 2025 by the **Business Council of Australia**, an industry peak body (ABN 75 008 483 216). Industry-led, not a government instrument; positions itself as a "positive vision to make Australia a globally recognised AI leader by 2028" [Accelerating p.1]. Contents structured around 16 Actions across 6 areas. Released ahead of, and explicitly intended to shape, the still-pending National AI Capability Plan referenced inside the document [Accelerating p.9].

**National AI Plan (NAP):** Foreword by **Senator the Hon Tim Ayres** (Minister for Industry and Innovation; Minister for Science) and **The Hon Dr Andrew Charlton MP** (Assistant Minister for Science, Technology and the Digital Economy) [NAP p.5]. Whole-of-economy plan that complements but is distinct from defence/intelligence/law-enforcement arrangements [NAP p.2]. Acknowledgement of Country and Indigenous Data Sovereignty framing throughout [NAP p.3]. CC BY 4.0 licence.

**APS AI Plan:** Foreword by **Senator the Hon Katy Gallagher** (Minister for Finance) [APS p.4]. Jointly owned by the **Department of Finance + Digital Transformation Agency (DTA) + Australian Public Service Commission (APSC)** [APS p.12]. ISBN 978-1-925205-75-6. Aligned with the broader National AI Plan; both released within the same week (NAP and APS Plan both November 2025).

The three documents form **a deliberate stack**: BCA (industry view + Capability Plan input) → National AI Plan (whole-of-economy strategy) → APS AI Plan (public-service operational playbook). Citations below use the prefixes `[Accelerating p.x]`, `[NAP p.x]`, `[APS p.x]`.

## Strategic framing — "AI maker, not AI taker" + "Capture, Spread, Keep Safe"

### BCA Accelerating (industry framing)
> "We must focus our efforts in six areas: adoption, skills, regulation, infrastructure, data, and R&D." [Accelerating p.3]

Quotes the UK Action Plan verbatim — "an aim to become an 'AI maker' not merely an 'AI taker'" [Accelerating p.10] — and adopts that positioning explicitly. Vision: "By 2028, Australia will be a global leader in AI – shaping trusted, transformative technologies that power economic growth, improve lives, and build a more resilient nation." [Accelerating p.11]

BCA's positioning notes Australia has **largely missed the first major economic opportunity of AI** (generative AI / LLMs) and that **the era dominated by LLMs is already giving way to agentic AI** — so the strategy is partly fast-follower / smart-adapter [Accelerating p.22].

Phased over 3 financial years:
- **Year 1 (Jul 2025–Jun 2026)** — Establishing essential foundations (Actions 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 13)
- **Year 2 (Jul 2026–Jun 2027)** — Capacity building and infrastructure development (Actions 4, 6, 8, 14, 15, 16)
- **Year 3 (Jul 2027–Jun 2028)** — Advancing collaboration and optimising data utilisation (Actions 9, 10, 11, 12)

### National AI Plan (government framing)
> "AI for Australia: capturing the opportunities, spreading the benefits, keeping us safe." [NAP p.9]

Anchored in **3 goals × 9 Actions**:
- **Capture the opportunities** (Actions 1–3): infrastructure, capability, investment
- **Spread the benefits** (Actions 4–6): adoption, skills, public services
- **Keep Australians safe** (Actions 7–9): mitigate harms, responsible practices, global norms

Ministers' foreword is explicit about ideological framing: "This government's approach to the transformative technologies of today is grounded in **timeless Labor values: fairness, inclusion and opportunity**. Success will rightly be measured by how widely the benefits of AI are shared, how inequalities are reduced, how workers and workforces can be supported, and how workplace rights are protected." [NAP p.5]

The Plan positions AI within Labor's broader **Future Made in Australia** agenda — "By building sovereign capability in AI, supporting local innovation and ensuring that Australian workers and businesses are equipped to lead in the global digital economy, we are laying the foundations for a more resilient and competitive Australia." [NAP p.5]

### APS AI Plan (operational framing)
> "Every public servant will have the training and guidance required to use generative AI safely and responsibly, alongside secure access to generative AI tools, and every agency will have a Chief AI Officer to drive adoption, with AI use tracked and reported on." [APS p.5]

3 pillars: **Trust** (transparency, ethics, governance) × **People** (capability building) × **Tools** (access, infrastructure). 11 named deliverables with explicit lead agencies and dates.

## Quantitative anchors

### Headline numbers (NAP)
- **$10 billion in data centre investment** in 2024 — Australia ranked **2nd globally** (after USA) [NAP p.6]
- **$100 billion+** in announced data centre investments 2023–2025, scaling to Project Southgate alone potentially **$73.3B** [NAP pp.10, 12]
- **$460 million in existing AI funding** consolidated under the Plan, plus **$1B National Reconstruction Fund** for critical tech, plus **$950M R&D Tax Incentive** AI-registered across 2022–24 [NAP p.13–14]
- **1.9% of world's AI research publications** (vs Australia's 0.3% of global population) [NAP p.6]
- **3rd globally in consumer use of Claude** (per-capita, Anthropic data) [NAP pp.6, 18]
- **$700M private investment in AI firms in 2024** [NAP p.6]
- **>1,500 AI companies** active in Australia [NAP p.6]
- **AI-skilled worker demand has tripled since 2015** [NAP p.6]

### Productivity targets (APS Plan)
- **Up to 4.3 per cent labour productivity growth over the next decade** in the market sector — Productivity Commission estimate — equivalent to **~$116 billion in GDP** [APS p.7]
- **Public sector gross value added up 13% by 2030** — **$19 billion in annual value** [APS p.7]

### BCA targets
- **Automation could add $600 billion annually** to Australia's economy [Accelerating p.9]
- **Generative AI's contribution: $45–115 billion annually by 2030** depending on adoption speed [Accelerating p.22]
- **R&D target: 3% of GDP by 2035** (currently below OECD average) [Accelerating p.36]
- **National compute target: 1,350 MW (2024) → 3,100+ MW by 2030** data centre capacity [Accelerating p.29]
- **Singapore AI for Science equivalent scaled per-capita: AUD $650M** [Accelerating p.35]

## BCA Accelerating Australia — 16 Actions across 6 areas

### Area 1: Adoption (Actions 1–2)
- **Action 1 (Year 1)** — Government as exemplar: pioneering AI in public services. Three sub-actions inspired by **US Executive Office memos M-25-21 and M-25-22** [Accelerating p.23]: (a) remove barriers to responsible AI use, agency-specific strategies within 6 months; (b) appoint **Chief AI Officers (CAIOs) within 2 months and AI Governance Boards within 3** in every Commonwealth agency, with a CAIO Council government-wide; (c) AI risk management policies for high-risk uses.
- **Action 2 (Year 1)** — Boost funding for the **National AI Centre (NAIC)** (established 2021, currently funded at $21.6M over 4 years from 2024-25). Push to expand to deliver AI implementation grants and build on the AI Adopt Centres.

### Area 2: Skills (Actions 3–4)
- **Action 3 (Year 1)** — Create a **National AI Skills Compact** modelled on NSW's Digital Skills and Workforce Compact, with two streams: (a) national AI apprenticeship model drawing on **Germany's dual VET system** and **Singapore's AI Apprenticeship Programme (AIAP)**; (b) scale up industry-led AI microcredentials based on **NSW Institute of Applied Technology Digital**. Calls for ≥20% of entry-level AI roles to non-traditional pathways.
- **Action 4 (Year 2)** — Reform AI skills development pathways. Embed AI literacy across school, university, VET and lifelong learning.

### Area 3: Regulation (Actions 5–6)
- **Action 5 (Year 1)** — Implement clear, risk-based regulations. Conduct review of existing legislation first (BCA submits this has not yet been adequately completed); only enact framework legislation if gaps found. **Explicitly rejects EU AI Act model** as targeting foundation models and imposing burdens that disadvantage SMEs and start-ups [Accelerating pp.26-27].
- **Action 6 (Year 2)** — Establish the **Australian AI Safety Institute (AAISI)** as a non-regulatory public-private body focused on technical standards, evaluation methodologies, and frontier-safety research. Partners internationally with AISI counterparts in **US, UK, Japan, South Korea, Canada, France and Singapore** [Accelerating p.28].

### Area 4: Infrastructure (Action 7)
- **Action 7 (Year 1)** — Simplify data centre development. "Speed-to-build" framing; **two months is becoming best practice globally** for planning approval [Accelerating p.29]. Public-private council across federal/state/territory/local with power grid upgrades and sustainable water solutions in parallel.

### Area 5: Data (Actions 8–11)
- **Action 8 (Year 2)** — **Reform copyright through the National AI Capability Plan**. Shift work from **Attorney-General's Department** (legalistic/harms framing) to **DISR** (opportunity framing). Create **text and data mining (TDM) exception** with rightsholder opt-out [Accelerating p.32]. Explicit call to follow EU TDM exception + UK Action Plan path. **Direct conflict with NAP position** (which rules out TDM exception, see [NAP p.29]).
- **Action 9 (Year 3)** — Expand frameworks for public-private data access and sharing. Build on existing **DATA Scheme**; "open by default" policy modelled on UK [Accelerating p.32].
- **Action 10 (Year 3)** — **Establish the Australian National Data Library (ANDL)** modelled on UK's National Data Library. Federated model with data staying decentralised but accessible through common infrastructure [Accelerating p.32].
- **Action 11 (Year 3)** — Create secure private sector data collaboration frameworks. References **EU Gaia-X, iSHARE, Dutch JoinData** as models [Accelerating p.33]; sandbox model from Australia's Consumer Data Right.

### Area 6: R&D and innovation (Actions 12–16)
- **Action 12 (Year 3)** — Empower researchers + enhance research infrastructure. FAIR data principles for publicly funded research; reliable HPC access. Calls for **AUD $650M scaled equivalent of Singapore's AI for Science** [Accelerating p.35].
- **Action 13 (Year 1)** — Establish the **AI Research Consortium (AIRC)** modelled on Belgium's **IMEC**. Industry + academia co-located for pre-competitive AI research [Accelerating p.35].
- **Action 14 (Year 2)** — Fix R&D settings; modernise **Research and Development Tax Incentive (RDTI)** to align with **OECD Frascati Manual**, recognising software/iterative AI model development. Target **3% GDP R&D by 2035**, 10-year national R&D strategy.
- **Action 15 (Year 2)** — Create the **AI Commercialisation Accelerator (AICA)** modelled on **UK Catapult Network**. Shared infrastructure + technical capability + commercialisation support in clean energy, advanced manufacturing, digital health, agriculture [Accelerating p.36].
- **Action 16 (Year 2)** — Establish a national **AI missions program** modelled on **Japan's Moonshot R&D initiative**. Works with NAIC, AIRC, AICA to align public and private investment [Accelerating p.37].

## National AI Plan — 9 Actions across 3 goals

### Capture the opportunities
- **Action 1: Build smart infrastructure** [NAP pp.10–12]. National data centre principles (sustainability, renewable energy, water efficiency); NBN expansion incl. LEO satellite + mobile coverage; coordinated approvals across states; mapping national compute. **Investor Front Door + Major Project Facilitation Agency** for major investments [NAP p.16]. Sustainable cooling cited via **CDC Data Centres** case study (LiquidCore closed-loop, near-zero water; ~47% Commonwealth-linked ownership via Future Fund + CSC).
- **Action 2: Back Australian AI capability** [NAP pp.13–15]. New **'AI Accelerator' funding round of the Cooperative Research Centres (CRC)** program. **GovAI** as sovereign AI hosting service for public sector. Explore opportunities to unlock high-value datasets including **ABS economic datasets for AI training**. Case study: **Harrison.ai** healthcare diagnostics ($32M NRFC equity investment Jan 2025); **AI and Indigenous Knowledge: Managing Kakadu's wetlands** (Bininj Traditional Owners + CSIRO + Parks Australia + Microsoft Custom Vision).
- **Action 3: Attract investment** [NAP p.16]. Austrade promoting AI investment; superannuation funds + foreign direct investment. Project Southgate, Amazon $20B, Microsoft $5B all referenced.

### Spread the benefits
- **Action 4: Scale AI adoption** [NAP pp.18–20]. NAIC consolidating Adopt Program ($17M). Practical tools: **Guidance for AI Adoption (NAIC 2025)**; First Nations Digital Support Hub + Network of Digital Mentors; Infoxchange Digital Transformation Hub for not-for-profits (>20,000 organisations supported). Explicit **regional–metro divide note: 29% regional vs 40% metro AI adoption** [NAP p.18]. **Indigenous Data Sovereignty** anchored to NIAA Framework for Governance of Indigenous Data.
- **Action 5: Support and train Australians** [NAP pp.21–24]. National Skills Agreement (NSA); Future Skills Organisation FSO Skills Accelerator – AI; Next Generation Graduates Program ($47M); Jobs and Skills Councils sector-specific AI gaps; **TAFE Responsible AI microcredentials >150,000 enrolments**. Workers'-voice / union-consultation framing pervasive. **Generative AI Capacity Study (Aug 2025)** by Jobs and Skills Australia.
- **Action 6: Improve public services** [NAP pp.25–26]. **GovAI** centralised AI hosting; AI-triage lung cancer detection from 16% to 64% stage-1 (NLCSP); Department of Veterans' Affairs AI-enhanced search; National Library oral history transcription of **58,000 hours**; Tiwi Islands Ranger Ghost Nets Program (AI + drones). Directly references the APS AI Plan as the operational layer.

### Keep Australians safe
- **Action 7: Mitigate harms** [NAP pp.28–31]. Targeted action against specific harms via existing technology-neutral laws + sector-specific regulators. **Ruled out a text and data mining exception** in Australian copyright law to protect creators and media workers [NAP p.29] — directly opposite to BCA's Action 8 recommendation. Modernising **Privacy Act 1988**. Establishing the **AI Safety Institute (AISI)** with international partnerships (Bletchley/Seoul declarations).
- **Action 8: Promote responsible practices** [NAP pp.32–33]. **Guidance for AI Adoption (NAIC 2025)** — 6 essential practices. **Being clear about AI-generated content** guide (NAIC 2025): labelling + watermarking + metadata recording. APRA + ASIC sector-specific guidance.
- **Action 9: Partner on global norms** [NAP pp.34–35]. Bletchley/Seoul/Paris signatory; UN Global Digital Compact; Hiroshima AI Process; Global Partnership on AI; G7 Energy and AI Work Plan. Bilateral MoU with **Singapore**; Framework Arrangement with **India**; strategic partnerships with **UK and Republic of Korea**; **bilateral Technology Prosperity Deal with the United States** (AI, quantum, critical technologies). DFAT + DISR will publish an **Australian Government Strategy for International Engagement and Regional Leadership on AI**.

## APS AI Plan — 3 pillars × 11 deliverables

Owned jointly by **Finance + DTA + APSC** [APS p.12]. Two milestones: July 2026 and July 2026+ [APS p.9].

### Trust (4 deliverables, lead: DTA or Finance)
- **AI in government policy and guidance updates** (DTA, Dec 2025) — strengthen accountability + mandate AI impact assessment tool for in-scope use cases [APS p.14]
- **AI Review Committee** (DTA, Mid 2026 → Full maturity early 2027) — whole-of-government oversight; meets every 6 weeks; cross-disciplinary scrutiny including Australian Information Commissioner, Privacy Commissioner, Commonwealth Ombudsman; non-binding advice on high-risk uses [APS p.15]
- **Clear expectations of external service providers** (DTA, by end-2025) — Management Advisory Services + People Panels suppliers must advise of any AI use; Commonwealth Contracting Suite + ClauseBank clauses for transparency/accountability [APS p.15]
- **AI strategic communication initiatives** (Finance, early 2026) — central messaging framework; CAIO cohort to operationalise [APS p.15]

### People (4 deliverables, lead: APSC or Finance)
- **Foundational learning** (APSC, late 2025–2026) — mandatory AI fundamentals training for ALL APS staff; 12-month rollout; DTA module via APS Academy; senior leader masterclasses [APS p.18]
- **Staff consultation and engagement** (APSC, end-2025/early-2026) — Circular with consultation standards aligned with APS Enterprise Agreements; explicit role for unions [APS p.19]
- **AI delivery and enablement (AIDE)** (Finance, 2026 staged) — central function to accelerate adoption; resolve compliance uncertainties; bring core work back into public service [APS p.19]
- **Chief AI Officers** (Finance, all agencies by 2026) — SES-level leaders in every agency to drive adoption; supported by AI Community of Practice; **separate from but coordinated with AI Accountable Officials** (governance/compliance role mandated under existing policy) [APS p.19]

### Tools (7 deliverables, lead: Finance or DTA)
- **GovAI: centrally hosted AI services** (Finance, late 2026 milestones throughout 2026) — vendor-agnostic model brokerage including **onshore OpenAI GPT instance**; use case library; shared knowledge stores [APS p.22]
- **GovAI Chat** (Finance, mid-2026 beta) — universally accessible secure chat to all APS staff [APS p.23]
- **Guidance on public and enterprise AI services** (DTA/Home Affairs, completed Oct 2025) — Protective Security Policy Framework Policy Advisory 1 — use of OFFICIAL information with public generative AI (ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini) [APS p.23]
- **Support for AI tool procurement** (DTA, Dec 2025) — AI-specific subcategories in **BuyICT.gov.au + Digital Marketplace**; AI Model Contract published Oct 2025; future consideration of **gender equity objectives** in AI/STEM procurement [APS pp.23–24]
- **Re-using intellectual property** (Finance, late 2026) — upgraded **GovAI Use Case Library** (already 20 detailed APS use cases); explicit reference to **UK i.AI Incubator open-source GitHub** as analogue [APS p.24]
- **Central register of generative AI assessments** (Finance, mid-2026) — share FOCI, IRAP, cyber, impact assessments across agencies [APS pp.24–25]
- **New whole-of-government cloud policy** (DTA, Dec 2025 completed) [APS p.25]

## Distinctive mechanisms

### Three-document strategic-plus-operational stack
Unique to Australia in this comparator set: **industry whitepaper (BCA) → government strategic plan (NAP) → public-service operational plan (APS)**, with deliberate cross-references. The BCA paper was published 5 months ahead of the government plans and explicitly aimed at shaping the still-pending **National AI Capability Plan**. The two November 2025 government plans publish 6 days apart (NAP 12 Nov, APS 12 Nov) and explicitly co-reference each other.

### Indigenous Data Sovereignty
Pervasive throughout NAP and APS Plan; anchored to **NIAA Framework for Governance of Indigenous Data** [NAP pp.18, 25; APS pp.16, 18]. Case studies (Kakadu Bininj Traditional Owners; Tiwi Islands Ranger Ghost Nets Program). No equivalent framing in any other comparator (Portugal, France, UK, Sweden, Spain, etc.). This is a sovereign-rights design constraint, not just an ethics principle.

### Five Eyes / AUKUS / Indo-Pacific anchoring
BCA explicitly notes Australia's **Five Eyes intelligence-sharing arrangement (US + UK + Canada + NZ) and AUKUS (with US + UK) including AI and autonomy work stream** as giving Australia "privileged access to AI technology sharing and development initiatives" [Accelerating p.9]. NAP positions Australia as the **partner of choice in the Indo-Pacific** for trusted digital infrastructure [NAP p.34], with 15 international submarine cables [NAP p.11]. No comparator outside the Anglosphere has this exact framing.

### CAIO + AI Accountable Official separation
APS Plan formally separates two roles: **Chief AI Officer** (adoption + strategic change) and **AI Accountable Official** (governance + compliance with existing policy) — though small agencies may combine [APS p.19]. Mandated CAIO per agency by 2026 [APS Appendix A p.27]. UK does not formalise this duality; Italy and Spain rely on coordination bodies rather than agency-level officers.

### GovAI = sovereign onshore platform with vendor-agnostic model brokerage
GovAI is structurally distinct from Spain's ALIA or Portugal's AMALIA (both of which are sovereign LLM efforts). GovAI is **not a sovereign LLM** — it is **a brokerage platform** that hosts vendor models onshore (incl. OpenAI GPT) under Australian data sovereignty, plus shared infrastructure, use case library, IP reuse [APS p.22]. This is a fundamentally different design: it solves data sovereignty without requiring foundation-model training.

### Explicit anti-EU regulatory stance
BCA explicitly argues against the EU AI Act model: "regulating the high-risk application to regulating the underlying technology imposes substantial burdens and is something Australia must avoid" [Accelerating p.27]. NAP softens this but maintains targeted/sector-based regulation built on existing technology-neutral laws + sector-specific regulators [NAP p.28]. Australia is the only comparator that explicitly distances from the EU AI Act approach while still committing to international forums (Bletchley/Seoul/Hiroshima).

### Disagreement within the stack — copyright TDM
BCA Action 8 calls for a **text and data mining (TDM) exception with rightsholder opt-out** [Accelerating p.32]. National AI Plan Action 7 has **ruled out a TDM exception** to protect creators and media workers [NAP p.29]. This is a live, public, unresolved disagreement within Australia's AI stack and represents how the government has acted contrary to the most-recent industry-side recommendation.

### Productivity Commission as quantitative anchor
Both NAP and APS Plan ground their benefit estimates in **Productivity Commission analysis**: $116B GDP from 4.3% market-sector labour productivity over a decade; $19B/year in public-sector value-added by 2030 (Merom, 2025) [APS p.7]. No other comparator names an equivalent statutory independent productivity body.

### AISI vs AAISI tension
- **NAP's AI Safety Institute (AISI)** [NAP pp.28–32] — government body, focused on upstream risks + downstream harms, supports regulators, statutory positioning unclear.
- **BCA's Australian AI Safety Institute (AAISI)** [Accelerating p.28] — **public-private partnership** with **statutory independence**, non-regulatory, technical-only, builds export market for AI assurance services.

Government has built the AISI route; BCA's AAISI design is materially different but not adopted as proposed.

## What Australia does that Portugal's ANIA does NOT

### Strategic-plus-operational stack
Portugal has ANIA (strategic only). Australia has 3 layers: industry whitepaper + government strategic plan + public-service operational plan, each named, dated, with explicit ownership. Portugal lacks both the industry-input layer and the operational-AP-playbook layer.

### Productivity Commission anchor
Portugal's ANIA cites GDP targets (€18–22B/yr add, 2.7pp productivity) but does not name an independent statutory body whose modelling underlies the figures. Australia anchors all figures to the Productivity Commission [APS p.7].

### Every-public-servant training mandate
Australia: **every APS staff member must complete foundational AI literacy training** within 12 months of the policy update (Dec 2025 → Dec 2026) [APS p.18]. Portugal's ANIA III.1 covers general AI literacy but does not mandate universal training across PA with named deadline.

### Chief AI Officer per agency
Australia: SES-level CAIO in every Commonwealth agency by 2026 [APS p.19, Appendix A p.27]. Portugal's ANIA mentions PA centre of excellence (II.11) but does not formally create per-ministry AI officers.

### AI Review Committee
Australia: cross-disciplinary committee with Information Commissioner + Privacy Commissioner + Ombudsman doing **non-binding reviews of high-risk AI uses every 6 weeks** [APS p.15]. Portugal has no equivalent named cross-watchdog AI review body.

### GovAI as sovereign brokerage platform
Australia: vendor-agnostic onshore platform with central use case library + IP reuse + central register of FOCI/IRAP/cyber assessments [APS pp.22–25]. Portugal's AMALIA is a sovereign LLM ambition; there is no Portuguese-equivalent onshore-brokerage platform.

### Public/Indigenous data sovereignty framing
Australia weaves **Indigenous Data Sovereignty** through every Action; case studies (Kakadu, Tiwi). Portugal has no analogue (no Indigenous data constraint), but **Atlantic-Lusophone data sovereignty** would be a natural Portuguese analogue (Açores cultural archives, Lusophone language data) and is not articulated in ANIA.

### Risk-based regulation aligned with sectoral regulators
Australia explicitly **does not pass new framework legislation unless gaps found** [Accelerating p.27; NAP p.28]. Portugal/EU AI Act mandates an upfront framework regardless of gap analysis. This is a defensible alternative model that ANIA could examine for non-AI-Act-scope domains.

### Explicit named investment numbers
Australia: **$460M existing AI funding + $1B NRF tech + $950M RDTI AI-registered** [NAP pp.13–14]. Per BCA: 3% R&D target by 2035, Singapore AI-for-Science scaled equivalent $650M. Portugal ANIA mentions €18–22B GDP target but does not pre-commit named line-item AI funding totals at the same granularity.

### CRC AI Accelerator program
Australia: new **'AI Accelerator' funding round of the Cooperative Research Centres** program [NAP p.14]. Portugal has CoLAB / CMU Portugal / MIT Portugal structures but no AI-named accelerator funding round announced for ANIA.

### Procurement instrument changes
Australia: **BuyICT.gov.au AI subcategories**, AI Model Contract (Oct 2025), Commonwealth ClauseBank clauses for AI [APS pp.23–24]. Portugal has CCP (Código dos Contratos Públicos) but no published AI-specific contracting addendum.

### Indo-Pacific positioning + Tech Prosperity Deal
Australia explicitly positions as Indo-Pacific AI partner of choice, with **bilateral Technology Prosperity Deal with USA + MoU Singapore + Framework Arrangement India + partnerships UK and ROK** [NAP pp.34–35]. Portugal's natural analogue would be **Atlantic / Lusophone partnerships** (CPLP, Brazil, Angola, Mozambique, Cabo Verde) and these are mentioned in ANIA but without bilateral instrument-level commitments.

## What Portugal's ANIA does that Australia does NOT (or does less)

### EU AI Act anchoring
Portugal cleanly anchors to EU AI Act + GDPR. Australia explicitly distances from EU AI Act and relies on existing technology-neutral law + sector regulators. For Portugal, EU AI Act compliance is a binding given; for Australia, it is an active negative reference. **This is a feature, not a bug, for Portugal** — single coherent regulatory regime across EU27.

### Single coherent national document
Portugal's ANIA is one 22-page document. Australia has 3 documents totalling 111 pages with internal tensions (TDM exception, AAISI vs AISI). Portugal's coherence is an asset for stakeholder communication.

### Sovereign LLM with named identity
Portugal: **AMALIA** as named Portuguese LLM ambition (II.7). Australia: GovAI is a brokerage, not a sovereign LLM. Australia's NAP gestures at "Australian-developed models" [NAP p.13] but does not name one. Portugal has a stronger sovereign-LLM identity story for the Portuguese-speaking world (~250M speakers).

### 6 named guiding principles
Portugal: 6 named guiding principles (Ethics, Sustainability, Inclusion, etc.) explicitly listed. Australia: Labor values "fairness, inclusion and opportunity" [NAP p.5] are stated but not enumerated as a principle set. Portugal's enumeration is more compact and citable.

### Sector-pillar architecture
Portugal has 4 named pillars × 32 initiatives, each owned. Australia spreads across 3 documents with overlapping ownership (DISR for NAP; Finance/DTA/APSC for APS Plan; BCA for industry). Portugal's pillar architecture is cleaner for delivery tracking.

### Sub-1-page ANIA-on-a-page
Portugal's ANIA "on a page" is a single page. Australia has a NAP "on a page" (Figure 1, [NAP p.9]) but the overall stack requires reading 111 pages to fully grasp.

## Critique-lens conclusion

Australia provides **the closest analogue in the comparator set to what Portugal's ANIA could mature into**: a strategic-plus-operational stack with explicit per-agency operational ownership and Productivity-Commission-anchored quantitative targets. However, Australia's stack is **not single-document coherent** — there are live internal tensions (TDM exception, AAISI vs AISI, framework legislation timing) that Portugal could avoid by drafting all three layers in coordination.

### Top moves Portugal should import

1. **Publish a Portuguese "Plano de IA para a Administração Pública" (PIAAP)** alongside ANIA — the direct analogue of Australia's APS AI Plan. Lead: AMA + DGAEP + Centro para a IA Responsável. Trust/People/Tools 3-pillar structure. Mandatory AI fundamentals training for every public servant within 12 months of publication. **This is the single highest-leverage import** for ANIA delivery — Portugal currently has only the strategic layer.

2. **Adopt the "Capture, Spread, Keep Safe" goal triad** as named ANIA framing alongside the existing 4 pillars × 32 initiatives. This goal-triad triages the 32 initiatives into action streams that are easier to communicate publicly. Portugal's current 4-pillar architecture optimises for delivery tracking; Capture/Spread/Keep-Safe optimises for communication.

3. **Establish a Portuguese Chief AI Officer (CAIO) per ministry** under ANIA II.11, with explicit separation from the AI Accountable Official role (the latter responsible for EU AI Act compliance, the former for adoption + strategic change). Match Australia's role architecture: CAIO drives transformation, Accountable Official manages risk register.

4. **Create "GovAI Portugal" as a vendor-agnostic onshore AI brokerage platform**, distinct from AMALIA. GovAI Portugal hosts vendor models (OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral, etc.) on Portuguese-territory or EU-region infrastructure with national-government data sovereignty. AMALIA = sovereign Portuguese LLM ambition; GovAI Portugal = the platform every ministry actually uses day-to-day. This pair architecture solves both the long-term LLM-sovereignty goal AND the immediate "every ministry needs ChatGPT-equivalent today" problem.

5. **Establish an AI Review Committee** under ANIA IV.4 with cross-watchdog membership: CNPD (Privacy), Provedor de Justiça (Ombudsman), CADA (Document Access Commission), and ERSE/ANACOM/ASAE sectoral regulators. Non-binding 6-weekly reviews of high-risk AI uses in PA. Aligned with EU AI Act Article 26 deployer obligations.

6. **Adopt the Productivity-Commission anchor model**. Portugal has the CFP (Conselho das Finanças Públicas) — empower it (or stand up an equivalent) to produce annual AI Productivity Reports modelling labour productivity uplift from AI adoption. Portugal's ANIA quantitative targets (€18–22B, 2.7pp) need a recognised independent statutory body backing them.

7. **Publish a Portuguese "AI Adoption Tracker"** modelled on NAIC's AI Adoption Tracker [NAP p.8]. Quarterly publication; SME-segmented; regional/metropolitan split. Portugal has no comparable public dataset.

8. **Establish bilateral Tech Prosperity arrangements**: a Portugal-Brazil Tech Prosperity Deal (priority); Portugal-CPLP (Comunidade de Países de Língua Portuguesa) AI Framework Arrangement (extends ANIA's Lusophone framing); Portugal-Singapore MoU (smallest-economy Lusophone AI hub model). Currently ANIA mentions Lusophone cooperation but without instrument-level commitments.

9. **AI-specific procurement subcategories in BASE.gov.pt + AT (Autoridade Tributária)**, plus a Portuguese AI Model Contract (analogue to Australia's Oct 2025 contract). Build on existing CCP framework with AI-specific addendum.

10. **Central register of AI impact assessments** shared across PA (modelled on APS Plan's FOCI/IRAP register). Helps every ministry reuse the legal/security/privacy assessment work done by the first ministry to deploy a given vendor.

11. **National AI Skills Compact** with industry + academia + government, modelled on the BCA's Action 3. Cross-references ANIA III initiatives but with industry-co-designed microcredentials + apprenticeship streams (German dual-VET + Singapore AIAP models).

12. **AI Accelerator funding round of the existing CoLAB programme**. Adapt the Australian CRC AI Accelerator template into Portugal's CoLABs. Lead: FCT + ANI. Per-CoLAB AI commercialisation grants targeting health, agriculture, blue economy, advanced manufacturing.

13. **Foundation Atlantic Data Library (FADL)** — Portuguese analogue of Australia's ANDL or UK's NDL. Anchored to Atlantic-Lusophone geographic and cultural data assets; federated across PT institutions; designed to be open-by-default for non-sensitive data.

14. **Resolve the EU TDM stance explicitly in ANIA** — Portugal benefits from the EU CDSM Directive Article 4 TDM exception with opt-out. ANIA should explicitly name this as Portugal's working position (not contested like Australia's). This protects creators while permitting AI training.

15. **Portuguese AI Missions Programme** modelled on Japan's Moonshot + Australia's national AI missions [Accelerating p.37]. 3–5 missions, time-bound (10 years), outcome-driven. Natural picks: Atlantic-Lusophone LLM at frontier scale; Blue Economy AI; Climate AI (wildfire prediction + carbon flux); Health-Diagnostics AI; Heritage-AI (Portuguese language + culture).

### What Portugal should NOT copy

- **The 3-document fragmented structure**. Australia's stack has internal contradictions (BCA's TDM exception vs NAP's TDM ban; AAISI vs AISI). Portugal should draft any new operational-layer document (the PIAAP recommended above) in coordination with ANIA, not as a separate work stream.
- **Explicit anti-EU regulatory stance**. Portugal is correctly anchored to EU AI Act. BCA's anti-EU framing would be self-defeating for Portugal.
- **The Indigenous Data Sovereignty framing wholesale**. Portugal does not have an Indigenous population with the same legal-political status as Australia's First Nations. The Australian framing should inspire a Portuguese **Atlantic-Lusophone Data Sovereignty** equivalent for Portuguese-language data + cultural heritage data + diaspora data, but not be copied verbatim.
- **Five Eyes / AUKUS framing**. Not applicable to Portugal. NATO / EU defence + EU AI Office framing is the correct equivalent.

### Strategic complementarity with other comparators

Australia + UK together provide **the most complete strategic+operational template** in this set. Portugal should adopt:
- **UK structure** (strategic plan + operational playbook + documented case studies) [from `uk_summary.md`]
- **Australia operational layer specifics** (CAIO per agency + AI Review Committee + GovAI brokerage + Foundational training mandate) [this summary]
- **Spain budget format** (Tabla 6: per-initiative € + funding source) [from `spain_summary.md`]
- **France recommendation format** (per-recommendation Lead Ministry) [from `france_summary.md`]
- **Italy coordination model** (Fundação ANIA under PM Office) [from `italy_summary.md`]
- **Japan PDCA cycle** (annual review + iterative refinement) [from `japan_summary.md`]
- **Singapore AI Verify open-source toolkit** for trustworthy AI testing [from `singapore_summary.md`]

The Australian addition to this set is specifically: **(a) the every-public-servant training mandate, (b) the CAIO-per-agency model, (c) the central AI Review Committee, (d) the central register of AI impact assessments, and (e) the vendor-agnostic GovAI brokerage** — five concrete operational mechanisms that turn strategic plans into delivery.
