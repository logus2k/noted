Document number: **Guo Fa [2025] No. 11** (国发〔2025〕11号). Formulated 21 August 2025, published 26 August 2025.

**Original title:** 国务院关于深入实施"人工智能+"行动的意见 — *State Council Opinion on Deeply Implementing the "AI+" Action*.

**Issuing body:** State Council of the People's Republic of China — China's chief executive organ. Coordinating ministry: **National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC, 国家发展改革委)**.

## Document character

This is a **State Council "Opinion" (国务院意见)** — a strong policy directive binding all ministries, provinces, autonomous regions, direct-controlled municipalities, and SOEs, but not legislation in the Western sense. It is the **highest-level current AI-specific national plan** in China, superseding the 2017 "New Generation AI Development Plan" (新一代人工智能发展规划). The "+" framing is an explicit parallel to Premier Li Keqiang's 2015 "Internet+" (互联网+) initiative — signalling that AI is a horizontal integration across every sector, not a standalone industry.

The Opinion sits alongside, but is operationally above, several recent companion documents:
- **Global AI Governance Action Plan** (July 2025) — China's outward-facing diplomatic proposal
- **15th Five-Year Plan (2026-2030)** recommendations (announced October 2025) — places robotics and AI at the core of the modern industrial system
- **Cybersecurity Law amendment** (October 2025, effective January 2026) — first formal inclusion of AI in national cybersecurity law
- **2017 New Generation AI Development Plan** — predecessor, now superseded

## Vision and framing

**Strategic statement** [§I]:

> "Promote extensive and in-depth integration of artificial intelligence across economic and social sectors and industries, reshape human production and lifestyle patterns, and foster **revolutionary leaps in productivity and profound changes in production relations**."

The "production relations" framing is Marxist political-economy terminology repurposed under Xi for technology-led productivity (新质生产力 — "new productive forces"). This places AI within the same conceptual frame as steam power, electrification, and computing in classical historical-materialist analysis.

**People-centred development approach** + Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era frame the ideological positioning. **National strengths leveraged:**
- "Abundant data resources" (the population scale + state-collected data)
- "Complete industrial systems" (vertically integrated manufacturing base)
- "Expansive application scenarios" (the domestic market as testbed)

## Quantitative targets

| Year | Target |
|---|---|
| **2027** | Deep integration with **six priority sectors**; new-generation smart terminals and intelligent entities **>70% adoption**; AI core industries expand rapidly; AI strengthens public governance |
| **2030** | AI comprehensively empowers high-quality development; smart terminal/intelligent-entity adoption **>90%**; intelligent economy becomes significant growth driver |
| **2035** | Full transition to intelligent economy and society development phase; supports basic socialist modernization |

These are **the most aggressive AI adoption targets in the entire 12-country comparator set**. By 2030, 90% smart-terminal/intelligent-entity adoption is a stock-of-deployment target that exceeds even UK's £400B-by-2030 framing.

No aggregate budget figure is published — typical of Chinese State Council Opinions, which signal direction rather than committing specific appropriations (those follow in subsequent ministry-level implementation plans and 5-year-plan allocations).

## Structure — Four sections × Six priority actions × Eight foundational supports

| Section | Coverage |
|---|---|
| **I. Overall Requirements** | Strategic framework, ideological positioning, three quantitative milestone targets |
| **II. Implementing Priority Actions** | Six "AI+" domains (six pillars) |
| **III. Strengthen Foundational Support Capacity** | Eight enabling mechanisms |
| **IV. Implementation** | Party leadership, NDRC coordination, regional adaptation |

### Six "AI+" priority domains [§II]

| # | Domain | Sub-actions |
|---|---|---|
| (I) | **AI + Science and Technology** | Scientific discovery, R&D innovation, philosophy/social-science methods |
| (II) | **AI + Industrial Development** | Intelligent native models; industry-wide intelligent development; agricultural digital-smart transformation; service industry innovation |
| (III) | **AI + Consumer Quality Enhancement** | Service consumption scenarios; product consumption new industries (smart vehicles, AI smartphones, robots, smart homes, wearables, AI+metaverse/low-altitude flight/3D printing/BCI) |
| (IV) | **AI + Public Welfare** | Intelligent working methods; learning methods; quality of life (health, culture, eldercare, childcare, disability, fitness, housing) |
| (V) | **AI + Governance Capacity** | Social governance human-machine coexistence; multi-stakeholder public safety; "Beautiful China" ecological governance |
| (VI) | **AI + Global Cooperation** | AI inclusive sharing (Global South capacity-building); co-construction of global AI governance framework via UN |

### Eight foundational supports [§III]

| # | Support | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| (VII) | Model Foundation Capabilities | Diverse architecture innovation, efficient training/inference, model evaluation systems |
| (VIII) | Data Supply Innovation | High-quality AI datasets; data property rights; value-contribution-based compensation; data annotation/synthesis |
| (IX) | **Intelligent Computing Power Coordination** | AI chips; ultra-large computing clusters; **"Eastern Data, Western Computing" (东数西算)** national programme; equitable/efficient/green/safe supply |
| (X) | Application Development Environment | National pilot testing bases; **Model-as-Service (MaaS), Intelligent-Entity-as-Service (AaaS)**; standards coordination |
| (XI) | **Open-Source Ecosystem** | Open-source community development; **integrate open-source contributions into university credit systems and faculty recognition**; globally-influential projects |
| (XII) | Talent Development | K-12 through higher ed; public AI literacy; extraordinary leadership; **equity/options enterprise incentive mechanisms** |
| (XIII) | Policy and Legal Protections | **Long-term capital, patient capital, strategic capital**; legal frameworks; ethical guidelines; safety assessment + registration |
| (XIV) | Safety Capacity | Models/algorithms/data/infrastructure/apps; **black-box, hallucination, algorithmic bias** risks; **"inclusive but prudent" (包容审慎)** classification-based regulation |

## Distinctive Chinese features (vs. the other 11 comparators)

### 1. The "+" integration metaphor

No other comparator uses this **horizontal integration framing**. Portugal's ANIA implicitly does horizontal integration across its 32 initiatives but doesn't make it the headline; China explicitly names the "+" as the strategic vehicle. The "+" signals: AI is not a separate sector but a layer that joins to every other sector (AI+manufacturing, AI+agriculture, AI+education, AI+healthcare, AI+governance, AI+global cooperation).

### 2. Global Cooperation as a peer pillar

Six of the eleven other comparators treat international cooperation as one initiative among many (Portugal IV.7 — one sentence; Spain's EU-LAC outreach; France's World AI Organization proposal). **China elevates it to a full pillar** (AI+ Global Cooperation, §II.VI). Two sub-actions:
- **AI Inclusive Sharing** — positions AI as international public good; Global South capacity-building; bridges global AI divides
- **Co-construction of Global AI Governance Framework** — supports UN leadership; alignment with international organisations

This is a **direct counter-positioning to US-led AI governance** and to G7/Bletchley/UK AI Safety Summit forums. China's framing is "broad participation" via UN rather than alliance-led mechanisms.

### 3. Three-tier capital framing

"Long-term capital, patient capital, strategic capital" (长期资本、耐心资本、战略资本) [§XIII] is a **state-directed-investment philosophy** distinct from Western frames. It explicitly contrasts with venture-capital cycles — committing to multi-decade investment horizons appropriate to AI infrastructure build-out. Closest Western parallel: **France's €27B/5yr "France & AI" fund** (Aghion-Bouverot Commission Rec 12) and **UK's Sovereign AI unit** (Rec 50), both of which also reject "invisible hand" framings for activist industrial policy.

### 4. "Smart-for-good" (智向善) ethics

Distinct ethics framing from EU "trustworthy/human-centric AI" or US "free-speech/American-values." 智向善 ("smart-for-good") emphasises **collective benefit through guided development** rather than individual-rights-based protections. The phrase "build warmer intelligent society" [§II.IV.3] captures the framing — AI as a tool to strengthen community, family, eldercare, childcare, disability support.

### 5. "Inclusive but prudent" classification-based regulation (包容审慎)

[§XIV] — Risk-tier framework structurally similar to the EU AI Act (low/medium/high-risk classification) but with explicit developmental-state framing: risk management combined with proactive promotion. Includes:
- Algorithm auditing
- Data security
- Application transparency mechanisms
- Mandatory impact assessments
- "Government guidance + industry self-discipline" (vs. EU AI Act's externally-enforced compliance regime)

### 6. "Eastern Data, Western Computing" (东数西算)

National compute infrastructure programme leveraging China's geography — eastern coastal regions generate data demand, western interior regions supply compute capacity (cooler climate, renewable energy availability, lower land cost). **No equivalent geography-leveraging compute strategy in the other 11 comparators** — though Sweden's Polar Connect and the Iberian-French data-centre strategies have parallel logic at smaller scale.

### 7. Open-source contributions count for academic credit

[§XI] — "Integrate open-source contributions into university credit systems and faculty recognition." This is a **specific academic-policy intervention** to make open-source AI contributions count toward tenure and undergraduate degrees. No other comparator makes this explicit institutional move (France mentions open-source ecosystem support; UK supports open sourcing in code mandate; but neither ties it to academic credit).

### 8. Ultra-aggressive adoption targets (90% by 2030)

The 90% smart-terminal/intelligent-entity adoption target by 2030 is **the most aggressive in the comparator set**. Compare:
- UK: £400B AI adoption boost by 2030 (stock); 20× AIRR compute expansion
- France: doubled growth, €250-420B GDP by 2034
- Portugal: €18-22B/yr ongoing GDP add
- Spain: ~€2.6B cumulative budget; sector-by-sector targets

China's adoption target is a **scale of deployment**, not a financial figure — appropriate for a country with 1.4B population and a state-led economic model.

## Strategic posture relative to Portugal

### Where China and Portugal diverge fundamentally

1. **Geopolitical alignment**: Portugal is anchored in the **EU AI Continent + Apply AI strategy** [Portugal ANIA p.2, p.20]; China explicitly positions for an **alternative UN-led global AI governance** framework. These two postures are not compatible at the policy level — Portugal cannot adopt Chinese governance positioning without breaking EU coherence.

2. **Industrial policy model**: China's state-directed "long-term/patient/strategic capital" + state-coordinated NDRC implementation is the **most activist industrial-policy model** in the comparator set — closer to Japan-MITI/Singapore-EDB-1960s than to EU norms. Portugal's ANIA correctly anchors to PRR + EU AI Continent funding mechanisms rather than activist state-led financing.

3. **Ideological framing**: Xi Jinping Thought + Marxist "new productive forces" terminology is the political vocabulary. Portugal's vision rests on **economic competitiveness within EU framework** + responsibility/ethics — not on a comparable ideological superstructure.

4. **Civil-liberties posture**: China's "social governance human-machine coexistence" + "intelligent governance" + multi-stakeholder safety covering "natural persons, digital entities, and intelligent robots" + government AI services framing differs substantially from EU AI Act's rights-based protections. Portugal's IV pillar (Responsibility and Ethics) is rights-anchored.

### Where Portugal can still learn from China's framing

Even with the fundamental divergence above, several **structural moves** are worth Portugal's selective import:

1. **The "AI+" horizontal-integration framing**. Portugal's 32 initiatives are organised by Pillar (Infrastructure/Innovation/Talent/Responsibility), which is a vertical framing. Adding an "**AI+ Portugal**" horizontal framing — AI+Health, AI+Education, AI+Tourism, AI+Blue Economy, AI+Industry, AI+Justice — would make ANIA's strategic bets more legible and align with Portugal's "Apply AI" EU strategy alignment.

2. **Elevate Global Cooperation / CPLP outreach to a peer pillar**. Portugal IV.7 (technology diplomacy) is one initiative buried in Pillar IV. China makes Global Cooperation a top-level pillar with two named sub-actions. Portugal has a strong CPLP+EU-LAC opportunity (260M Lusophone speakers + Atlantic-Iberian-Latin America corridor) that warrants peer-pillar status alongside Infrastructure/Innovation/Talent/Responsibility.

3. **"East-West" geographic compute distribution analogue**. China's "Eastern Data, Western Computing" leverages geographic asymmetry — east demand, west supply. Portugal has analogous opportunities: continental Portugal (data demand) + Azores/Madeira (compute supply: low ambient temperature, renewable energy availability for the Azores especially, submarine-cable hub positioning). Portugal I.4 (National Data Centre Plan) could explicitly adopt this Atlantic-corridor framing.

4. **2027 / 2030 / 2035 three-milestone target structure**. Portugal's ANIA targets a 2026-2030 horizon with no equivalent 2035 outlook. Adding a 2035 milestone (e.g., "fully AI-enabled Portuguese public administration by 2035") would mirror China's three-tier structure and align with EU AI Continent long-range planning.

5. **Open-source contributions counting for academic credit**. A specific Portuguese intervention: FCT could formally count open-source AI contributions toward tenure decisions and undergraduate credit. Low-cost, high-signal.

6. **Aggressive adoption targets at sector level**. Portugal's ANIA cites the current adoption gap (9.4% small companies, 18.2% medium, 49.1% large) [p.12] but doesn't set a future target. Setting "70% SME AI adoption by 2030" (analogous to China's 70% smart-terminal target by 2027) would sharpen accountability.

7. **The "warmer intelligent society" framing**. China's §II.IV.3 frames AI explicitly around community, family, eldercare, childcare, disability support. Portugal has demographic challenges (ageing population, low fertility, regional depopulation) that this framing speaks to. ANIA could absorb this human-centred social-welfare framing without abandoning its responsibility/ethics anchor.

### What Portugal should explicitly NOT copy

- **State-directed "patient capital" at China's scale** — Portugal's PRR + FEDER + Next Tech model is the right scale; replicating Chinese state-direction is neither feasible nor desirable
- **"Government guidance + industry self-discipline" regulatory model** — Portugal correctly anchors to EU AI Act's externally-enforced compliance regime via AESIA-equivalent (forthcoming national authority under ANIA IV.4)
- **Xi Jinping Thought ideological superstructure** — obviously not portable
- **The 90% adoption target** — politically incompatible with EU AI Continent's growth-not-coercion framing
- **Counter-EU global governance positioning** — Portugal's diplomatic anchor is EU + CPLP + EU-LAC; not UN-vs-OECD

## Critique-lens conclusion

China's "AI+" Action Opinion is **politically incompatible with Portugal's strategic posture** at the framing level — Xi Jinping Thought, "new productive forces" Marxist vocabulary, state-directed capital, UN-led counter-OECD governance positioning, and 90% adoption coerciveness are all features Portugal cannot and should not adopt within the EU framework.

**But the structural moves are selectively importable**, after stripping the ideological framing:

1. **Add an "AI+" horizontal-integration layer** above Portugal's vertical 4-Pillar / 32-initiative structure, naming the cross-cutting integrations: AI+Saúde, AI+Educação, AI+Turismo, AI+Mar/Economia Azul, AI+Indústria, AI+Justiça, AI+Administração Pública. This makes the strategic bets (currently listed at [p.7]) more legible.

2. **Elevate CPLP+EU-LAC outreach to a peer pillar** alongside the existing 4. China's "AI+ Global Cooperation" pillar template is directly importable, scoped to the Lusophone Atlantic corridor.

3. **Set 2035 long-range milestone** complementing the 2026-2030 Action Plan window. EU AI Continent Action Plan also looks toward 2035; aligning ANIA explicitly would strengthen EU coherence.

4. **Adopt Atlantic-archipelago geographic compute distribution analogue** — Continental Portugal as "demand", Açores as "supply" (low ambient temperature + Atlantic submarine-cable hub + renewable energy). Reframe I.2 Gigafactory and I.4 National Data Centre Plan around this.

5. **Open-source academic credit policy** — direct, low-cost institutional change FCT could implement under II.4 (non-academic PhDs in AI) and II.1 (fundamental research).

6. **Adoption targets at sector level by 2030** — "70% SME AI adoption", "90% PA processes AI-augmented", quantifying ANIA's current adoption gap data [p.12] as aspirational targets.

7. **Adopt the "warmer intelligent society" social-welfare framing** in Pillar II — connect AI to Portugal's demographic challenges (ageing, low fertility, regional depopulation, dependency-ratio rising).

8. **Three-tier capital framing** at smaller scale — Portuguese variant of "long-term capital, patient capital, strategic capital" via BfP, IFIC, SIFIDE, Next Tech: explicitly position Portuguese state-backed AI funds as patient capital (multi-decade horizons) distinct from VC cycles.

### The 12-country comparator set is now complete

With China added, Portugal has a full 12-country comparator set covering:
- **EU peers** (Spain, France, Italy, Finland, Sweden) — closest regulatory + economic peers
- **UK** (post-Brexit, AI maker positioning) — strategic + operational stack reference
- **USA** (2025 Action Plan) — frontier-AI dominance posture
- **China** (2025 "AI+" Opinion) — state-led activist alternative
- **UAE, Qatar** (Gulf state) — branding-heavy national ambitions
- **Argentina** (Latin America baseline) — 2018 digital cautionary

Each provides specific importable moves; together they triangulate the design space for Portugal's ANIA evolution.
