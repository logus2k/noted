# South Africa — AI Planning: Adoption of AI by Government (Oct 2023 draft)

**Source:** [South Africa National_AI_Government_Summit.pdf](../../domains/national_ai_agendas/sources/South%20Africa%20National_AI_Government_Summit.pdf), ~46 pages, prepared by **Alfred "Ali" Mashishi** (Chief Director, ICT Forecasting and Modeling) on behalf of the **Department of Communications and Digital Technologies (DCDT)**, October 2023. Foreword by **Mondli Gungubele MP** (Minister of Communications and Digital Technologies).

## Important caveat

The document carries an explicit disclaimer on page i: *"This draft document is for the general discussion purpose and not for publication or academic use. (…) All rights reserved as this draft is for policy formulation discussion purposes."* Despite the filename, this is **not a finalised National AI Strategy**: it is a policy-formulation discussion draft scoped to **adoption of AI by government**, not a whole-of-economy national AI agenda. Two consequences:

1. **Status gap.** Most other comparator jurisdictions (UK, France, Italy, Spain, Singapore, Japan, Germany, Korea, Portugal) have approved, published, multi-year strategies; South Africa, as of the document in this corpus, has a draft anchored to the **Presidential Commission on the 4th Industrial Revolution (PC4IR)** report (2020) and the **National Development Plan 2030**. A successor document may have been adopted since.
2. **Scope gap.** Where Portugal's ANIA has 32 initiatives across four pillars covering Development, Application, Talent, and Responsibility, the South African document treats government-side AI adoption as its primary unit of analysis, with the rest of the economy addressed indirectly through the institutional vehicle (AIISA) and its university hubs.

## Vision and strategic framing

The document positions AI as a tool to **"advance social and economic prosperity"** [Foreword], anchored to:

- **NDP 2030** (the long-term national development frame)
- **PC4IR report** (the Presidential Commission on the 4th Industrial Revolution, which approved an AI Framework as part of a Strategic Implementation Programme)
- **DCDT mandate** (the line ministry driving the digital agenda)

The headline framing is that South Africa sits in the **"middle strata"** between digital, gig, and circular economy models and has chosen the **digital economy** as the agenda [p.21]. The AI agenda is the technological lever to deliver that choice.

> "South Africa vision on AI is defined by the intention to establish a national AI Institute that will drive the economic and social policies designed to advance national interest and global competitiveness." [p.29]

## Structure

The document has three sections plus an implementation roadmap:

| Section | Coverage |
|---|---|
| **A. Modelling AI within the SA Landscape** | Global AI discourse (politics, economics, militarisation), investment trends, global concerns, components of AI (ML/NN/DL), regional vs national approaches, SA conceptual framework |
| **B. SA Landscape, Opportunities and Pitfalls** | PC4IR adoption, Applied vs Generative AI impact, state considerations (law, governance, regulation), SA's global and continental aspirations |
| **C. South African AI Plan** | Priority focus areas and enablers, enabling environment, AIISA effectiveness criteria, Data/AI/IoT mandate, AI governance and institutional mechanisms, AI plan adoption and timelines |

## The four core priorities [Section C, p.23-24]

The four-priority frame is presented as a national plan of action "co-owned by government, business, labour and educational leaders":

1. **Smart approach to embrace AI for all citizens** — define the AI agenda, prioritise sectors with competitive edge (e.g., **mining**, **manufacturing**), scope the jobs and economy risks
2. **Invest in AI skills generation** — rethink education and learning ecosystem, strengthen STEM, ramp up TVET (Technical and Vocational Education and Training), drive graduate conversion rate
3. **Mitigate AI jobs impact** — identify non-portable jobs, reintegrate displaced workers, support AI start-ups in advanced manufacturing and agro-processing, rebalance social-support mechanisms
4. **Foster step-up scenario** — close infrastructure and housing gap with AI-enabled investment, embrace clean-energy transition through AI

## The six dimensions framework [p.25]

The plan organises execution around six dimensions, each with components and activities:

| Dimension | Key components | Sample activities |
|---|---|---|
| **Skills** | Data and Literacy; Data and AI Specialists; Data and AI Experts | Instil basic AI skills for workforce + students; build sustainable specialist pool; nurture scientists via local/international partnerships |
| **Policy and Regulations** | Policy/regulatory frameworks; Company and Talent incentive programmes | Attract local and foreign AI companies; attract foreign AI talent |
| **AI Investment** | AI Investment Funds; Investor support programme; Administrative services | Targeted investment funds; specialised investor support |
| **Research and Innovation** | R&D innovation excellence; National innovation test beds | Elevate AI R&D capabilities in universities and research centres; AI test beds in mega-projects and smart cities |
| **AI Ecosystem** | Data and AI sector strategies; Data and AI platforms; Smart cities; Government systems | Develop sector-specific AI; accessible digital platforms; smart city agenda; data management in government |
| **Governance** (implicit, elaborated in C2) | Cabinet, Minister, Advisory Council, AIISA Programme, AI Hubs, AI Management Office, Data and Cloud Office, IoT and e-Gov Office | Coordination, regulation, advisory, hub network management |

## Quantitative anchors

The headline NDP-aligned targets [p.28]:

| Target | Value |
|---|---|
| Workforce trained on basic AI/data literacy | **30%** |
| Local AI specialists in workforce | **20,000** |
| AI experts | **5,000** |
| Country rank in Applied AI index | **Top 20** |
| AI startups | **100–300** |
| Government + local + FDI investment in AI | **R70 billion** |
| Country rank in peer-reviewed AI publications | **Top 20** |

Companion infrastructure estimates [p.32]:
- **R70 billion minimum** dedicated to 5G innovation
- **R100 billion+** for AI systems and national cybersecurity needs

These targets are aggregate national outcomes; no per-initiative budgets or timelines are given. There is no headline GDP-uplift figure equivalent to the UK's £400B or Portugal's €18–22B/yr.

## Institutional architecture

**AI Institute of South Africa (AIISA)** is the central institutional vehicle, established by DCDT in November 2022 in partnership with **University of Johannesburg (UJ)** and **Tshwane University of Technology (TUT)** [p.30]. Its design principle is **networked**: AIISA is the coordinator, facilitator, and policy-setting body, while **AI Hubs / Centres of Excellence** at universities do the technical and operational work [p.35]. The plan targets **11 AI Hubs/Centres of Excellence by 2025** [p.44].

A parallel body, the **Centre for Artificial Intelligence Research (CAIR)**, was established earlier by the **Department of Science and Innovation (DSI)**, predating AIISA [Foreword].

Governance structure [p.41]:

- **Cabinet / IMC on Digital Transformation** — approve and guide the national AI plan
- **Minister + Ministerial AI Expert Advisory Council** — set overall strategic direction
- **AIISA Programme** — facilitate execution of the plan and strategic initiatives
- **AI Hubs** — generate localised solutions, applications, skills, and software
- **AI Management Office** — motivate innovation and build capabilities
- **Data and Cloud Office** — regulate national data and drive compliance
- **IoT and e-Government Office** — manage national data and draw insights

## Sectoral priority areas [p.27]

Five priority sectors named for integrated Data and AI deployment:

- **Education** — align education with labour-market needs, improve student journey
- **Government** — smarter and more efficient public sector
- **Healthcare** — increase access, enhance preventative care, accommodate growing demand
- **Energy** — increase capacity, enhance efficiency, develop adjacent industries
- **Mobility** — regional hub, smart-city mobility tech, traffic safety

## Regulation and ethics posture [C2.1-C2.2]

South Africa has **no AI-specific Act**. The document references:

- The **EU AI Act** as "the first comprehensive piece of legislation in the global AI space" and "the de facto global AI regulation" [p.40]
- **OECD** (Trustworthy AI), **UNESCO** (AI Ethics), **ITU** (AI for Good), **UN** (AI for Development) as global ethical frames
- **Access Partnership AI Policy Lab (2023)** as the cited analytical source

The proposed SA regulatory direction is principles-based, anchored to the **Constitution Chapter 2 (Bill of Rights)** and aiming for **alignment with global practices, ethics, and human-rights protection**. Specific regulatory concerns flagged: disinformation/fake news, bias and discrimination, copyright abuse (WIPO alignment), privacy.

## Continental and international positioning

South Africa positions itself as both a **continental anchor** and a **global participant**:

- **African Union** + **SADC** + **BRICS+** + **G20** member-state engagement on AI governance
- **AfCFTA** (African Continental Free Trade Area) as the framework for a continental data collaborative space
- Benchmarking and fact-finding partnerships explicitly named with: **Saudi Data and AI Authority (SDAAI)**, **AI Institute of the Netherlands**, **NITI Aayog (India)**, **UNESCO AI Ethics Centre**, **AI Sweden**, **AI Technology Centre (Egypt)** [p.36]

## What South Africa does that Portugal's ANIA does NOT

1. **Foundational anchoring to NDP 2030 + PC4IR** — a single, named, long-term national development plan that the AI agenda explicitly serves. Portugal's ANIA references EU plans (AI Continent, Apply AI) but not a single Portuguese long-horizon development plan with the same anchoring weight.

2. **Continental positioning (AfCFTA + AU + SADC + BRICS+)** as a structural feature of the agenda. Portugal sits inside EU positioning, which gives reach but less of an explicit continental-leadership role.

3. **Networked AI Institute + university hubs model (AIISA + UJ + TUT, target 11 hubs by 2025)** as the principal institutional vehicle. Portugal's sectoral AI centres (II.5) are sector-anchored rather than hub-network-anchored, and the Centro para a IA Responsável (IV.3) is a single body rather than a federation.

4. **Aggregate workforce targets at scale** (30% workforce AI-literate, 20K specialists, 5K experts). Portugal's Talent pillar (III) sets targets per initiative but no headline whole-workforce figure of this scale.

5. **"Top 20 country" benchmarks** (Applied AI index, publications index) as binary aspirational targets. Portugal does not benchmark itself with a single ranking target.

6. **AI Hyper-scale Data and LLM Centre by 2028** as a named infrastructure milestone. Portugal's AMALIA (II.7) commits to a Portuguese-language LLM but does not name a specific hyper-scale data/LLM facility.

7. **R70B + R100B infrastructure investment estimate** as a single national funding floor for 5G + AI + cybersecurity. Portugal lists initiative-level funding through PRR + EuroHPC without a single headline national-investment figure.

8. **Integrated Data + IoT + AI institutional architecture** (Data and Cloud Office + IoT and e-Government Office sitting alongside AI Management Office). Portugal treats data spaces (I.3), IoT, and AI as separate threads.

9. **Six-dimensions framework (Skills, Policy/Regulation, Investment, R&I, Ecosystem, Governance)** as a cross-cutting design grid. Portugal organises around four pillars and 32 initiatives without an explicit cross-cutting dimension grid.

10. **Continental data collaborative space as a forward initiative** under AfCFTA scope. Portugal's external positioning is EU-anchored without an equivalent Lusophone-Atlantic data-space commitment, despite the natural CPLP/PALOP scope being available.

## What Portugal's ANIA does that South Africa's draft does NOT

1. **Final, approved, published status** — Portugal's ANIA is Jan 2026 official policy; South Africa's document is an October 2023 discussion draft with an explicit disclaimer against academic citation.

2. **Per-initiative cards** with named responsible entities, start semesters (2S 2025 → 1S 2027), and indicative budgets. South Africa's roadmap has area-level activities with target years but no per-activity responsibility, scope, or budget breakdown.

3. **EU AI Act anchoring as binding framework** (IV.4 + IV.6). South Africa references the EU AI Act as a global benchmark to be assessed, not as a framework Portugal has committed to operationalise.

4. **Named strategic bets** (Cybersecurity, LLMs, AI+Robotics for Industry, Blue Economy, Responsible AI). South Africa's sectoral priorities (Education, Government, Healthcare, Energy, Mobility) are broader and less differentiated.

5. **Named sovereign LLM commitment (AMALIA)** with technical scope. South Africa plans an LLM Centre by 2028 but does not commit to a national-language model with the same explicit scope.

6. **AI literacy citizen campaign (III.8)** and **AI Generation career campaign (III.7)**. South Africa addresses AI literacy through workforce-target language without an equivalent named public-facing campaign.

7. **Regulatory sandbox as a named forward initiative** (IV.5). South Africa's regulatory roadmap is principles-and-timeline-based without a named sandbox instrument.

8. **Quantified macroeconomic case** (€18–22B/yr ongoing GDP add, +2.7pp productivity). South Africa cites investment requirements (R70B + R100B) but not a headline macroeconomic uplift.

## Critique-lens conclusion

South Africa's October 2023 draft is best read as a **government-adoption blueprint anchored to PC4IR and NDP 2030** rather than as a whole-economy national AI strategy. Its strongest features are (a) the **networked AIISA + university hubs institutional model**, (b) the **integrated Data + IoT + AI architecture**, and (c) the **continental positioning under AfCFTA**. Its weakest features for cross-comparison are the **draft status**, the **absence of per-initiative ownership and budgets**, and the **scope limited to government adoption** rather than the whole AI economy.

### Top moves Portugal could import from South Africa

1. **Anchor the AI agenda to a single named long-horizon national plan**. Portugal's ANIA links to EU plans well; an explicit anchor to a Portuguese 2030/2035 national-development frame (e.g., a Portuguese equivalent of NDP 2030 or the National Recovery and Resilience Plan extended horizon) would tighten the narrative.

2. **Networked AI hubs at universities as the institutional backbone**. Portugal's sectoral AI centres (II.5) could be reorganised or augmented as a **named hub network** with a target count and named host universities (FCUL, FEUP, UMinho, IST, U.Coimbra, U.Aveiro, U.Algarve as candidates). The AIISA networked model is more explicit than Portugal's sector-anchored design.

3. **Continental data collaborative space ambition**. Portugal can credibly play the same continental role within **CPLP / PALOP / Atlantic Lusophone space** that South Africa plays within AU/SADC/AfCFTA. The ANIA omits this; a named CPLP AI Data Space initiative would slot naturally between II.7 (AMALIA) and IV pillar.

4. **Headline workforce targets at scale**. Portugal has training targets per initiative; a single headline target (e.g., "X% of Portuguese workforce AI-literate by 2030; Y AI specialists; Z AI experts") would give the talent pillar a comparable centre of gravity.

5. **AI Hyper-scale Data and LLM Centre as a named facility milestone**. AMALIA is the model-level commitment; an explicit named **National AI Compute and LLM Facility** (paired with EuroHPC) would parallel SA's 2028 facility commitment.

6. **Integrated Data + IoT + AI institutional design**. Portugal treats these threads separately; the SA pattern of a Data and Cloud Office plus an IoT and e-Government Office alongside the AI Management Office is a coordination model worth adapting.

### What Portugal should NOT copy

- **The draft status** — Portugal's ANIA is the cleanly finalised baseline that South Africa's document is reaching toward. Reverting to discussion-draft form would be a regression.
- **The "top 20 country" framing** — single-rank aspirations are a softer KPI than per-initiative deliverables; Portugal's specificity is the better template.
- **The discursive style** — large sections of the SA document are global-context exposition (Section A) and discursive framing (Section B). Portugal's compact 22-page format is the better template for a Mayor / Minister-facing strategy.
- **The exclusive focus on government adoption** — Portugal correctly addresses the whole economy across four pillars; narrowing to government-side adoption would lose the Development and Talent pillars.

### Strategic complementarity with other comparators

South Africa's networked hub model and continental positioning combine well with **UK's strategic + operational stack**, **Spain's per-initiative budget transparency**, **France's per-recommendation Ministry assignment**, and **Singapore's centralised execution office**. A Portuguese ANIA evolution drawing the best from all five would:

- Adopt **UK operational depth** (Playbook + Scan→Pilot→Scale + transparency standard)
- Adopt **Spain per-initiative budgets** (Tabla 6 format)
- Adopt **France per-recommendation Ministry assignment**
- Adopt **Singapore execution discipline**
- Adopt **South African networked university hub backbone** and **continental data-space ambition**
