# Finland — Artificial Intelligence 4.0 Programme: Finland as a Leader in Twin Transition (Dec 2022)

**Source:** [finland_TEM_2022_63.pdf](../../domains/national_ai_agendas/sources/finland_TEM_2022_63.pdf), 34 pages. Publications of the Ministry of Economic Affairs and Employment (TEM) 2022:63. Final report of the AI 4.0 Programme prepared by a steering group chaired by Jussi Herlin (Kone Corporation), appointed Nov 2020 by Minister Mika Lintilä [p.4]. ISBN PDF 978-952-327-918-6.

## Document type

This is the **final report of a multi-year programme**, not a stand-alone national strategy in the Portuguese ANIA sense. The programme was launched in 2020 and concluded in 2023. The report consolidates the steering group's two interim reports and proposes **11 concrete measures** plus a **strategic-level roundtable** to sustain delivery after the programme's term [p.10, p.29].

## Vision and framing — "Twin Transition" [p.7-11]

- Vision 2030: **"Making Finland a winner in twin transition"** — the digital and green transitions treated as a **single integrated goal**.
- Distinct Finnish concept: **"Carbon handprint"** — the positive climate impact of solutions sold to others, contrasted with "carbon footprint" (own emissions) [p.17, Measure 3]. Public funding is channelled to technologies that **increase the carbon handprint of digitalisation**.
- AI is one of six "key technologies" treated together: wireless networks, AI, microelectronics & photonics, quantum, space, smart manufacturing [p.12].
- Posture is **EU-aligned and pro-European**, emphasising European technological sovereignty, ELLIS/ELLIS Network, ADRA PPP, AI TEFs, EDIH, Horizon Europe, Digital Europe Programme, IPCEI on Microelectronics, Chips JU [p.13-16].

## Four 2030 objectives [p.11]

1. **Nature-smart companies** bring Finland a "triple victory" in sustainable development via digital technologies
2. **Strengthen AI leadership** through investment in key tech development and adoption
3. **Industrial SMEs reach top global rankings** in digital maturity
4. **Finland increasingly central in EU AI/data/industrial strategy**

## Structure — 3 development priorities × 11 measures

| Priority | Measures |
|---|---|
| **A. Strengthening high-quality research and RDI** | M1: RDI clusters via expertise and long-term inputs (Talent Boost, ELLIS Helsinki to institute level) [p.13]; M2: National RDI agenda crossing key tech sectors (incl. quantum strategy, cybersecurity centre with Defence Forces) [p.15]; M3: Public funding for technologies increasing carbon handprint [p.17]; M4: HPC/LUMI for companies (20% LUMI reserved for industry) [p.18] |
| **B. Digital skills and SME adoption** | M5: Digital agents + peer groups + SME development forum [p.20]; M6: Industry 5.0 programme for SMEs (5-year horizon) [p.22]; M7: Data Economy growth programme (built on Sitra IHAN) [p.22]; M8: Micro-credentials via SECLE (Service Centre for Continuous Learning and Employment) [p.23] |
| **C. International frontrunner status** | M9: Companies into EU-level decision-making + propose appointment of an "**AI Envoy**" under next EU Commissioner [p.25]; M10: Transatlantic technology and trade-policy positioning [p.26]; M11: Carbon-handprint exports [p.27] |

## Quantitative KPIs — by priority [p.13, 19, 25]

Finland's plan is **the only one in this set with explicit numeric KPIs**:

- Public + private RDI inputs in key technologies: **+30% per year (average)**
- Patent applications in key tech: increase (Finnish Patent Office)
- Global Talent Competitiveness Index: **from 7th → top 3** (2022 baseline)
- Global AI Index Talent: **from 13th → top 5**
- DESI Index "Use of AI in enterprises": **stay or improve from rank 3** (2021)
- WEF Global Competitiveness Index: **from 8th → top 3** (2022 baseline)
- LUMI HPC usage statistics (sessions, intensity): monitored as growth indicator

## Governance — the "Strategic-Level Roundtable" proposal [p.29-30]

Critical structural innovation: the report does **not** create a permanent agency. Instead it proposes a **strategic-level roundtable** with:

- Representation from key industrial ecosystems, employer organisations, **trade unions**, entrepreneurs, research, education, funding bodies, and ministries
- A supporting **secretariat**
- Mandate to anticipate industrial change, monitor implementation, and activate measures
- Sits alongside existing structures (coordination group for digitalisation, digital compass, Sustainable Industry X, Sitra data-economy roadmap)

The roundtable is the explicit answer to the post-programme continuity problem.

## What Finland does that Portugal's ANIA does NOT do

1. **Explicit linkage of AI policy to green transition** ("twin transition") as the overarching frame [p.7-11]. Portugal's ANIA mentions renewables as a Portuguese competitive advantage [Portugal ANIA p.4] but does not **link AI policy to climate goals as a unified strategic objective**.

2. **"Carbon handprint" funding channel** [Measure 3, p.17]. Public funding deliberately steered toward AI/digital solutions that **reduce others' emissions**, not just the country's own footprint. Portugal has no equivalent instrument.

3. **Explicit quantitative international-ranking targets** [p.13, 19, 25] — Finland names the index, the current rank, and the target rank. Portugal's ANIA's only quantitative claims are GDP/productivity impact [Portugal ANIA p.3] and challenge metrics; **no aspirational ranking targets**.

4. **"AI Envoy" at EU level** [p.25, Measure 9]: explicitly propose appointment of a high-level AI Envoy under the next Commissioner for the industrial/digital internal market, and lobby for a Finn in the role. Portugal IV.7 (technology diplomacy) is **structurally weaker** — no equivalent named EU role to capture.

5. **Trade-union representation in the governance mechanism** [p.29]. Portugal's 32 initiative cards list IPAMEI, FCT, ARTE, etc., but **no labour representation** in the implementation structure, despite the III.5+III.7+III.8 talent track touching workforce restructuring (1.3M reskilled, 320k displaced [Portugal ANIA p.16]).

6. **Digital agents and digital peer groups for SMEs** [Measure 5, p.20]. **Human-mediated SME advisory**, not just a platform. Portugal II.10 (PME.IA platform) provides low-code/no-code products but lacks the human-advisor channel.

7. **Industry 5.0 framing** [Measure 6, p.22]. Industry 5.0 is the EU's post-Industry 4.0 concept — beyond efficiency, towards human-centric and sustainable manufacturing. Portugal's "AI and Robotics applied to Industry" strategic bet [Portugal ANIA p.7] does not adopt this frame.

8. **Sectoral low-carbon roadmaps** [p.8, footnote 6]: pre-existing private-sector-led roadmaps that the AI programme plugs into. Portugal lacks pre-existing **sectoral roadmaps** for AI deployment (II.5 Sectoral AI Centres will need to be the carrier).

9. **Quantum strategy as a deliberate cross-sectoral document** [p.16]. Portugal mentions quantum in passing as a strategic technology but ANIA has no dedicated quantum track.

10. **Cybersecurity centre of expertise** built jointly with **Finnish Defence Forces + CSC + research institutes + Finnish Information Security Cluster FISC** [p.16]. Portugal names cybersecurity as a strategic bet [Portugal ANIA p.7] but assigns **no specific institutional anchor**.

11. **20% of LUMI HPC capacity reserved for companies** [p.18, Measure 4]. Portugal's MareNostrum5 share is "5% research and 8-9% innovation for Startups, SMEs and PA" [Portugal ANIA p.10] — a comparable framing, **but smaller share**.

## What Portugal does that Finland does NOT do

1. **Explicit economic case in € terms.** Portugal: €18-22B/yr GDP add, +2.7pp productivity, 8% GDP at peak impact [Portugal ANIA p.3]. Finland's plan has KPIs but **no aggregate GDP figure**.
2. **Per-initiative start dates and responsible entities.** Portugal lists 32 initiative cards with semester (2S 2025 → 1S 2027) and entity columns [Portugal ANIA p.11-21]. Finland's 11 measures are **timeless** within the 2030 horizon.
3. **Stronger public-administration AI track.** Portugal Pillar II has 3 PA-specific initiatives (II.11 Center of Excellence, II.12 National AI Calls for PA, II.13 Procurement Code Guide) [Portugal ANIA p.15]. Finland's plan is **industrially focused** and barely covers government services.
4. **Explicit regulatory-sandbox initiative.** Portugal IV.5 [Portugal ANIA p.21]. Finland defers to EU AI Act implementation.
5. **AI talent fast-track visa.** Portugal III.5 (AIMA-administered) [Portugal ANIA p.18]. Finland references Talent Boost as a cross-ministry programme but **no AI-specific visa instrument**.
6. **AI literacy campaign for citizens.** Portugal III.8 [Portugal ANIA p.18]. Finland's M8 is workforce micro-credentials, not citizen literacy.

## Critique-lens conclusion

Finland's AI 4.0 is the **closest peer document to Portugal's ANIA** in this set: both are EU-aligned, both target SME adoption, both span research → adoption → international. Portugal can usefully import several specific Finnish moves:

1. **Add an explicit "twin transition" linkage** at the ANIA vision level — couple AI productivity gains with Portugal's existing green-transition commitments. Currently the energy/renewables advantage [Portugal ANIA p.4] is asserted but not operationalised in the 32 initiatives.

2. **Steal the "carbon handprint" funding channel** — a Portuguese version of Measure 3 [Finland p.17] would route a slice of ANIA funding specifically to AI solutions that **reduce others' emissions**. This is a clean expansion of the existing "Apply AI" strategic bet [Portugal ANIA p.7] and aligns with Portugal's Blue Economy framing.

3. **Add explicit ranking-based aspirational KPIs.** Portugal cites where it is (75% of EU productivity, 11% advanced AI skills, OECD-lowest maturity [Portugal ANIA p.16]) but **does not name where it wants to be** in any global index. Pick 2-3 indices (Global Talent Competitiveness, Stanford AI Index, DESI AI-in-enterprise) and name target ranks.

4. **Borrow Finland's "AI Envoy" idea**, scoped to Portugal. III.4 (goPortugal expansion) and IV.7 (technology diplomacy) [Portugal ANIA p.18, p.21] are vague on which EU role(s) Portugal targets. Naming a target role (e.g., Portuguese national in DG-CNECT AI unit; a Portuguese candidate for the next AI-Continent Action Plan steering body) sharpens this.

5. **Add a tripartite governance layer** — modeled on Finland's strategic roundtable — including trade unions and employer organisations, not just public agencies. Portugal's 1.3M reskilling / 320k displacement challenge [Portugal ANIA p.16] is a labour-market shock that will need union buy-in to land.

6. **Add a "Digital Agents" (human advisor) channel for SMEs** sitting between II.9 (AI in SMEs) and II.10 (PME.IA platform). Currently ANIA's SME path is product-mediated; Finland's evidence is that SMEs need **humans to navigate the offering jungle** [Finland p.20].

7. **Build a Portuguese sectoral-roadmap exercise** like Finland's 2019 sectoral low-carbon roadmaps. Pillar II.5 (Sectoral AI Centres) lists "Health, Education and Industrial" as initial focus [Portugal ANIA p.14] but with no per-sector roadmap document.

What Finland's plan does **not** help with, where Portugal needs to look elsewhere:

- Defence / national security AI track (Finland has only the cybersecurity centre)
- Deepfake / synthetic media coverage (absent in both)
- A consolidated multi-year budget envelope (absent in both)
- A K-12 schools AI curriculum (absent in both)
- Frontier-model safety / evaluation (absent in both; Finland defers to EU AI Office)
