# Kenya — Artificial Intelligence Strategy 2025–2030 (March 2025)

**Source:** [Kenya AI Strategy 2025 - 2030.pdf](../../domains/national_ai_agendas/sources/Kenya%20AI%20Strategy%202025%20-%202030.pdf), 88 pages, published March 2025 by the **Ministry of Information, Communications and the Digital Economy (MICDE)**. Cabinet Secretary: **H.E. Hon. William Kabogo Gitau**; Principal Secretary, State Department for ICT and the Digital Economy: **Eng. John Tanui (CBS)**. Project implemented by the **Global Partnership for Sustainable Development Data**, funded by the **European Union** and **Germany's Federal Ministry of Economic Cooperation and Development (BMZ)** through **GIZ** (the FAIR Forward programme is named in the steering committee composition).

## Strategic framing

> "A regional leader in AI R&D, innovation and commercialization for inclusive socio-economic development." [Vision, p.62]

The strategy positions Kenya as **Africa's leading AI hub for model innovation, driving sustainable development, economic growth, and social inclusion**, with explicit ambition to be a **net exporter of high-quality AI technology and services** [p.62]. Anchored to:

- **Kenya's Bottom-up Economic Transformation Agenda**
- **Kenya Vision 2030**
- **Kenya National Digital Master Plan**

## Methodology — distinctive multi-method primary data collection [Section 1.4]

The strategy was developed through:

1. **Literature review + environmental analysis** (PESTLE-style, with global / regional / national context per dimension)
2. **Primary data collection**: Key Informant Interviews (KIIs), Focus Group Discussions (FGDs), **Town Hall meetings** convened by AI Kenya, EldoHub, Lake Hub, Qhala, KEPSA, Data Privacy and Governance Society, **United Disabled Persons of Kenya**, and Dedan Kimathi University of Technology; **online survey**
3. **Stakeholder analysis** (Influence-Interest Matrix, Table 3.3)
4. **SLOC analysis** (Strengths, Limitations, Opportunities, Challenges) [Tables 3.4–3.7]
5. **Validation** with multi-stakeholder feedback

This is the **only strategy in the comparator set with explicitly named Town Hall convenors across the country (including a national disability advocacy organisation)** as part of the drafting methodology.

## Eight guiding principles [p.63]

1. Inclusivity and non-discrimination
2. Participation and co-creation
3. Transparency and accountability
4. Ethical and responsible AI
5. **Cultural preservation and contextualization** (Kenyan cultural values, contextual relevance)
6. Environmental sustainability
7. Economic benefit and self-sufficiency (net exporter ambition)
8. **Local first approach** (local talent, resources, solutions; international partnerships as augmentation)

## Structure — 3 strategic pillars + 4 enablers (7 themes total) [Table 5.1]

| Theme | Sub-themes |
|---|---|
| **1. AI Digital Infrastructure** (pillar) | AI-ready national digital infrastructure; compute infrastructure (data centres); green energy sources to power AI; **domestic manufacturing for AI-specific hardware**; cybersecurity infrastructure; partnerships and collaborations |
| **2. Data** (pillar) | Data governance framework; secure data sharing; quality AI training datasets |
| **3. AI R&D and Innovation** (pillar) | AI R&D capabilities; AI innovation; scaling local AI enterprises; market for local AI solutions |
| **4. Talent Development** (enabler) | Foundational AI skills in schools; specialised AI skills in tertiary institutions and industry; partnerships for AI talent development and placement; acquisition of quality foreign AI talent |
| **5. Governance** (enabler) | Policy framework for AI and emerging technologies; agile AI legal and regulatory frameworks; **AI risk and safety frameworks**; stakeholder collaborations |
| **6. Investments** (enabler) | Private-sector investments in AI; public-sector investments in AI; Kenya as an investment destination for AI |
| **7. Ethics, Equity, and Inclusion** (enabler) | Ethical, responsible, and inclusive AI development and deployment; inclusivity and national values in AI development and deployment; **public AI literacy** |

Each theme has an overall **Goal**, with **objectives**, **flagship projects**, **intermediate outcomes**, and **KPIs** broken out in Tables 5.2 to 5.8.

| # | Goal |

## Selected flagship projects [Tables 5.2–5.8]

### Infrastructure (Theme 1)
- **1.1.1 National Broadband Expansion Program** (high-speed internet, 5G networks)
- **1.1.2 Edge computing capabilities** for AI research, development, and deployment
- **1.2.1 Build 3 TIA 942 (ANSI standard) AI-capable data centres within 5 years**
- **1.5 Cybersecurity infrastructure**
- **1.6 Partnerships and international collaborations**

### Data (Theme 2)
- Data governance framework establishment
- Secure cross-border data transfer mechanisms

### R&D and Innovation (Theme 3)
- AI research hubs, academic-industry collaborations, open research practices
- Local AI enterprise scaling support (funding, mentorship, incubation)
- Market-stimulation instruments for local AI solutions

### Talent (Theme 4)
- Integration of foundational AI skills into national curricula
- Specialised AI skills in tertiary institutions
- **Public Service Internship Program (PSIP)** for AI specialisation
- Simplified immigration for quality foreign AI talent

### Governance (Theme 5)
- National AI policy framework
- Agile AI legal and regulatory structures
- AI risk and safety frameworks
- Cross-border data governance pursuit

### Investments (Theme 6)
- Tax breaks, grants, innovation funds for private-sector AI investment
- Government funding for foundational research and infrastructure
- Kenya as an AI investment destination (ease-of-doing-business enhancements)

### Ethics, Equity, and Inclusion (Theme 7)
- **7.2.1 Data labelling and classification policies that address bias**
- **7.2.2 Ethical framework and AI rights, ethical principles and considerations**
- **7.2.4 Public repository of ethical AI use-cases and best practices**
- **7.2.5 Inclusivity framework** including gender mainstreaming and **persons with disabilities (PWDs)** representation
- **7.3.1 Public awareness campaign** on AI rights, disinformation, misinformation, protection
- **7.3.2 Educate government employees on ethical, equitable and inclusive AI**

## Priority use cases [Section 5.3, p.83]

The Kenyan government and ecosystem have identified priority use cases across sectors aligned with the Bottom-up Economic Transformation Agenda and Vision 2030:

- **Healthcare**: maternal health chatbot in local dialects; expanded disease advisory system
- **Education**: intelligent tutoring systems; multilingual teacher training modules
- **Agriculture**: farmer-friendly audio formats in local languages; AI-powered fertilizer recommendation systems
- **Public service delivery**: multilingual chatbots and virtual assistants
- **Security**
- **MSMEs** (micro, small and medium-sized enterprises)
- **Creative sector**: AI-powered national digital creative platform
- **Sustainability**

The **multilingual / local-language emphasis** in healthcare, education, agriculture, and public-service delivery use cases is distinctive: Kenya names this as part of the deliverable, not as a separate principle.

## Regulation posture

Kenya has **no AI-specific Act yet**. The strategy commits to:

- **National AI policy framework** under Goal 5
- **Agile AI legal and regulatory structures** that evolve with the technology
- **AI risk and safety frameworks**
- **Cross-border data governance** pursuit

The legal environment analysis in Section 3.4 covers global (EU AI Act, OECD, UNESCO), regional (African Union Continental AI Strategy, Smart Africa, EAC), and national contexts. The Kenyan regulatory backbone is built on the **Data Protection Act 2019** and existing ICT-sector regulation under the **Communications Authority** and the **Office of the Data Protection Commissioner**.

## What Kenya does that Portugal's ANIA does NOT

1. **Multi-method primary data collection methodology with named Town Halls** across the country (Eldoret via EldoHub, lakeside region via Lake Hub, etc.). Portugal's ANIA was developed through consultation but does not publish an equivalent regional townhall map.

2. **United Disabled Persons of Kenya as a named townhall convenor** and PWD representation as an explicit KPI under Theme 7. Portugal does not name disability advocacy organisations or PWD-representation KPIs in the ANIA.

3. **"Local first approach" as an explicit guiding principle**. Portugal's ANIA prioritises Portuguese sovereignty implicitly (AMALIA, sectoral centres) but does not name "local first" as a principle.

4. **Cultural preservation and contextualisation as an explicit guiding principle**. Portugal references the Portuguese language through AMALIA but does not name cultural preservation as a principle alongside ethics/inclusion.

5. **Local-language AI use cases as named flagship projects** (maternal health chatbot in local dialects, farmer-friendly audio in local languages, multilingual teacher training). Portugal has AMALIA at the model level but does not name multilingual citizen-facing applications at the flagship-project level.

6. **SLOC analysis published in the strategy itself** (Tables 3.4–3.7). Portugal's ANIA refers to enablers and constraints but does not publish a comparable structured SLOC matrix.

7. **Standardised data-centre target with named standard** — 3 TIA 942 (ANSI standard) AI-capable data centres within 5 years. Portugal's I.4 (National Data Centre Plan) is comparable in intent but does not name the TIA 942 standard or the count target.

8. **Public Service Internship Program (PSIP) for AI specialisation in the civil service**. Portugal has III.1 (Doutor AP) and III.2 (PA training) but no named internship instrument that targets AI specialisation specifically.

9. **Public AI literacy KPI** in % increase of public awareness. Portugal's III.8 (AI literacy citizen campaign) is comparable in spirit but does not publish a single headline KPI.

10. **Donor-funded strategy development model** (EU + BMZ via GIZ FAIR Forward). Portugal's ANIA is government-internal; the Kenyan model demonstrates that bilateral cooperation funds can credibly underwrite a national strategy without losing national ownership.

11. **UNESCO + Oxford Insights AI Readiness diagnostic baselines** explicitly published in the strategy (Tables 3.1 and 3.2). Portugal cites EU and OECD benchmarks but does not publish these specific readiness indices as baseline tables.

12. **Strategy House visualisation** (Figure 5.1) as a single-page summary of the strategic architecture. Portugal does not include a comparable single-image summary.

## What Portugal's ANIA does that Kenya's strategy does NOT

1. **Per-initiative cards** with named responsible entities, start semesters, and indicative budgets. Kenya has goals + objectives + flagship projects + KPIs but no per-flagship owner or budget breakdown in the published strategy.

2. **Sovereign-language LLM at model level (AMALIA)**. Kenya names multilingual use cases but does not commit to a Kenyan-language LLM at the model level for Kiswahili and other national languages.

3. **EU AI Act + GDPR as binding regulatory anchors**. Kenya references the EU AI Act as one of several international benchmarks; it is not binding.

4. **Quantified macroeconomic case** (€18–22B/yr GDP add, +2.7pp productivity). Kenya cites global IMF/IFC/World Bank figures but no headline Kenyan-specific GDP uplift attributed to AI.

5. **Named strategic technology bets** (Cybersecurity, LLMs, AI+Robotics for Industry, Blue Economy, Responsible AI). Kenya names priority sectors (health, education, agriculture, public service, security, MSMEs, creative, sustainability) but no equivalent enumeration of strategic technology bets.

6. **Regulatory sandbox as a forward initiative (IV.5)**. Kenya names agile governance but no specific sandbox instrument.

7. **Public Procurement Code Guide for AI (II.13)**. Kenya names AI investment and incentive instruments but no equivalent procurement-code instrument.

## Critique-lens conclusion

Kenya's strategy is the **most participatory and inclusion-forward** in the comparator set. Its strongest distinctive features are (a) the **multi-method primary data collection methodology** with named town hall convenors, (b) the explicit **"local first" + "cultural preservation"** principle pair, (c) the **local-language AI use cases at flagship level**, and (d) the **PWD inclusion KPIs** with United Disabled Persons of Kenya as a named townhall convenor. The **donor-funded development model** (EU + BMZ via GIZ FAIR Forward) is a credible alternative to fully-internal strategy development for any country needing additional technical or fiscal capacity.

Its weakest features for cross-comparison are (a) the **absence of per-flagship ownership and budgets**, (b) the **discursive 88-page length** which makes operational navigation harder than Portugal's 22-page initiative cards, and (c) the **lack of a named Kenyan-language LLM commitment** despite Swahili being a credible model-level target.

### Top moves Portugal could import from Kenya

1. **Town hall convenor model for citizen and ecosystem consultation**. Portugal can adapt this for future ANIA revisions by naming **regional convenors** (e.g., FabLab Lisboa, FabLab Oeiras, the AI4COVID network, IST Tagus Park, INESC Porto, U.Algarve Faro hub) to host structured ANIA-revision town halls covering Norte, Centro, Lisboa, Alentejo, Algarve, Açores, and Madeira. This addresses Portugal's known centre-vs-interior gap that Argentina's summary also flagged.

2. **Disability advocacy organisation as a named co-developer**. Portugal's IV pillar (Responsibility) could explicitly include a Portuguese disability federation (e.g., FENACERCI, ACAPO) as a named co-developer of inclusion KPIs.

3. **"Cultural preservation and contextualization" as an explicit guiding principle**. Portugal's natural framing is **"linguistic and cultural specificity"** — covering Portuguese-language AMALIA, Lusophone variants, and the cultural specificity of Portuguese institutions. The principle would give the AMALIA initiative (II.7) a stronger frame.

4. **Multilingual citizen-facing AI applications at flagship level**, mirroring Kenya's health/education/agriculture multilingual use cases. For Portugal, this means naming flagship multilingual applications across the Portuguese variant spectrum (PT-PT, PT-BR, Cape Verdean Creole as relevant immigrant community language, English for digital-economy reach).

5. **SLOC analysis published in the ANIA itself**. Portugal cites strengths and opportunities in introduction but does not publish a structured SLOC matrix. Doing so would give the strategy clearer baseline analysis.

6. **PWDs + gender as explicit KPIs** in the Responsibility or Talent pillars. Portugal does not currently publish these KPIs.

7. **Public AI literacy KPI as a headline metric** (e.g., "% of Portuguese adults reporting basic AI literacy by 2027/2029"). Portugal has the III.8 initiative but no equivalent headline metric.

8. **Standardised data-centre count target with named standard (TIA 942 or equivalent)**. Portugal's I.4 (National Data Centre Plan) could name a count and standard.

### What Portugal should NOT copy

- **The donor-funded development model** — Portugal does not need EU+BMZ funding to develop its own AI strategy; replicating Kenya's donor-funded model would be redundant and politically inappropriate given Portugal's EU member-state status.
- **The 88-page discursive length** — Portugal's 22-page compact format with per-initiative cards is the better template for an operational strategy.
- **The absence of per-flagship budgets** — Portugal's specificity is the better template.

### Strategic complementarity with other comparators

Kenya's participatory methodology and inclusion framing combine especially well with UK operational depth and Spain's per-initiative budget transparency. An ideal Portuguese ANIA evolution drawing from Kenya specifically would adopt:

- **Kenyan multi-method consultation methodology** (town halls + KIIs + FGDs) for the next ANIA revision
- **Kenyan "local first + cultural preservation" principle pair** (adapted as Portuguese linguistic and cultural specificity)
- **Kenyan PWD + gender KPI structure** in the Responsibility pillar
- **Kenyan public AI literacy KPI** as a headline metric
- **Kenyan multilingual citizen-facing flagship use cases** in health, education, agriculture, public-service delivery
