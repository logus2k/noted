# Singapore — National AI Strategy 2.0 (NAIS 2.0, December 2023)

**Source:** [singapore_ national_ai_strategy_2023.pdf](../../domains/national_ai_agendas/sources/singapore_%20national_ai_strategy_2023.pdf), 68 pages. Singapore Government. **Foreword by Lawrence Wong, Deputy Prime Minister & Minister for Finance** [p.3] (now Prime Minister Wong since May 2024). Published **4 December 2023** [p.66].

**Title strap:** "AI for the Public Good, For Singapore and the World"

## Document character

**NAIS 2.0** is the **second iteration** of Singapore's National AI Strategy, succeeding **NAIS 1.0 (2019)** — Singapore was one of the first countries globally to publish a national AI strategy. NAIS 2.0 was developed in response to:
- The 2022 ChatGPT release and the generative-AI breakthrough
- Greater concerns over AI safety/security risks (especially LLM hallucinations, inscrutability)
- Intensifying global competition for AI resources and talent
- Need to move "from Projects to Systems"

It is the **AI strategy of the world's most AI-mature small state**. Singapore ranks 1st per capita on Stanford Global AI Vibrancy Index (2021), 2nd on Oxford Insights Government AI Index (2021), and 3rd on Tortoise Media Global AI Index (2022) [p.7, fn 2]. This makes it a particularly relevant peer for Portugal — a small EU state aiming above its weight.

## Vision: "AI for the Public Good"

**Core conviction:** AI is "strategically important" and a "force for good" that must be steered to:
- Unlock the next frontier of economic growth
- Address risks from potential abuse and mismanagement

**Twin goals** [p.13]:
- **Excellence** — selectively develop peaks of excellence in AI
- **Empowerment** — raise up individuals, businesses, communities to use AI with confidence, discernment, trust

## Three shifts from NAIS 1.0 [p.10]

| From | To |
|---|---|
| Opportunity | **Necessity** — people "must know" AI |
| Local | **Global** — world-leading ambition |
| Projects | **Systems** — beyond flagship National AI Projects |

## Structure — 3 Systems × 10 Enablers × 15 Actions

This is the most architecturally distinctive framework in the comparator set. Every part of the strategy maps to one of three "Systems," each with named "Enablers" and concrete "Actions":

### System 1: Activity Drivers (Enablers 1-3, Actions 1-4)

**Enabler 1: Industry**
- **Action 1**: Anchor new **AI Centres of Excellence (CoEs)** across companies + explore **Sectoral AI CoEs** in key sectors
- **Action 2**: Strengthen AI start-up ecosystem (venture builders + accelerator programmes)

**Enabler 2: Government**
- **Action 3**: Improve Public Service productivity via AI (Smart Nation Priorities + Whole-of-Government functional domains: Finance, HR, Service Delivery)

**Enabler 3: Research**
- **Action 4**: Update national AI R&D plans across **5 dimensions**: Research Priorities, Industry-Academia Nexus, Talent, Compute, International Collaborations

### System 2: People & Communities (Enablers 4-6, Actions 5-9)

**Enabler 4: Talent**
- **Action 5**: Attract world's top **AI Creators** to work from and with Singapore
- **Action 6**: **Boost AI Practitioner pool to 15,000**

**Enabler 5: Capabilities**
- **Action 7**: Intensify enterprise AI adoption for industry transformation
- **Action 8**: Upskill workforce through sector-specific AI training programmes (via Industry Transformation Maps / Jobs Transformation Maps)

**Enabler 6: Placemaking**
- **Action 9**: Establish an **iconic AI site** to co-locate AI Creators and Practitioners, nurture the AI community

### System 3: Infrastructure & Environment (Enablers 7-10, Actions 10-15)

**Enabler 7: Compute**
- **Action 10**: Significantly increase high-performance compute (industry use + meritorious use cases via small subset)

**Enabler 8: Data**
- **Action 11**: Build capabilities in **data services and Privacy-Enhancing Technologies (PETs)**
- **Action 12**: Unlock Government data via a **"data concierge"** for high-value Public Good use cases

**Enabler 9: Trusted Environment**
- **Action 13**: Fit-for-purpose regulatory environment for AI (Model AI Governance Framework + **AI Verify**)
- **Action 14**: Raise security and resilience baseline for AI

**Enabler 10: Leader in Thought and Action**
- **Action 15**: Establish Singapore as an ambitious and pragmatic international partner on AI innovation and governance

## The "Creators / Practitioners / Users" trichotomy [p.33]

NAIS 2.0 segments AI talent into three explicit categories:

| Category | Definition | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| **AI Creators** | Top-tier AI talent driving leading-edge R&D | Attract world's top from industry + academia |
| **AI Practitioners** | Tech workers translating innovation into products | **Target: 15,000** |
| **AI Users** | Enterprises and general workforce using AI tools | Capability uplift via existing schemes |

This **structured talent trichotomy** is distinct from how other comparators frame the talent question. Portugal's III.2 (Smart Skills Framework) is more general.

## Distinctive Singaporean institutional mechanisms

### AI Verify [Action 13, p.56]
**World's first AI governance testing framework + open-source software toolkit**. Released by IMDA in May 2022 for international pilot; open-sourced June 2023; **AI Verify Foundation** set up June 2023 with **90+ corporate members**. 11 AI ethics principles consistent with internationally recognised frameworks. **The single most directly importable instrument from this strategy.**

### AI Trailblazers [p.17]
World's first rapid prototyping initiative (July 2023): MCI + DISG + SNDGO + Google Cloud. **100 GenAI use cases in 100 days**. Free Google Cloud access for up to 3 months. Spurred experimentation across private + public sectors.

### Pair [p.25]
LLM-powered suite of tools for Government officers (built on the same LLMs underlying ChatGPT). Includes Pair Chat — **thousands of public servants** regularly use it for productivity. Comparable to UK's GOV.UK Chat but specifically scoped to internal civil-service productivity.

### OneService Chatbot [p.24]
Municipal complaints via WhatsApp/Telegram. AI auto-classifies complaints + extracts details + routes to correct government agency. By Municipal Services Office + Government Technology Agency (GovTech).

### 100 Experiments (100E) [p.29]
AISG flagship programme: industry problem statement → AISG matches with public researchers → co-funding up to **S$330,000/project**. Examples:
- **Q&M Dental Group's EM2AI** — AI dental X-ray analysis, deployed across **150 clinics** in Singapore + Malaysia
- **Tencent Lightspeed Studios** (PUBG Mobile maker) — text-to-speech in Malay

### AI Apprenticeship Programme (AIAP) [p.36, fn 5]
9-month full-time deep-skilling company-led training programme. Trainees work alongside industry teams on real business problems. To be **scaled up significantly** under NAIS 2.0.

### TechSkills Accelerator (TeSA) [p.37]
IMDA + SkillsFuture Singapore + Workforce Singapore + NTUC. Company-Led Training + Career Conversion Programmes. **2,700+ individuals placed** in good AI/data jobs.

### Singapore Conference on AI (SCAI) [p.31]
Inaugural December 2023 — 40+ local + overseas top AI experts gathered to articulate top research problems.

### RAISE.SG [p.44]
Inaugural July 2023 — 27 Singapore-linked AI Creators/Practitioners gathered. To recur regularly.

### Asia Tech x Singapore (ATxSG) [p.63]
17,000+ global leaders + industry decision-makers. ATxAI track: 64 speakers + 2,900 attendees over 3 years.

### PET Sandbox [p.52]
IMDA, **launched July 2022** — Singapore's first sandbox for Privacy-Enhancing Technologies. Matchmakes use-case owners to PET solution providers + grant support + regulatory support.

### Data Concierge [Action 12, p.53]
Government-as-broker function: engages AI Activity Drivers, helps them discover + access public sector datasets, brokers data sharing agreements, can work upstream with agencies to collect data based on AI developer needs, also brokers private-sector datasets for high-value Public Good use cases.

### AI Singapore (AISG) [p.7]
Dedicated national AI institution. **S$500M+ committed** through AISG under Research, Innovation and Enterprise (RIE) 2020 and 2025 plans.

### Forum of Small States (FOSS) [p.62, fn 7]
Singapore established FOSS in 1992 as an informal grouping of small states — **108 member countries** now, Singapore is permanent Chair. **Digital FOSS** pillar added in 2022. Singapore positions AI capacity-building for the 108 FOSS members as a diplomatic deliverable.

### Sustainability framing [Action 10, p.49]
**Singapore is the only comparator in the set to explicitly name carbon, power, water, and land as binding constraints** on compute build-out. "Careful management is needed, as this infrastructure will take up significant power, carbon, water, and land, all of which are limited in Singapore." Net-zero green data centres on renewable energy as medium-long-term roadmap.

## Existing base cited [p.7-8]

- **80+** active AI research faculty
- **150** AI R&D and product teams
- **1,100** AI start-ups
- **S$500M+** committed via AISG under RIE 2020 + 2025

International postings:
- **GPAI member** (Global Partnership on AI)
- WEF AI Governance Alliance
- **UN High-Level Advisory Body on AI** — Dr He Ruimin (Singapore's Chief AI Officer + Deputy Chief Digital Technology Officer) selected as one of 39 experts
- Hosts the Singapore Conference on AI (SCAI) + Asia Tech x Singapore (ATxSG)
- Convenes Forum of Small States (FOSS), 108 members

## Strategic posture relative to Portugal

### Where Singapore and Portugal align most closely

Both are **small states aiming above their weight in AI**. Both prioritise public-good framing + responsible deployment. Both face talent pool scarcity. Both are open trading economies with strong international diplomatic networks. The structural match is very close.

### Where they diverge fundamentally

1. **Regulatory anchor**: Singapore is outside EU and uses its own **Model AI Governance Framework + AI Verify**, both pro-innovation and globally-pragmatic. Portugal anchors to **EU AI Act + GDPR**.

2. **Industrial policy model**: Singapore is **highly state-directed at a fine-grained level** — AISG, IMDA, EDB, SkillsFuture, Workforce Singapore, NTUC all coordinate as one. Portugal's 15+ entities are scattered without comparable coordination.

3. **Geographic constraints**: Singapore explicitly manages compute against carbon/power/water/land limits. Portugal has different constraints (more land, renewable energy advantage, submarine cable hub) and an entirely different geographic story (Atlantic-Iberian-Lusophone).

## What Singapore does that Portugal's ANIA does NOT

1. **AI Verify open-source governance testing toolkit** [Action 13, p.56] — world's first; 90+ corporate members in the Foundation. Portugal IV.6 (EU AI Act implementation guide) is still in preparation. **AI Verify is the single most directly importable instrument** — Portugal could either adopt it (open-source, freely usable) or develop an EU-AI-Act-aligned Portuguese variant.

2. **AI Creators / Practitioners / Users trichotomy** [p.33] as the explicit talent-strategy framework. Portugal's III.2 (Smart Skills Framework) is at higher abstraction level; this trichotomy would sharpen ANIA's talent strategy.

3. **15,000 AI Practitioner target** [Action 6, p.36]. Portugal has talent ambition (III.5 AI Fast Track visa, III.6 National AI Week) but **no quantified Practitioner pipeline target**.

4. **AI Apprenticeship Programme (AIAP)** [p.36] — 9-month full-time deep-skilling company-led training. Portugal has Doutor AP (III.1) but no equivalent industry-AI apprenticeship instrument.

5. **100 Experiments (100E)** [p.29] — industry-academia matchmaking with S$330K co-funding per project. Portugal has II.3 (R&D incentives for companies) but lacks the named, sized, scaled industry-academia matchmaking programme.

6. **AI Trailblazers world-first rapid-prototyping initiative** [p.17] — 100 GenAI use cases in 100 days with hyperscaler partner. Portugal has no equivalent.

7. **Pair LLM-powered government productivity tool** [p.25] — thousands of regular civil servant users. Portugal AMA + INA have no equivalent named tool. Closest UK equivalent is GOV.UK Chat (still in pilot).

8. **OneService municipal-complaints AI chatbot** [p.24] — WhatsApp/Telegram + auto-classification + auto-routing. Portugal Portal de Cidadão + ePortugal could be enhanced with such an AI layer.

9. **Data concierge** [Action 12, p.53] — Government-as-broker for public + private sector datasets. Portugal I.3 (sectoral data spaces) is comparable in spirit but lacks the concierge framing.

10. **PET Sandbox** [p.52] — Privacy-Enhancing Technologies sandbox launched July 2022. Portugal IV.5 (regulatory sandboxes) is still being defined.

11. **Iconic AI site (placemaking)** [Action 9, p.43] — explicit national physical place for AI Creators + Practitioners co-location. Portugal has Lisbon AI ecosystem but no national named-place strategy.

12. **Three Foundational AI themes** (Reasoning AI, Resource-Efficient AI, Responsible AI) [p.18] — explicit research priorities. Portugal's 5 strategic bets [ANIA p.7] are at higher abstraction; Singapore's are sharper.

13. **Sectoral AI CoEs** [Action 1, p.21] — explicit institutional template for sector-specific AI value creation. Portugal II.5 (Sectoral AI Centres) is comparable in spirit but smaller in scope.

14. **AISG (AI Singapore) as a dedicated national AI institution** with S$500M+ multi-year budget. Portugal has FCT but no dedicated single-purpose AI institution comparable.

15. **Forum of Small States (FOSS) AI capacity-building for 108 members** [p.62]. Portugal could use **CPLP (Comunidade dos Países de Língua Portuguesa) + UE-LAC + Atlantic dimension** as analogues — Singapore's FOSS template is directly transferable.

16. **Sustainability constraints explicitly named** [Action 10, p.49] — carbon, power, water, land. Portugal has renewable-energy advantage [ANIA p.4] but does NOT bind compute build-out to explicit sustainability constraints. Singapore's framing is more rigorous.

17. **Whole-of-Nation Movement framing** [p.65] — explicit consensus-building call. Portugal's ANIA has institutional framing but lacks comparable national-movement rhetoric.

18. **Quantified existing base** — 80 research faculty + 150 AI R&D teams + 1,100 startups + S$500M committed. Portugal ANIA cites 552 AI companies + €181M raised but with less institutional depth.

## What Portugal does that Singapore's NAIS 2.0 does NOT

1. **EU AI Act anchoring** — Singapore is outside EU.

2. **Per-initiative semester start dates** — Portugal's 32 cards with 2S 2025 → 1S 2027 sequencing. NAIS 2.0's 15 Actions are over a "3-5 year" horizon without per-action sequencing.

3. **Per-initiative responsible entities** [ANIA p.11-21] — Portugal lists "Relevant Entities" per card. NAIS 2.0 names lead agencies more implicitly.

4. **Macroeconomic GDP target** (€18-22B/yr GDP add, +2.7pp productivity) [ANIA p.3]. NAIS 2.0 lacks comparable aggregate figure (it has the S$500M+ AISG commitment but as an investment, not GDP impact).

5. **Six guiding principles** [ANIA p.6] — Portugal's clean conceptual scaffolding. NAIS 2.0 has twin goals (Excellence + Empowerment) + three shifts but no equivalent set of guiding principles.

6. **EU AI Continent + Apply AI strategy alignment** as anchoring — Portugal IV.4 references the EU regulatory pillar; Singapore explicitly avoids alignment with any particular regional bloc, preferring "pragmatic" multi-stakeholder positioning.

## Critique-lens conclusion

Singapore's NAIS 2.0 is **the closest small-state peer in the entire comparator set** for Portugal. Both are small countries with EU-equivalent regulatory ambitions, strong AI research bases, talent-pipeline challenges, and large international diplomatic networks (CPLP for Portugal, FOSS for Singapore). Singapore's **15-Action operational architecture is the most directly transferable framework** to Portugal's ANIA after accounting for EU-regulatory framework anchoring.

### Top moves Portugal should import

1. **Adopt AI Verify or build a Portuguese AI Verify equivalent.** Singapore's AI Verify open-source toolkit with 90+ corporate members is **directly available as open-source**. Portugal could (a) adopt it as the practical tool for IV.6 (EU AI Act implementation guide), customised for Portuguese language + EU compliance; or (b) join the AI Verify Foundation as a corporate member. **This is the single most importable Singapore move.**

2. **Adopt the Creators / Practitioners / Users trichotomy** as the explicit talent-strategy framework. Rewrite Portugal III.2 (Smart Skills Framework) around this three-tier segmentation. Set quantified targets for each tier (e.g., "5,000 Portuguese AI Practitioners by 2030" mirroring Singapore's 15,000).

3. **Establish a Portuguese AI Apprenticeship Programme (PT-AIAP)**, modelled on AISG's AIAP. 9-month full-time deep-skilling, company-led, integrated with II.4 (non-academic PhDs) and III.1 (Doutor AP). Combine with Italy's "high apprenticeship" tax-relief framing.

4. **Launch a Portuguese 100 Experiments (PT-100E)** — industry-academia matchmaking with EUR co-funding per project (e.g., €100k-€300k range). Anchored under II.5 (Sectoral AI Centres) + FCT. Document successful pilots as Portuguese case studies (à la UK Playbook + Italy flagship-project monitoring).

5. **Launch a Portuguese AI Trailblazers** — partner with one or two cloud hyperscalers (AWS, Microsoft, Google) for rapid-prototyping of 30-50 use cases in 100 days across Portuguese public + private sector. Anchored to the Portuguese AI Workshop for AP (proposed in UK summary). World's-first framing if Portugal acts quickly.

6. **Adopt the "Three Foundational AI Themes" structure** at the research priority level — alongside Portugal's 5 strategic bets, name 3 foundational AI research priorities (e.g., Portuguese-Language AI, Resource-Efficient AI for Energy Transition, Responsible AI / EU AI Act compliance research).

7. **Establish a Portuguese Data Concierge function** under ANIA I.3 (Data Spaces) — Government-as-broker for public + private sector datasets, with the Centro para a IA Responsável (IV.3) playing the role.

8. **Operationalise IV.5 Regulatory Sandbox as a PET Sandbox first**, modelled on IMDA's July 2022 launch. PET sandbox is lower-controversy starting point than full EU-AI-Act sandbox, and addresses GDPR + IV.6 (AI Act guide) needs.

9. **Establish a Portuguese iconic AI site** — explicit national named-place strategy. Lisbon has Hub Criativo do Beato, Beato Innovation District; Porto has Porto Tech Hub. Branding one of these as **"Pólo Nacional de IA"** would mirror Singapore's Placemaking strategy.

10. **Build a Portuguese AI Creators / Practitioners / Users database** — quantify Portugal's current base. Singapore publishes 80 + 150 + 1100. Portugal cites 552 AI companies but should also publish: # AI research faculty (FCT-registered), # AI R&D teams, # AI startups, # AI Practitioners working in PT-resident companies. **Without this baseline, Portugal cannot benchmark growth.**

11. **Frame CPLP as Portugal's FOSS analogue** — Singapore convenes 108 small states via FOSS + Digital FOSS pillar. Portugal already convenes 9-member CPLP + has Atlantic dimension (~260M Lusophone speakers). NAIS 2.0 Action 15 (international partner) is the template. **CPLP + EU-LAC AI capacity-building** could be a peer pillar in ANIA.

12. **Explicit sustainability constraints on compute** [Singapore Action 10] — Portugal renewable-energy advantage should bind ANIA I.4 (National Data Centre Plan) to net-zero targets. Singapore's framing: name carbon, power, water, land as binding inputs.

13. **AI Verify Foundation–style open-source-AI-governance community** — Singapore has 90+ corporate members. Portugal could anchor a **Lusophone AI Governance Foundation** with Brazilian, Mozambican, Angolan, and other CPLP corporate participation.

14. **"AI for Public Good" framing** — distinctive among comparators. Portugal's ANIA mentions "social well-being" and "public value" but doesn't use the "Public Good" framing as headline. Adopting it would align with EU AI Continent + Apply AI strategy.

15. **Whole-of-Nation movement rhetoric** — Singapore's framing for consensus-building. Portugal's ANIA is government-issued but could benefit from a "national movement" framing to coordinate Estado + universities + companies + civil society.

### What Portugal should NOT copy

- **Singapore's state-directed industrial coordination at fine grain** — IMDA + EDB + AISG + SkillsFuture + WSG + NTUC work as a single coordinated machine. Portugal's institutional landscape is structurally different and replicating this would require constitutional/administrative changes well beyond ANIA.
- **Singapore's regulatory autonomy** — Portugal correctly anchors to EU AI Act. Singapore-style "fit-for-purpose, pragmatic" regulation is incompatible with EU framework.
- **Singapore's geographic-constraint framing as a primary driver** — Singapore's sustainability + land constraint is acute; Portugal has the opposite (land + renewable energy abundance). Don't import the same constraint logic.

### Synthesis with other comparators

Singapore's framework + Spain's per-initiative budget transparency + UK's case-study documentation + Italy's Foundation-under-PM coordination model + France's per-recommendation Ministry assignment **= the ideal evolution path for ANIA**. The 13-country comparator set is now substantially complete; Portugal has a comprehensive design space to choose from.
