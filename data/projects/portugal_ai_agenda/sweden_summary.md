# Sweden — AI Strategy + Action Plan (2026)

**Sources:**
- [swedens-ai-strategy.pdf](../../domains/national_ai_agendas/sources/swedens-ai-strategy.pdf), 24 pages. "Production: Government Offices of Sweden 2026" [p.2]. Foreword signed by **Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson** and **Minister for Public Administration Erik Slottner** [p.3].
- [sweden_action-plan-for-swedens-ai-strategy.pdf](../../domains/national_ai_agendas/sources/sweden_action-plan-for-swedens-ai-strategy.pdf), 13 pages — annex with adopted and planned measures, each anchored to specific Government Bills, Committee Reports, Riksdag Communications, and Terms of Reference numbers.

**Replaces** Sweden's 2018 domestic approach for AI (N2018/03008) [Strategy p.23]. Built on the AI Commission's *Roadmap for Sweden* (SOU 2025:12) and *Sweden's Digitalisation Strategy 2025-2030* (Fi2025/01181) [Strategy p.6].

## Document character

This is the **only document in the set published in 2026** alongside Portugal's ANIA, making them the two most contemporaneous strategies. Sweden's split into a **Strategy + Action Plan** is an explicit structural choice — the Strategy sets direction, the Action Plan is an exhaustively-cited list of already-adopted government decisions and planned measures. Each Action Plan bullet references the specific statutory instrument (Gov't Bill, Committee Report, Riksdag Communication, Terms of Reference, ordinance number, etc.).

The combined effect is a strategy with **>50 specific government measures already in motion** at the time of publication, including ~100+ government agencies given AI/data assignments [Action plan p.4]. **Strategic continuity-of-execution is the document's calling card.**

## Vision and target

- **Top 10 globally in AI** [Strategy p.6, p.7]
- AI should be used and developed in pursuit of: (1) **societal benefit**, (2) **sustainable development**, (3) **competitiveness and innovation** [Strategy p.7]
- Sweden positions itself as leader in **climate-smart, competitive computing capacity** for both private and public sectors [Strategy p.20]
- Posture is **EU-aligned + Nordic-Baltic-clustered + NATO-interoperable** [Strategy p.11, p.20]

## Structure — 3 domains × ~14 sub-themes

| Domain | Sub-themes |
|---|---|
| **AI for societal benefit** | Legal framework & regulatory simplification; Data access and utilisation; Standardisation; Security and defence; **A public sector in the vanguard** [p.9-13] |
| **Sustainable development** | Human-centric approach; **Language models built on material from Sweden**; Climate and energy; Labour market and skills supply [p.14-17] |
| **AI for competitiveness and innovation** | The AI ecosystem; Business and entrepreneurship; **Digital infrastructure and computing capacity**; Research and expertise [p.19-22] |

## Standout institutional mechanisms

### 1. AI Workshop for Public Administration [Strategy p.13; Action plan p.6]

The single most distinctive operational mechanism. Foundation laid 2026, **fully operational by 2030**. It is simultaneously:
- A shared, secure, robust AI infrastructure for the public sector
- A national competence centre for procurement, legal, and ethical guidance
- A development+test+share venue for public AI solutions
- A vehicle for uniform high-grade information security across agencies

**Operationalised by:** Swedish Social Insurance Agency (Försäkringskassan) + Swedish Tax Agency (Skatteverket) jointly, with dedicated funding via Budget Bill 2025/26 [Action plan p.6, Fi2025/00018 + Fi2025/01523].

**Why it's distinctive:** It combines infrastructure + center-of-excellence + procurement-hub + standards-body in a single named institution. Portugal's II.11 (AI Centre of Excellence in PA) covers a slice; Sweden's AI Workshop covers all four dimensions with a 2030 completion target.

### 2. Mimer — Sweden's EU AI Factory at Linköping University [Strategy p.20; Action plan p.11]

Operational through EuroHPC JU. Provides researchers, companies, and start-ups access to compute, with Swedish Research Council + Vinnova co-financing. Sweden has **secured** an EU AI Factory (Spain is a candidate; Portugal mentions Gigafactory ambition without comparable progress).

The Government also welcomes plans for EU-wide **AI Gigafactories** and seeks one in Sweden, conditional on financing and energy requirements [Strategy p.20].

### 3. National Library of Sweden + National Archives as Foundation-Model Training-Data Providers [Action plan p.7]

Two cultural-heritage institutions explicitly funded to:
- **National Library**: analyse and prepare wider use of its language models in the public sector (interim report Jan 2026, final June 2026); increased appropriation for digital storage + compute + AI workshop participation [Action plan p.7]
- **Swedish National Archives**: continued digitisation of archives to enable training of AI models on **material from Sweden**

This makes cultural heritage → language model a deliberate sovereign-data pipeline. Distinctive among the comparators (Spain uses BSC-CNS as the technical anchor; Portugal uses FCT and academia).

### 4. Polar Connect — Strategic Submarine Cable Initiative [Action plan p.11]

Direct Swedish investment in undersea cables connecting:
- Nordic region → Indo-Pacific (via Arctic route, shortest to Asia)
- Nordic region → North America

**Funding:** SEK 23M in 2025 + SEK 25M in 2026 + SEK 30M in 2027 + SEK 40M in 2028. This is an explicit **geopolitical infrastructure** play — alternative routing for digital traffic.

### 5. Energy Performance of Data Centres Act (2025:570) [Action plan p.8]

**Already in force from 1 July 2025.** Data centres ≥500 kW IT power must publish energy performance to a common EU database. Sweden has already legislated AI-energy transparency — Spain is transposing the EU directive, Portugal mentions renewables as advantage but has no equivalent instrument.

### 6. Nordic-Baltic AI Centre with coordinating office in Stockholm [Action plan p.10]

Nordic Council of Ministers initiative. Mandate:
- Accelerate innovation and responsible AI use
- Increase cross-border cooperation and integration
- Attract international talent

A **geographic-cluster strategy** Portugal has no equivalent to (Iberian counterpart with Spain is the obvious analogue).

### 7. Implementation Council [Action plan p.3, Terms of Reference 2024:51]

A government body whose explicit mandate is to:
- "Emphasise the entrepreneurial perspective at an early stage of the EU process"
- **"Work to ensure that EU legislation is not implemented in Sweden beyond the specified minimum level"**

This is an **anti-gold-plating instrument** — a permanent governance mechanism to prevent over-implementation of EU rules. Politically distinctive. Portugal has no equivalent.

### 8. Real-time Biometric Facial Recognition Law for Police [Action plan p.5]

The Government has proposed a new act allowing the Swedish Police Authority and Security Service to use AI for **real-time facial recognition and biometric remote identification in public places** to combat crime (currently before the Council on Legislation). Operates within EU AI Act high-risk-system exception. Most explicit law-enforcement AI mandate in this comparator set.

### 9. Team Sweden AI [Action plan p.10]

Government initiative to invite Swedish stakeholders into a unified branding/coordination vehicle for international AI positioning. Built on the established "Team Sweden" pattern used for trade promotion.

### 10. Cybercampus Sweden at Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences [Action plan p.12]

National cooperation across universities + research bodies + government agencies + businesses for cybersecurity skills and research. Funded via increased appropriations to universities.

### 11. National Space Data Programme [Action plan p.12]

Swedish National Space Agency tasked with establishing a national space data programme. Explicit synergies with AI, security, digital and connected systems. Leverages **Esrange — the EU's only mainland-based space centre** [Strategy p.22].

### 12. Sweden's Authority for Privacy Protection AI Sandbox [Action plan p.3]

Already operational; appropriation increased via Budget Bill 2025/26 to develop the sandbox further. Aim is explicitly **to facilitate AI use by public administration**, not just to test private-sector compliance.

### 13. Data Steward Function via Statistics Sweden + Agency for Digital Government [Action plan p.4]

National guidance function for **good data management** embedded across public administration. Statistics Sweden's appropriation increased to establish it.

### 14. Simplification Council within Swedish Agency for Economic and Regional Growth [Action plan p.3]

Established by amending the Agency's instructions ordinance (2009:145). Mandate is to reduce companies' regulatory burden.

### 15. AI Commission's Roadmap (SOU 2025:12) [Strategy p.6]

Sweden ran a **dedicated AI Commission** that produced an independent Roadmap, which is one of the explicit bases for this Strategy. Independent commission as input to government strategy is a pattern Portugal does not use.

### 16. STEM Strategy + STEM Delegation [Action plan p.9]

Separate dedicated national strategy on Science, Technology, Engineering, Mathematics. STEM Delegation appointed; reports by 2 December 2027. Dedicated funding to Swedish Council for Higher Education for STEM recruitment.

Sweden does **not publish an aggregate AI budget number** comparable to Spain's €600M-deployed-plus-€2B-forward total. Instead, every measure cites its specific funding line through the Riksdag's Budget Bill, with Committee Report and Riksdag Communication numbers identifying the exact appropriation increase.

## Distinctive style and governance

- **Statutory citation density** — every Action Plan bullet anchored to specific Gov't Bill, Committee Report, Riksdag Communication, Terms of Reference, or ordinance number. Reads more like an annotated bibliography of government decisions than a strategy document. This is the **most legally-anchored AI strategy** in the comparator set.
- **Anti-gold-plating posture** — the Implementation Council [Action plan p.3] and recurring "regulatory simplification" framing make this an explicit *less-regulation-than-EU-minimum* stance, contrasting with Spain's *anticipate-the-EU-AI-Act* posture or Portugal's *EU-aligned* anchoring.
- **Annual review** linked to Digitalisation Strategy follow-up [Strategy p.23]
- Implementation supported by **Agency for Digital Government + Swedish Post and Telecom Authority** [Strategy p.23, Fi2026/0000]
- Strategy also reviewed by **OECD** within Digitalisation Strategy follow-up framework
- **EU + Nordic-Baltic + NATO** geopolitical triangle (rather than Portugal's EU-centric anchoring)

## International posture

- Strong EU alignment (EU AI Act 2024/1689, European Health Data Space Regulation 2025/327, EuroHPC JU)
- Nordic-Baltic cluster cooperation (Nordic Council of Ministers initiative, joint Nordic-Baltic AI Centre)
- NATO interoperability noted in security/defence section [Strategy p.11]
- Explicit Polar Connect investment for alternative submarine cable routing
- Active in international standards bodies (Strategy p.11)
- UNESCO ethics guidelines + Council of Europe Framework Convention on AI cited as governing references [Strategy p.15]

## What Sweden does that Portugal's ANIA does NOT

1. **AI Workshop for Public Administration** — combined infrastructure + competence-centre + procurement-hub + ethics-guidance institution with named operational owners (Försäkringskassan + Skatteverket) and a 2030 target. Portugal II.11 (PA Centre of Excellence) is narrower.

2. **National Library + National Archives as named training-data providers** for Swedish-language AI. Portugal has no equivalent institutional pipeline for Portuguese-language sovereign corpus (AMALIA is mentioned without an analogous data-feeding institution).

3. **Energy Performance of Data Centres Act in force** since July 2025 [Action plan p.8]. Portugal claims renewables as competitive advantage [Portugal ANIA p.4] but has no equivalent legislative instrument.

4. **Mimer EU AI Factory operational** at Linköping. Portugal's I.2 Gigafactory is "to attract and install."

5. **Polar Connect submarine cable investment** (SEK 118M 2025-2028) — explicit geopolitical-infrastructure positioning. Portugal references its submarine-cable advantage [Portugal ANIA p.4] but does not commit specific investment to extending/protecting routes.

6. **Implementation Council** as a permanent anti-gold-plating governance body. Portugal does not have an equivalent.

7. **Anti-gold-plating posture** more generally — Portugal's IV.4 (EU AI Act implementation) anchors **to** the EU framework, while Sweden's strategy anchors **against over-implementation**. Different political stance.

8. **Real-time facial-recognition law for police** (proposed) — Sweden goes further than EU peers within the AI Act exception. Portugal has no equivalent operational law-enforcement AI initiative.

9. **Nordic-Baltic AI Centre + Team Sweden AI + Cybercampus Sweden + STEM Strategy + STEM Delegation** — five named institutional anchors for cluster building, international positioning, cyber-AI integration, and talent supply. Portugal's equivalents (goPortugal, III.5 visa, IV.3 Centre for Responsible AI) are individually fine but lack the institutional density.

10. **National Space Data Programme with explicit AI/security/digital synergies** [Action plan p.12]. Portugal has Atlantic+ space ambitions but no equivalent AI-space integration in ANIA.

11. **AI Commission's independent Roadmap (SOU 2025:12)** as input to the strategy. Portugal does not use independent expert commissions for its AI agenda.

12. **Statutory anchoring of every initiative** — the cited Government Bills + Committee Reports + Communications make Sweden's strategy verifiable at Riksdag level. Portugal's 32 initiative cards name entities but not statutory instruments.

13. **~100+ agency assignments already issued** at the time of strategy publication. Portugal's ANIA initiatives are mostly forthcoming.

14. **AI Workshop's 2030-operational target** as an explicit milestone for joint public-sector AI capability. Portugal's 32 initiatives mostly start 2S 2025 – 1S 2027 but no aggregated end-state milestone.

## What Portugal does that Sweden's strategy doesn't

1. **Quantified GDP impact target** (€18-22B/yr, +2.7pp productivity contribution, 8% GDP at peak) [Portugal ANIA p.3]. Sweden mentions "several billion kronor annually" public-sector savings potential but no aggregate macroeconomic case.

2. **Six guiding principles** [Portugal ANIA p.6]. Sweden has 3 high-level objectives but no equivalent guiding-principles scaffolding.

3. **Hybrid Development/Application strategic positioning** [Portugal ANIA p.7]. Sweden implicitly does both but doesn't articulate the choice.

4. **Strategic bets explicitly named** (Cybersecurity, LLMs, AI+Robotics for Industry, Blue Economy, Responsible AI) [Portugal ANIA p.7]. Sweden has 14 sub-themes but no equivalent prioritisation.

5. **AI Fast Track talent visa** instrument (III.5) [Portugal ANIA p.18]. Sweden has talent attraction language but no dedicated AI-specific visa.

6. **AI literacy citizen campaign** (III.8) + **AI Generation career campaign** (III.7) [Portugal ANIA p.18]. Sweden's labour-market sub-theme covers skills broadly but no equivalent named citizen-awareness campaign.

7. **Regulatory sandbox as a forward initiative** with named Portuguese-flagged scope (IV.5) [Portugal ANIA p.21]. Sweden's sandbox is at Authority for Privacy Protection — operational but narrower scope.

## Critique-lens conclusion

Sweden's 2026 AI Strategy is **a model for how to publish a strategy that's already in motion**. Portugal's ANIA pushes 32 initiatives starting 2S 2025 → 1S 2027 — Sweden publishes the strategy *after* ~50+ government measures are already adopted, with statutory anchors for each. This is partly a function of Sweden's two extra years of execution (the 2018 AI approach being superseded), but it's also a deliberate choice to make the strategy an annotated record rather than a forward plan.

**Top moves Portugal should import:**

1. **Establish a Portuguese AI Workshop for Public Administration.** Sweden's AI Workshop [Strategy p.13] combines four functions (infrastructure + competence centre + procurement guidance + ethics support) under named operational owners with a 2030 target. Portugal II.11 + II.12 + II.13 [Portugal ANIA p.15] could be consolidated into a single Portuguese AI Workshop with the Agência para a Modernização Administrativa (AMA) + INA as operational owners.

2. **Mandate Biblioteca Nacional de Portugal + Arquivo Nacional Torre do Tombo as AMALIA training-data providers.** Sweden's pattern at the National Library + National Archives [Action plan p.7] is directly portable. Funding earmarked for digitisation of Portuguese cultural-heritage material to feed AMALIA and successor models. This sharpens II.7 from "continuation of AMALIA" to a sovereign-data pipeline.

3. **Legislate an Energy Performance of Data Centres Act.** Sweden's Act (2025:570) [Action plan p.8] is already in force; Spain is transposing EU Directive 2023/1791. Portugal's I.4 (National Data Centre Plan) [Portugal ANIA p.11] should become a legislative instrument, not a planning document.

4. **Establish a Polar Connect-equivalent for Portuguese subsea infrastructure.** Portugal already has the strategic submarine-cable advantage [Portugal ANIA p.4] but does not commit investment to extending/protecting these routes. A named Portuguese instrument (e.g., "Atlantic Connect") committing multi-year funding to subsea cable strengthening would operationalise the existing advantage.

5. **Create a Portuguese Implementation Council** to anchor EU regulatory transposition at the minimum-necessary level for innovation impact. Portugal's ANIA correctly anchors to EU AI Act + GDPR [Portugal ANIA p.20-21] — adding an Implementation Council protects against over-implementation that would erode the Iberian peninsula's combined competitive position.

6. **Commission an Independent AI Commission report** equivalent to Sweden's AI Commission (SOU 2025:12). Portugal's 32 initiatives would benefit from a midpoint independent review (e.g., by Conselho Nacional de Ciência, Tecnologia e Inovação or a dedicated commission). Annual would be too frequent; a 2027/2028 midpoint review of ANIA implementation gives a structured re-planning moment.

7. **Add a Portuguese National Space Data Programme with AI synergies.** Sweden's NSP [Action plan p.12] explicitly ties AI + security + digital + space. Portugal's Atlantic+ ambitions and Azores satellite-tracking station are equivalent strategic assets — currently not connected to AI in ANIA.

8. **Statute-anchor every ANIA initiative.** Sweden's pattern of citing Government Bill, Committee Report, Riksdag Communication, Terms of Reference is operationally rigorous. Portugal could anchor each of its 32 initiatives to a specific Decreto-Lei, Despacho, Resolução do Conselho de Ministros, or PRR component. Currently the cards list "Relevant Entities" but not the legal instrument.

9. **Add a Portuguese "Team Portugal AI"** branding/coordination vehicle for international positioning. AICEP already operates "Team Portugal" for trade — extending to AI is a low-cost institutional move.

10. **A Portuguese Implementation Plan annex** mirroring Sweden's split between Strategy and Action Plan. The 32 initiative cards in ANIA are tighter than Sweden's prose but suffer from the absence of statutory anchoring. A companion document publishing the legal/administrative anchors for each initiative would close that gap.

**What Portugal should not copy:**
- The **anti-gold-plating stance** as a foundational governance principle — Portugal's EU-aligned positioning is differentiating (and serves Iberian-cluster cooperation with Spain); replicating Sweden's posture would dilute that.
- **Real-time biometric facial recognition for police** as a national strategic priority — this is a Swedish political choice that does not match Portugal's traditional civil-liberties posture and would be politically costly without commensurate operational benefit.
- The **Top-10-globally framing** as the primary measurable target — Sweden has the digital-infrastructure and research base to make this credible; Portugal's GDP-impact framing (€18-22B/yr) is sharper and more actionable.

The closest geopolitical analogue is **Spain (Iberian peer)**; the closest stylistic analogue is **Finland (Nordic peer)**. Sweden sits between them: structurally peer to Finland, operationally peer to Spain, with the unique addition of dense statutory anchoring and an explicit anti-gold-plating governance instrument.
