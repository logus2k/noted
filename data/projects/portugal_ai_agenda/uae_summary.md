# United Arab Emirates — UAE National Strategy for Artificial Intelligence 2031 (2018)

**Source:** [uae_national_strategy_for_ai.pdf](../../domains/national_ai_agendas/sources/uae_national_strategy_for_ai.pdf), 45 pages (text extracted with `pdftotext`). Office of the Minister of State for Artificial Intelligence — **His Excellency Omar Sultan Al Olama** (the world's first Minister of State for AI, appointed 2017) [p.6]. Released October 2018 alongside the World Government Summit. Frames itself as a cornerstone of "UAE Centennial 2071" [p.44].

## ⚠️ Caveat on framing

This is a **2018 document — six years pre-GenAI, eight years old at the time of this critique (2026)**. References are to AI as defined in 2016-2018 (Cognos, Power BI, Qlik Sense as "AI-relevant skills" [p.15]; AI market sized at "USD 60 billion by 2021" [p.26]). The UAE has since published successive updates (the UAE AI Charter, regulator opinions, sector-specific strategies under Falcon LLM, G42 ecosystem activities) but this PDF is the original national strategy. Use it as the **founding-document baseline**, not as the current UAE position.

## Vision and economic case [p.8, p.10-12]

- **Vision:** "World leader in AI by 2031," aligned with UAE Centennial 2071 (be the best country in the world by 2071).
- **Quantified opportunity:** **AED 335 billion** increase in economic output if automation reaches full extent (≈ 26% GDP uplift). PwC's parallel estimate: AED 353 billion by 2030 (13.6% of GDP).
- **Industry-level estimates published** [p.12 chart]: Finance/Professional Services AED 103B; Mining/Resources AED 86B; Construction & Retail AED 39B each; Logistics AED 33B; Manufacturing AED 19B; ICT AED 18B; Government & Social Services AED 14B; Utilities AED 13B; Hospitality AED 5B; Agriculture AED 2B — published per sector, a level of transparency Portugal's ANIA does not match.

## Five priority sectors [p.10-11, p.22-25]

- **Resources & Energy** (UAE is 5th-largest oil exporter; renewable energy + utilities innovation)
- **Logistics & Transport** (Dubai Airport 60M passengers/yr, Abu Dhabi 26M, Jebel Ali port — Middle East's largest)
- **Tourism & Hospitality** (consumer-AI as export sector)
- **Healthcare** (Dubai Genomics whole-genome programme; rare-disease focus given diverse population)
- **Cybersecurity** (regional hub already; global spending USD 1T+ over 5 years)

## Eight strategic objectives × concrete sub-initiatives [p.18-19, p.20-43]

| # | Objective | Concrete sub-initiatives |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build a reputation as an AI destination | **UAI Brand** — 4-level Seal of Approval (Public Sector / Private Sector / Institutional / Product) [p.20-21] |
| 2 | Increase competitive assets in priority sectors via AI deployment | **Proof-of-Concept Support in Priority Sectors** — government funds or brokers pilots across the 5 priority sectors [p.25] |
| 3 | Develop a fertile ecosystem for AI | **AI Network** (research/industry/policy expert network); **Applied AI Accelerator** (incubator); **AI Incentive Scheme for Overseas Companies**; **Business Support for UAE AI Firms** [p.26-29]. Mohammed bin Rashid Innovation Fund: **AED 2 billion** [p.26] |
| 4 | Adopt AI across customer services to improve lives & government | **National AI Challenges programme** (modeled on Australia's National Missions); **UAE Artificial Intelligence and Blockchain Council** (federal + emirate representation) [p.30-31] |
| 5 | Attract and train talent for future jobs enabled by AI | **Public AI Training** (UAE AI Summer Camp, 5000+ trained); **Upskilling Students** (target: **1/3 of UAE STEM graduates per year = 2000 students upskilled in AI**); **Government Training** (target: **100% of senior leadership** trained in AI); **Professional Upskilling**; **Employment Transition Support** [p.32-35] |
| 6 | Bring world-leading research capability | **National Virtual AI Institute** (aggregated capacity, not new university); **Key Thinkers Program** (attract global AI researchers to UAE); **AI Library** (open-access bilingual Arabic/English research) [p.36-39] |
| 7 | Provide data and infrastructure as a test bed for AI | **Data Sharing** (modeled on Estonia X-Road); **Secure Data Infrastructure** (modeled on Australia's SURE virtual data library) [p.40-41] |
| 8 | Ensure strong governance and effective regulation | **National Governance Review** (by AI & Blockchain Council); **Second Global Governance of AI Roundtable** (with UNESCO/OECD/IEEE); **Intergovernmental Panel on AI** ("IPCC for AI" — co-proposed with France/Macron's strategy) [p.42-43] |

## Existing baseline cited [p.14-16, p.32]

- **22% of UAE graduates in STEM** (vs 16% US, 26% UK, 31% India)
- **40% of UAE workforce has good digital skills** (vs 56% UK — the top-rated nation in 2017 AI Readiness Index)
- **45% of existing work activities** are automatable today by current tech (slightly below 50% global average)
- **70% of Emiratis employed in the public sector** — automation is a workforce-restructuring issue concentrated in government
- **~300,000 jobs** in UAE Administrative + Government may be impacted by automation; **~125,000 held by UAE nationals**
- **0.19% of population working as full-time researchers in S&T&I** [p.38 chart] — well below Korea (0.69%), Singapore (0.66%), UK (0.44%), US (0.42%) — UAE acknowledges weak domestic research base

## What's institutionally distinctive

1. **The world's first Minister of State for AI** [p.6] — appointed 2017, Omar Sultan Al Olama. This is the strategy's strongest single feature: AI has a **dedicated ministerial owner** with cabinet-level seat, not a programme inside another ministry.
2. **The "AI Office"** as a standing coordinating body [throughout]. Distinct from the Minister himself, the office brokers initiatives across emirates and ministries.
3. **UAE Artificial Intelligence and Blockchain Council** [p.30] — federal + emirate representation; main mandate is to identify where AI fits in government, and review national governance approaches.
4. **UAI Brand + 4-Level Seal of Approval** [p.20-21] — a national AI quality certification scheme. Issued for Public Sector, Private Sector, Institutional, and Product levels. Aimed at trust + global marketing.
5. **"IPCC for AI" proposal** [p.43] — co-developed with France's 2018 AI strategy (Villani report era). Multi-government scientific-advisory body for AI risk and impact assessment.
6. **Hard talent targets:** 2000 STEM graduates upskilled to AI per year; 100% of senior government leadership trained in AI. Quantified in a way Portugal's ANIA doesn't match.

## What the document explicitly lacks (or that has aged poorly)

- **No EU AI Act equivalent reference** — UAE federal data law came later (Personal Data Protection Law 2021). Strategy refers to GDPR as model but doesn't anchor to a domestic framework.
- **No regulatory sandbox** — Council reviews are the governance instrument, not codified sandboxes.
- **No carbon/sustainability dimension** — entirely absent.
- **No timeline per initiative** — all targets are 2031 horizon.
- **No budget total** — only AED 2B in MBR Innovation Fund cited; no consolidated AI-strategy envelope.
- **Research base is weak and acknowledged** [p.38] — UAE bets on **attracting** foreign talent rather than building domestic capacity in the medium term.
- **GenAI absent** (document predates ChatGPT by 4 years).
- **Defence/military AI not addressed** — strikingly absent for a national strategy in this geopolitical neighbourhood.

## What UAE does that Portugal's ANIA does NOT do

1. **A dedicated Minister of State for AI with the AI Office** as a permanent coordinating body. Portugal has **no AI minister** and ANIA's 15+ entities sit across IPAMEI, ARTE, FCT, ANI, BFP, Startup PT, IPQ, INA, AIMA, IEFP, DGERT, ANACOM, IMPIC, CNCS… without a single accountable ministerial owner [Portugal ANIA p.11-21].

2. **A national AI Seal of Approval** [p.20-21] — a 4-level quality certification mark for AI products/companies/institutions/public-sector deployments. Portugal IV.5 (sandboxes) + IV.6 (EU AI Act guide) [Portugal ANIA p.21] cover compliance, not branded quality. The UAI mark is a marketing instrument too — Portugal has nothing branded like this.

3. **Quantified talent targets** [p.33]: 2000 STEM graduates upskilled in AI per year; 100% senior government leadership trained. Portugal's III.1 (Doutor AP) and III.2 (Smart Skills Framework) [Portugal ANIA p.17] do not name **numeric coverage targets**.

4. **Per-industry GDP impact table** [p.12 chart] — UAE publishes AI's contribution to each industry separately. Portugal cites €18-22B/yr aggregate [Portugal ANIA p.3] but not a per-sector breakdown.

5. **An "AI Library" open-access bilingual repository** [p.39] — publicly funded research and government AI programmes summarised in Arabic + English. Portugal has no parallel public AI-knowledge resource.

6. **A formal IPCC-for-AI proposal** [p.43] with named multilateral partners (UNESCO, OECD, IEEE, French Government). Portugal IV.7 (technology diplomacy) [p.21] is much more abstract.

7. **Explicit "Key Thinkers Program"** [p.39] — programme to attract globally leading AI researchers for time-limited residencies. Portugal III.4 (goPortugal — MIT, UT Austin, Carnegie Mellon partnerships) [Portugal ANIA p.18] is in the same family but doesn't have the named-individuals visiting-fellow framing.

## What Portugal does that UAE's 2018 strategy does NOT

1. **EU AI Act alignment** (Portugal IV.4) and **regulatory sandboxes** (IV.5) and **EU AI Act implementation guide** (IV.6) [Portugal ANIA p.20-21].
2. **Per-initiative start dates and responsible entities** (Portugal's 32 initiative cards [p.11-21]). UAE's 2031 horizon has no sequencing.
3. **Explicit Innovation/Application/Hybrid positioning debate** [Portugal ANIA p.7]. UAE just says "world leader."
4. **Strategic bets explicitly named** (Cybersecurity, LLMs, AI+Robotics for Industry, Blue Economy, Responsible AI) [Portugal ANIA p.7]. UAE has 5 priority sectors but no equivalent "what we will not do" list.
5. **A national AI compute strategy** (I.1 Advanced Computing/EuroHPC + I.2 Gigafactory) [Portugal ANIA p.11]. UAE strategy is silent on national compute capacity.
6. **AI literacy campaign for citizens** (III.8) [Portugal ANIA p.18]. UAE has Public AI Training but it's awareness-raising, not a sustained literacy programme.
7. **Recent vintage** — Portugal Jan 2026; UAE strategy Oct 2018.

## Critique-lens conclusion

UAE's 2018 strategy is **ambition-heavy and brand-led**, much less operationally specific than Portugal's ANIA. But three structural moves remain directly importable into ANIA, even after factoring in the 8-year age gap and the post-GenAI evolution:

1. **Appoint a single ministerial AI owner.** Portugal urgently needs a Portuguese-flag equivalent of UAE's Minister of State for AI + AI Office. ANIA currently disperses accountability across 15+ entities [p.11-21]. Even without ministerial-level appointment, a named **national AI coordinator** with a permanent secretariat (similar to Finland's roundtable proposal, plus UAE's permanence) would tighten delivery.

2. **Create a Portuguese-flagged AI quality certification scheme.** UAI's 4-Level Seal of Approval [p.20-21] is a clean template. Portugal already has **NP 4595** (referenced in IV.2 [Portugal ANIA p.20]) and the planned EU AI Act conformity assessment regime — combining these into a publicly-recognisable national AI quality mark would (a) help Portuguese AI exports, (b) create a domestic compliance ladder for SMEs, (c) be EU-AI-Act-compatible.

3. **Publish per-sector AI GDP-impact tables.** Portugal's €18-22B/yr aggregate [Portugal ANIA p.3] is good but UAE's per-industry breakdown [p.12 chart] is sharper — it lets each sectoral minister/regulator see their own number, which makes the case for sectoral AI Centres (II.5) [Portugal ANIA p.14] political-economically much stronger.

Secondary moves: a Portuguese "Key Thinkers Program" (named, visiting-fellow framing instead of generic partnerships); explicit numeric talent targets (e.g., "upskill 2000 STEM graduates/year in AI", "train 100% of senior public-administration leadership"); a bilingual (PT/EN) "AI Library" open-access portal aggregating Portuguese AI research output.

What Portugal should **not** copy:
- The unbounded 2031 horizon with no sequencing.
- The reliance on **attracting foreign research talent** as primary strategy — Portugal has its own established research base (FCT-funded, with 9.6 researchers per thousand workers [Portugal ANIA p.4]) and should invest in retention more than the UAE model.
- The 2018 governance-light approach (committee reviews instead of sandboxes/EU AI Act alignment) — Portugal's IV pillar is correctly anchored.
- The IPCC-for-AI framing — by 2026, the UN Secretary-General's High-Level Advisory Body on AI and the Bletchley AI Safety Summit network have largely occupied this space; Portugal can join existing fora rather than propose new ones.
