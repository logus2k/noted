# Japan — Artificial Intelligence Basic Plan: "Japan Rebooted" through "Trustworthy AI" (December 2025)

**Source:** [japan_ai_basic_plan.pdf](../../domains/national_ai_agendas/sources/japan_ai_basic_plan.pdf), 17 pages (provisional English translation). **Cabinet Decision: 23 December 2025**. The plan is established under **Article 18, paragraph (1) of the Act on Promotion of Research and Development, and Utilization of Artificial Intelligence-related Technology (Act No. 53 of 2025)** — known as Japan's **AI Act**.

**Subtitle:** "Japan Rebooted" through "Trustworthy AI"

**Vision target:** "Become the most AI-friendly country in the world"

## Document character

**Japan's AI Basic Plan is the only national AI strategy in the entire comparator set with a direct legislative anchor.** Article 18(1) of the AI Act (Act No. 53 of 2025) mandates the Plan's existence; the Plan in turn operationalises the Act. This dual legal-strategic anchoring is structurally distinct from all 12 other comparators — even the EU AI Act in Portugal/Spain/Italy is a regulatory framework rather than a national strategy.

The plan is explicit about Japan's **acknowledged AI lag**:

> "In Japan AI is not yet actively used in daily life and work. Investments and development relating to AI also lag behind not only major economies but even some countries with smaller economic scale, with this lag becoming increasingly pronounced each year." [p.2]

This is the **most candid acknowledgment of national AI weakness in the comparator set**. Combined with Japan's well-known demographic challenges (acute labor shortages, population decline, stagnant wages), it frames AI not just as opportunity but as existential national-policy necessity.

## Vision: "Japan Rebooted" + "Trustworthy AI"

**Strategic positioning** [p.2-4]:

> "Now is the time to proactively advance AI utilization and research & development, and build and execute a national strategy for AI, starting with the promotion of 'AI innovation' — launching transformations in the economic and social structure as well as creating additional value."

**"Trustworthy AI"** as Japan's competitive edge — leveraging high-quality data in industry, healthcare, and research alongside world-class communications infrastructure.

**A "strategic offensive"** rather than catching up — Japan acknowledges falling behind in investment scale but bets on differentiation through trustworthiness.

### Chapter 4: Implementation Structure

- **Artificial Intelligence Strategic Headquarters** — headed by the Prime Minister, comprising **all Cabinet members**
- **Council for the Promotion of Artificial Intelligence Strategy** — relevant ministries and agencies
- **Expert Panel on AI Strategy** — independent expert input
- **Annual revision in principle** — recognising extremely rapid technological progress
- Coordination with **Basic Plan for Science, Technology and Innovation** (Act No. 130 of 1995) + **Priority Plan for the Formation of a Digital Society** (Act No. 35 of 2021)

## Distinctive Japanese institutional mechanisms

### 1. AI Act (Act No. 53 of 2025) [Throughout]
**Japan is the only country in the comparator set with a dedicated national AI law.** Article 3 (basic principles), Article 13 (guidelines for AI R&D and utilization), Article 16 (response to AI misuse), and Article 18(1) (the AI Basic Plan itself) are all explicitly invoked. Even China's State Council Opinion is non-legislative; the EU AI Act is regulation rather than national strategy. Japan combines both.

### 2. AI Safety Institute (AISI) Japan [p.10]
Modeled on UK AISI. Explicit benchmark: ~200 personnel + initial £100M budget (UK AI Security Institute as of September 2025). **Japan immediately doubles AISI staff** as priority action under §3(1)(v). Strongest single resource commitment in the plan.

### 3. Hiroshima AI Process [§3(2)]
Japan-spearheaded international AI governance framework (initiated under Japan's G7 presidency 2023). Continues to be Japan's premier international AI policy vehicle, with the **Hiroshima AI Process Friends Group** as its diplomatic instrument.

### 4. GPAI Tokyo Expert Support Center [§3(2)(v)]
Japan-hosted facility within the Global Partnership on AI. Project-based support for AI governance and social implementation worldwide.

### 5. AI Transformation (AIX) [§4(1)]
Explicitly framed as parallel to Digital Transformation (DX): "Leveraging AI to transform products, services, and business models based on customer and societal needs, while simultaneously transforming operations themselves, organizations, processes, and corporate culture and climate to establish competitive advantage."

### 6. "Advanced Essential Workers" [§4(3)(v)]
**Specific Japanese concept:** "essential workers who utilize digital technologies, including AI, to earn higher wages than they do now." Specific reskilling support according to occupation and job content. No other comparator names this category.

### 7. "Watt-Bit Collaboration" + All-Photonics Network + Beyond 5G [§2(4)(i)]
Distinctively Japanese infrastructure framing — coupled development of efficient power + communications + next-generation networks. Watt-Bit Collaboration is a Japanese policy neologism.

### 8. Next-generation flagship supercomputer (Fugaku successor) [§2(4)(iii)]
Japan's compute strategy explicitly commits to a Fugaku-successor flagship. Fugaku has been the world's #1 supercomputer at points; the successor is positioned as continued infrastructure leadership.

### 9. Government AI / Government AI platform [§1(1)(i)]
Specific named national platform for AI in government. Specific implementation by Digital Agency.

### 10. Personal Information Protection Commission amending personal information law [§1(4)(iv)]
The Personal Information Protection Commission will examine **regulations on obtaining data subjects' consent** for AI development, aiming for early submission to the Diet of a bill to amend the Act on the Protection of Personal Information (Act No. 57 of 2003). Direct legal modernization.

### 11. STATION Ai and AI Hokkaido Conference [§1, intro]
Specific regional initiatives cited as examples — STATION Ai in Nagoya is Japan's largest startup support facility for AI; AI Hokkaido Conference is a regional verification/implementation forum.

### 12. Open-source AND open-weight AI models [§2(3)(ii)]
"Positioning Japan as a hub for the development of trustworthy AI, foster trustworthy AI ecosystem encompassing **open-source and open-weight AI models**." Most explicit open-source commitment in the comparator set after UK + France.

### 13. PDCA cycle [Three Principles, Principle 2]
Japanese management framework (Plan-Do-Check-Act) explicitly institutionalised as one of the three foundational principles. No other comparator names PDCA as a strategic principle.

### 14. "Digital deficits" framing [Multiple references]
Japan explicitly addresses its trade deficit in digital services as a strategic concern. Closing the digital deficit motivates open-source strategy + AI export emphasis.

### 15. Green Transformation (GX) link [§2 intro]
"Realize a new 'Technology-Driven Nation' and contribute to Green Transformation (GX) across society." Energy-efficient foundation models as Japanese differentiator.

### 16. Cabinet-level AI Strategic Headquarters [Ch. 4 §1]
Headed by Prime Minister with **all Cabinet members** as members. Comparable institutional weight to China's State Council-level coordination.

### 17. Annual revision [Ch. 4 §2]
"In principle annually" — most aggressive revision cadence in the comparator set. Acknowledges AI's rapid technological progress.

## Notable Japanese contextual framing

**Demographic + economic context** explicitly drives the strategy [p.2]:
- Population decline
- Insufficient domestic investment
- Stagnant wages
- Acute labor shortages across regions

**This is the most direct demographic-driven justification for AI in the comparator set** — closer to Italy's framing than to Finland's "twin transition" or China's "new productive forces."

**Hiroshima AI Process** as Japan's signature international diplomatic vehicle — frames Japan as global AI-governance leader despite acknowledged technology lag.

**Trustworthy AI** as competitive differentiator rather than mere ethical requirement — Japan bets that Japanese society's "trustworthy" reputation (long cultivated) can be a market advantage in AI even if Japan cannot match US/China frontier capability.

## What Japan does that Portugal's ANIA does NOT

1. **National AI Act (Act No. 53 of 2025)** as legislative anchor for the Plan. **The single most distinctive Japanese feature.** Portugal could pass a Portuguese AI Law referencing EU AI Act + national specifics (data governance, public-administration AI use, sovereign LLM commitments).

2. **AI Safety Institute (AISI) Japan with explicit UK-AISI benchmark** [§3(1)(v)]. Portugal IV.3 (Centre for Responsible AI) is comparable in spirit but much smaller. Portugal should upgrade IV.3 to AISI-equivalent scale (~50-100 personnel benchmark, proportional to Portugal's population).

3. **PM-led AI Strategic Headquarters with all Cabinet members** [Ch. 4]. Portugal has no equivalent PM-level coordination body. Closest analogue would be CTIC (Conselho Tecnológico para a Inovação e Competitividade) but it's not AI-specific.

4. **Annual revision cadence** [Ch. 4 §2]. Portugal's ANIA is 2026-2030 without annual review. A Portuguese annual ANIA review aligned with EU AI Act enforcement schedule would close this gap.

5. **PDCA cycle as a foundational principle** [Three Principles]. Portugal's monitoring approach is unspecified.

6. **AI Transformation (AIX) explicitly named** as DX-parallel concept [§4(1)]. Portugal has Digital Transformation funding via PRR but no AI-specific organisational-transformation framing.

7. **"Advanced Essential Workers" concept** [§4(3)(v)]. Portugal's III.2 (Smart Skills Framework) doesn't name this category. Portuguese specific framing could connect to "trabalhadores essenciais" definitions used in COVID-era policy.

8. **Hiroshima AI Process leadership** [§3(2)(i)]. Portugal can join but cannot lead an equivalent international forum. CPLP + EU-LAC are Portugal's natural analogues at smaller scale.

9. **GPAI Tokyo Expert Support Center analogue** — Portugal could host a CPLP AI Support Center for capacity building across Lusophone countries.

10. **Open-source + open-weight AI models explicit commitment** [§2(3)(ii)]. Portugal's AMALIA distribution is not explicitly framed as open-source/open-weight strategic commitment.

11. **Personal Information Protection Commission amending personal information law for AI** [§1(4)(iv)]. Portugal could position CNPD (Comissão Nacional de Proteção de Dados) reform analogously, addressing GDPR + EU AI Act intersections.

12. **Data linkage platform for semi-public sectors** [§2(1)(i)] — explicit named infrastructure. Portugal I.3 (sectoral data spaces) is comparable but lacks the linkage-platform framing.

13. **Watt-Bit Collaboration** explicit infrastructure framing — coupling energy + communications + AI infrastructure. Portugal's renewable-energy advantage could be reframed similarly.

14. **Drug discovery AI** as named MHLW initiative [§2(2)(iii)]. Portugal has health AI in II.5 (Sectoral AI Centres) but no specific pharma+AI initiative.

15. **Liberal arts education for AI era** [§4(4)(ii)]. Distinctively Japanese — humanities + STEM integration. Portugal could connect to existing humanities-strong universities (Coimbra, Lisbon, Porto).

16. **Ministerial accountability with ◎ symbol** — every initiative has a named lead ministry plus supporting ministries. Portugal's ANIA lists "Relevant Entities" but with less granularity. ◎ symbol convention is operationally clearer.

17. **Government AI platform** [§1(1)(i)] — named national platform. Portugal lacks equivalent.

18. **Acknowledgment of national AI lag** [p.2] as opening framing. Portugal cites comparative metrics (75% EU average productivity) but the framing is less direct. **Honest framing of weakness as strategic motivation** is a useful rhetorical move.

## What Portugal does that Japan's plan does NOT

1. **Per-initiative timeline with semester dates** — Portugal's 32 cards with 2S 2025 → 1S 2027 sequencing. Japan's 4 Sections have open-ended horizons within annual revision.

2. **Aggregate quantified GDP target** (€18-22B/yr GDP add, +2.7pp productivity) [Portugal ANIA p.3]. Japan lacks comparable macroeconomic target.

3. **EU AI Act direct anchor** — Portugal IV.4 + IV.6 explicitly anchor to EU AI Act + GDPR. Japan participates in international frameworks (Hiroshima AI Process, GPAI, AISI network) but isn't directly bound by EU AI Act.

4. **AMALIA sovereign LLM as named centerpiece initiative** [Portugal ANIA II.7]. Japan has trustworthy AI foundation models in development [§2(3)] but lacks a comparable single-named flagship project (closest is the Fugaku-successor supercomputer).

5. **Six guiding principles** [Portugal ANIA p.6] — Portugal's clean conceptual scaffolding. Japan has Three Principles + Four Basic Policies; structurally clear but less aphoristic.

6. **Strategic bets explicitly named** (Cybersecurity, LLMs, AI+Robotics, Blue Economy, Responsible AI) [Portugal ANIA p.7]. Japan has Physical AI + AI for Science as bets but doesn't enumerate at Portugal's level.

7. **AI Fast Track visa** instrument [Portugal III.5]. Japan has talent attraction language but not a specific named visa instrument.

8. **Pillar IV — Responsibility and Ethics** as discrete Pillar [Portugal ANIA p.20-21]. Japan has Section 3 (Leading AI Governance) which is structurally similar but anchored to the AI Act + AISI rather than EU AI Act.

## Critique-lens conclusion

Japan's AI Basic Plan is **the most legally-anchored AI strategy in the entire comparator set** — uniquely so. It is also the most candid about national AI weakness, the most demographic-driven in motivation, and the most explicitly oriented toward **trustworthiness-as-competitive-edge** as opposed to frontier-AI capability.

Portugal and Japan share remarkable structural parallels:
- Both face acute demographic challenges driving AI as a labor-substitute imperative
- Both have strong manufacturing/industrial traditions
- Both have **trustworthiness** + **responsible AI** as natural national positioning
- Both acknowledge AI investment lag relative to US/China
- Both rely on small-state diplomatic positioning (Japan: Hiroshima AI Process; Portugal: CPLP/EU-LAC)
- Both balance domestic AI development with international cooperation

### Top moves Portugal should import

1. **Pass a Portuguese AI Law** (Lei da Inteligência Artificial Portuguesa) anchoring the ANIA's existence in legislation. Japan's Act No. 53 of 2025 is the cleanest template in the set — combines (a) basic principles, (b) R&D and utilization promotion, (c) governance, (d) the Basic Plan itself. Portugal could integrate EU AI Act transposition + national specifics (data governance, public administration AI, AMALIA, CPLP cooperation) into a single national AI law. **This is the single most distinctive Japanese move worth importing.**

2. **Upgrade the Centre for Responsible AI (IV.3) to a Portuguese AI Safety Institute (PT-AISI)** with explicit benchmark to UK AISI / Japan AISI scale. For Portugal, a 30-50-personnel benchmark would be proportionally appropriate. Anchor to ANIA IV.6 (EU AI Act implementation guide).

3. **Establish a PM-led AI Strategic Headquarters** with full Cabinet membership, **with annual revision cadence**. Japan's Cabinet-level coordination is the structural answer to Portugal's scattered-15-entities problem. Mirror in Italy's "Foundation for AI under PM Office" but at policy-coordination level rather than implementation level.

4. **Adopt the PDCA cycle methodology** explicitly in ANIA monitoring. Annual revision of the 32 initiatives with KPI tracking, modeled on Japan's PDCA principle.

5. **Adopt the "AI Transformation (AIX)" framing** parallel to Digital Transformation (DX). Portugal has PRR-funded DX programs but no AI-specific organisational-transformation framing. AIX as a named concept would clarify strategic intent for enterprises.

6. **Adopt the "Advanced Essential Workers" concept** for Portuguese AI-augmented essential-worker reskilling. Portugal has COVID-era "trabalhadores essenciais" definitions — extending these to AI augmentation framing would be politically resonant.

7. **Position CPLP as Portugal's Hiroshima AI Process analogue** — Japan-spearheaded Hiroshima AI Process Friends Group provides the model for a Portuguese-spearheaded "CPLP AI Cooperation Framework" with 9 member states + Lusophone Latin America (Brazil) + Lusophone Africa (Angola, Mozambique, Cabo Verde, etc.).

8. **Establish a CPLP AI Expert Support Center** in Lisbon, modeled on the GPAI Tokyo Expert Support Center. Project-based support for AI governance and social implementation across CPLP.

9. **CNPD reform analogous to Personal Information Protection Commission move** — Portugal's CNPD (Comissão Nacional de Proteção de Dados) could examine GDPR/EU AI Act intersections for AI development consent regimes. Aim for legislative proposal to the Assembly.

10. **Explicit open-source + open-weight AI commitment** in ANIA II.7 (AMALIA). Japan §2(3)(ii) framing — "positioning Japan as a hub for the development of trustworthy AI, foster trustworthy AI ecosystem encompassing open-source and open-weight AI models" — is directly portable.

11. **Watt-Bit Collaboration analogue** — Portugal's renewable-energy advantage [ANIA p.4] coupled with submarine cable infrastructure and data centre planning (I.4). Reframe as "Watt-Bit-Atlantic" or similar to capture renewable-power + Atlantic-connectivity coupling.

12. **"Most AI-friendly country" framing applied to Portugal** — Portugal cannot claim global frontier-AI leadership but can claim **"most EU-AI-Act-compliant economy"** or **"most trustworthy CPLP+EU AI hub"** as differentiation. Japan's framing pattern is transferable.

13. **Acknowledgment of weakness** as opening rhetorical move — Portugal's ANIA cites 75% EU average productivity [p.3] but with less direct framing. Honest "Portugal is behind" + "this is why AI matters now" framing strengthens political mandate.

14. **Drug discovery AI** as named MHLW-equivalent initiative — Portugal has pharma sector + INFARMED. Adding a "AI for Drug Discovery" line under II.5 Sectoral AI Centres would be a high-leverage move.

15. **Multi-ministerial accountability with ◎ symbol** — Portugal's "Relevant Entities" could be replaced with **lead ministry (●) + supporting ministries (○)** convention. Operationally clearer than current format.

### What Portugal should NOT copy

- **Japan's AI lag framing at full intensity** — Japan candidly admits being behind even smaller economies. Portugal's positioning vis-à-vis Spain + Italy + Finland is more competitive; full Japan-style "lag" framing would be politically suboptimal.
- **Annual-revision tempo for all 32 initiatives** — Japan's pace is appropriate for the rapid-evolution Japanese context. Portugal's 2026-2030 with mid-cycle 2027/28 review is more realistic.
- **Fugaku-successor scale supercomputer** — beyond Portugal's compute scale; MareNostrum 5 access + Gigafactory ambition (I.2) is the correct Portuguese scale.

### The 14-country comparator set is now substantively complete

With Japan added, Portugal's ANIA can be triangulated against:

**EU peers** (Spain, France, Italy, Finland, Sweden) — closest regulatory + economic peers, all EU AI Act anchored.

**Anglosphere** (UK, USA) — strategic vision + operational playbook stack reference.

**East Asia** (China, Singapore, Japan) — three distinct East Asian models: China (state-led activist alternative), Singapore (small-state pragmatic), **Japan (legally-anchored "Trustworthy AI" with PM coordination)**.

**Gulf states** (UAE, Qatar) — branding-heavy ambition.

**Latin America** (Argentina) — 2018 cautionary baseline.

The 14-country comparator set covers the full design space. The most directly relevant peers for Portugal: **Spain (Iberian neighbour, same EU framework) + Italy (Mediterranean industrial twin) + Japan (demographic + trustworthiness twin) + Singapore (small-state ambitious twin)**. These four are the prioritized sources for ANIA's evolution.
