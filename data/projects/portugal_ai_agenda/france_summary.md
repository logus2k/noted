# France — AI: Our Ambition for France (March 2024)

**Source:** [france_ai_our_ambition_for_france.pdf](../../domains/national_ai_agendas/sources/france_ai_our_ambition_for_france.pdf), 127 pages (text extracted with `pdftotext`). **French Artificial Intelligence Commission**, set up September 2023 by the French Government. Co-chaired by **Philippe Aghion** (College de France economist, Clark Medal 2001) and **Anne Bouverot** (tech executive). Report submitted March 2024 — fed directly into the **Paris AI Action Summit (February 2025)**.

## Document type — a Commission report, not a Government strategy

This is **not the French Government's official AI strategy** but the **report of an independent expert commission** assembled to advise the Government. Distinct in this set from Portugal ANIA / Spain Estrategia / Sweden Strategy. The Commission's recommendations are **proposals to government** — many were subsequently adopted (Paris AI Action Summit Feb 2025, France 2030 AI envelope, IA Cluster strategy), but the document itself is advisory.

**Why it matters for Portugal:** Because of its rigorous methodology (15 members + 15 rapporteurs, 600 expert hearings, 7,000 citizen contributors via Agora app, 23 plenary sessions [Method p.13-15]), this is the **single most economically-grounded national AI document** in the comparator set. It explicitly references **Cédric Villani's 2018 report** as predecessor [p.9], so this is the second major commission cycle in 6 years.

## Vision and six main lines of action [Executive Summary p.4-12]

The Commission proposes a **collective, massive, immediate and long-term mobilization** representing **€5 billion annual public investment over five years = €27 billion total** = 0.3% of total public spending [p.4-5, p.122].

The six main lines:
1. **Launch a plan to raise awareness and train the nation** — public debates, "AI cafés", structured higher education offer, lifelong learning, AI as object and tool of social dialogue
2. **Redirect savings to innovation** — create short-term **€10 billion "France & AI" fund** combining corporate private equity + public support
3. **Make France a major hub for computing power** — secure European supply, solicit private compute installation with public usage guarantees + simplified procedures, set up **AI tax credit** for model training
4. **Facilitate data access** — modernize CNIL mandate + revise board composition + abolish certain prior authorisation procedures for health data + reduce response times; build technical infrastructure for cultural-data AI training while respecting IP
5. **Adopt an "AI exception" in public research** — free researchers from administrative constraints, upgrade salaries, **double public AI research resources**
6. **Promote global governance of AI** — create a **World AI Organization (WAIO)**, an **International Fund for Public Interest AI** (€500M/yr), and a **"1% AI" solidarity mechanism** for developing countries

## Macroeconomic case [Executive Summary p.8-9, throughout Chapter 1]

- **AI deployment could double France's annual economic growth**
- After 10 years, **GDP increase between €250 and €420 billion** — "like adding a second industrial sector"
- Productivity gains are temporary but may be **complemented by a permanent acceleration of innovation rate** (AI-accelerated R&D in proteins, materials, etc.)
- France invests **~20× less than the US** in AI; **3-4× less per comparable wealth**
- US digital services sector **2.5× larger** than EU+UK combined; same ratio for AI-specialised companies funded 2013-2022
- "It's not too late to (re)act" — Europe is positioned across entire AI value chain, French higher ed is training world-class engineers

## Top distinctive mechanisms

### 1. World AI Organization (WAIO) [Recommendation 22, p.111-115]

The Commission's most ambitious diplomatic proposal. Treaty-based international body with:
- **Equal-share governance**: state representatives + four colleges of individual representatives (research, public-interest structures, companies, territories)
- **Treaty-based binding standards** for AI systems and audit processes
- **IPCC-like** function for state-of-AI knowledge and impact assessment
- **Strategic orientation** for AI projects of global general interest
- Separate **specialised auditing body** with high autonomy (Anglo-Saxon "trust" model)
- Distinct from a separate **International Fund for Public Interest AI** (€500M/yr) — to avoid conflicts of interest
- **"1% AI" solidarity mechanism** — major compute investors commit 1% to developing countries

France's bid to host the WAIO was tied to the **Paris AI Action Summit (Feb 2025)** — the natural French diplomatic moment.

### 2. "AI Exception" in Public Research [throughout Sovereignty section]

Radical regulatory simplification specifically for public AI research:
- **Zero hindrance for researchers** as explicit goal
- Commitment on response times to administrative requests
- Indicator of administrative simplicity
- **Upgrade salaries** of researchers and teacher-researchers
- Facilitate part-time work with companies / socio-economic players
- **Doubling public AI research resources**

### 3. France & AI Fund — €10 billion [Recommendation 12, Executive Summary p.10]

**Corporate private equity + public support** to:
- Bring about emergence of AI ecosystem
- Accelerate transformation of French economic fabric
- Pool activity data to drive digital projects (data + funding tandem)
- **"Unprecedented scale of resources" in France** [p.10]

### 4. AI Tax Credit for Model Training [Executive Summary p.5; Recommendation 14]

Tax instrument supporting R&D projects involving leasing of computing power, **subject to use of a computing center established in France/Europe**. Industrial-policy mechanism complementing existing French R&D tax credits.

### 5. CNIL Modernization with Abolished Prior Authorizations [Recommendation 15]

Most aggressive data-access reform in the comparator set:
- **Modernize CNIL mandate** to include innovation focus
- **Revise composition of board**
- **Abolish certain prior authorization procedures for health data**
- **Reduce response times** of CNIL
- Increase resources of CNIL

### 6. Cultural Heritage AI Training Infrastructure [Recommendation 16]

Technical infrastructure bringing AI developers + cultural-heritage data holders. Implements EU AI Act's transparency obligations for training data while respecting **opt-out clause** of literary and artistic property rights.

### 7. AI Cafés + Citizen Engagement [Recommendation 1]

Operational citizen-engagement infrastructure:
- Ongoing public debates "in everyday places"
- "AI cafés" — places for experimentation and public engagement
- Digital information tool for citizens
- **Competition for positive use cases of AI**

### 8. Decentralized Regulatory Implementation [Box on p.117-118]

**Anti-centralization governance posture** — explicit rejection of a single "French AI Safety Office":
- Designate market surveillance authorities **as close as possible to sector markets**
- Support skill development of sector-specific bodies
- Pool resources for evaluation capabilities
- **"If AI is indeed a general-purpose technology, all sector authorities need to address it"**

Distinct from UK's centralised AI Safety Institute approach or Spain's AESIA centralisation.

### 9. Hybrid Higher Education Courses [Recommendation 6]

- **"AI + biology", "law + AI"** type cross-disciplinary degrees
- **AI chairs in design schools**
- AI awareness course for **ALL civil servants**
- Generalise AI deployment in **all** higher education courses
- Acculturate **secondary education** to make specialised courses accessible

### 10. Semiconductor Industrial Policy as #1 Spend [Recommendation 13, €7.7B]

European semiconductor component industry adapted to AI systems. Single largest line item in the entire plan. Leverages existing French SOITEC + STMicroelectronics + Dutch ASML.

### 11. Healthcare Data Circulation as #3 Spend [Recommendation 10, €3.0B]

Most substantial sectoral AI commitment in the report. Goes hand-in-hand with the CNIL prior-authorization-abolition reform (R15) — explicitly framed as "therapeutic innovations" enabler.

### 12. Open-Source AI Ecosystem International Strategy [Recommendation 4]

International strategy supporting open AI systems development, third-party inspection capabilities, and benevolent uses as countermeasures to malicious uses. **Political stance**: France advocates open-source as a European competitive advantage.

### 13. Dynamic Competition Policy [Recommendation 25, p.120-121]

Move EU competition policy doctrine from **static** ("what market share does this company hold today?") to **dynamic** ("what market shares could this company hold tomorrow?"). Mobilise Digital Markets Act + supplement for AI specifics.

## What France's Commission report does that Portugal's ANIA does NOT

1. **€27B 5-year envelope with €M per recommendation** — most operationally-anchored in the comparator set. Portugal: no aggregate budget published.
2. **Independent Commission methodology** with academic rigour. Portugal: internal government drafting.
3. **Macroeconomic case backed by published economics** (Aghion is a top growth theorist). "Doubled growth" + €250-420B is sharper than Portugal's €18-22B/yr.
4. **AI Exception in public research** — radical regulatory simplification for researchers. Portugal: no equivalent.
5. **AI tax credit for model training** — fiscal instrument complementing R&D credits. Portugal: SIFIDE exists but no AI-specific version.
6. **€10B France & AI fund** combining private equity + public support + data pooling. Portugal: scattered instruments (BfP, IFIC, PRR Components).
7. **CNIL modernization** with abolished prior authorizations for health data. Portugal: CNPD reform not addressed in ANIA.
8. **World AI Organization** proposal as a coherent diplomatic initiative. Portugal IV.7 (technology diplomacy) is one sentence.
9. **Decentralized regulatory implementation** — explicit rejection of single AI agency. Portugal IV.4 still defining its single authority.
10. **AI cafés + citizen engagement infrastructure** + competition for positive use cases. Portugal III.8 (AI literacy campaign) is comparable but less operational.
11. **Cultural heritage data trust infrastructure** with opt-out implementation. Portugal: no equivalent.
12. **AI chairs in design schools** + hybrid degrees (AI+biology, law+AI). Portugal III.2 (Smart Skills Framework) is a mapping, not a curriculum specification.
13. **Semiconductor industrial policy** as #1 spend (€7.7B). Portugal I.2 Gigafactory is computing infrastructure not chip fab.
14. **"AI awareness course for ALL civil servants"** — mass mandatory training. Portugal III.1 Doutor AP is more selective.
15. **Healthcare AI €3B over 5 years** as sectoral commitment. Portugal: Health Sectoral AI Centre under II.5 is unsized.
16. **Open-source ecosystem international strategy** as political stance.
17. **Doubling public AI research resources** as explicit target. Portugal II.1 (fundamental research) has no quantified target.
18. **Reference to Cédric Villani 2018 report** as predecessor — explicit strategic continuity over 6 years. Portugal: 2026 is the first AI agenda.
19. **Dynamic competition policy doctrine** — proactive anti-monopoly stance. Portugal: no equivalent.

## What Portugal does that France's report does NOT do

1. **Per-initiative start dates** (Portugal: 2S 2025 → 1S 2027 per initiative card; France: 5-year envelope without sequencing).
2. **32 initiative cards with named entities per card** (Portugal); France has 25 recommendations with Lead Ministry but no equivalent named co-implementing entities.
3. **Strategic bets explicitly named** (Cybersecurity, LLMs, AI+Robotics for Industry, Blue Economy, Responsible AI) [Portugal ANIA p.7]. France lists priorities by Ministry but no equivalent prioritisation among technology domains.
4. **AI Fast Track visa instrument** (Portugal III.5). France's R18+R19 talk about attracting talent but don't define a specific visa instrument.
5. **PME.IA low-code/no-code platform** for SMEs (Portugal II.10). France's R20 is much broader.
6. **AI Generation career campaign for youth** (Portugal III.7). France has AI cafés for citizens but no youth-career-specific campaign.
7. **EU AI Act sandbox** as a named initiative (Portugal IV.5). France's R24 (evaluation ecosystem) covers this but with less specific instrument naming.
8. **AI as Action #20 of Portugal's Digital Strategy 2026-27** — anchored in a broader digital plan. France's report is standalone.

## Style stance comparison

France's Commission is **internationally outward-facing**: World AI Organization, "1% AI" for developing countries, EU-LAC-style outreach via open-source ecosystem strategy. Portugal's ANIA is **EU-aligned but inward-facing** on operational delivery.

France emphasises **economic-growth doubling** + sovereignty as headline framing. Portugal emphasises **EU competitiveness** + ethics + responsible innovation as headline framing.

Both reject the US/China-dominant model. Both privilege EU regulatory anchoring. France goes further in **regulatory simplification within EU framework** (AI Exception in research, abolish prior CNIL authorizations).

## Critique-lens conclusion

France's Commission report is the **most rigorously economically-grounded comparator** and the **richest source of specific policy mechanisms** in the entire set. Portugal can directly import 8-10 specific moves:

### Top moves Portugal should import

1. **Publish aggregate ANIA budget envelope with per-initiative €M.** France: €27B over 5 years, with per-recommendation €M. Portugal: 32 initiatives without budget. Adding even rough per-card €M (drawing from PRR, BfP, IFIC, FEDER, etc.) would close the largest comparator gap. **Spain's Tabla 6 + France's per-recommendation €M = the gold standard combined**.

2. **Commission a "Portuguese AI Commission"** mid-cycle (2027 or 2028) as a midpoint review of ANIA. Mixing experts + civil society + companies (modeled on Aghion-Bouverot composition). 600-hearing + 7,000-citizen-contributor methodology adapted to Portuguese scale (~150 hearings + ~2,000 citizen contributors via a Portuguese Agora).

3. **Adopt an "AI Exception" for public research** — Portuguese variant. Free FCT-funded AI researchers from administrative bottlenecks, commitment on response times to grant decisions, indicator of administrative simplicity, upgrade salaries via a dedicated AI track within the FCT career path, facilitate part-time work with companies. Portugal II.4 (non-academic PhDs) is the closest existing initiative — needs upgrade.

4. **AI Tax Credit for Model Training** within the SIFIDE framework. Specifically support R&D projects involving compute lease, subject to using **Portuguese or European compute centers** (MareNostrum 5 / Deucalion / forthcoming Gigafactory). Adds a fiscal lever to Portugal I.1 (Advanced Computing).

5. **CNPD Modernization** mirror of France's CNIL reform. Modernize the Portuguese data protection authority's mandate to include innovation, revise its composition, **abolish prior authorisation procedures for health data**, reduce response times. Combine with II.13 (PA Procurement Code) and IV.6 (EU AI Act implementation guide).

6. **AI Cafés / Citizen Engagement Infrastructure.** Portugal III.8 (AI literacy campaign) is comparable but Portugal lacks the **AI cafés** physical-venue + **competition for positive use cases** mechanism. Low cost; high public-engagement value.

7. **Decentralized Regulatory Implementation.** Portugal IV.4 still defines a single competent authority for the EU AI Act. France's commission **explicitly rejects** the single-agency model. Portugal can adopt a hybrid: a national coordinator (e.g., Centro para a IA Responsável upgraded) + sector market-surveillance bodies (Banco de Portugal for finance, INFARMED for medical AI, ANACOM for comms, etc.).

8. **AI Chairs in Design Schools + Hybrid Degrees.** Portugal has strong design schools (IADE, ESAD Matosinhos). A Portuguese "AI + Design" + "AI + Biology" + "Law + AI" track would extend Portugal III.2 Smart Skills Framework.

9. **Healthcare AI sized commitment** matching France's €3B / 5yr (Portugal proportional: ~€300-500M). Sectoral AI Centre under II.5 should have a specific Health line item.

10. **Cultural-Heritage Data Trust Infrastructure.** Biblioteca Nacional de Portugal + Arquivo Nacional Torre do Tombo as institutional data providers for AMALIA, modeled on France's R16 — but combine with Sweden's pattern (National Library + Archives) and Spain's ALIA consortium model.

### What Portugal should NOT copy

- **The €27B macro figure** — France-scale. Portugal proportional would be ~€2.5-3B (similar to Spain's already-published €2.6B). Portugal needs to publish ~that scale, not bigger.
- **The World AI Organization** as a Portuguese diplomatic project — France is the natural convener. Portugal can be a strong supporter but not a leader. (Portugal could lead a parallel **CPLP AI Governance** initiative.)
- **The semiconductor industrial policy** as #1 spend — France has SOITEC, STMicroelectronics, ASML neighbors. Portugal's industrial base is different; the I.2 Gigafactory is the right scale.
- **Universal "AI awareness for ALL civil servants"** as a mandate — too heavy-handed for the Portuguese PA. Portugal III.1 Doutor AP's leadership-first approach is more realistic.

### What Portugal can offer France in return (if the two coordinate)

- **AI Fast Track talent visa** as an operational instrument (Portugal III.5 ahead of France on this).
- **AMALIA + ALIA "Ibéricos" + a French equivalent** as a coordinated Romance-language sovereign-LLM cluster (with Italy as natural fourth).
- **CPLP outreach** (Brazil, Lusophone Africa) as complement to France's francophone diplomatic network.
- **Atlantic submarine cable hub** complementing France's Mediterranean + Atlantic positioning.

The most productive Portugal-France move would be a **bilateral Iberian-French AI infrastructure compact** — joint sovereign compute, joint cultural-data infrastructure, coordinated talent visas, common AI evaluation ecosystem under a single decentralized model.
