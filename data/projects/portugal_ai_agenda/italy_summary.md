# Italy — Italian Strategy for Artificial Intelligence 2024-2026

**Source:** [Italian_strategy_for_artificial_intelligence_2024-2026.pdf](../../domains/national_ai_agendas/sources/Italian_strategy_for_artificial_intelligence_2024-2026.pdf), 38 pages (text extracted with `pdftotext`). Published by **AGID — Agenzia per l'Italia Digitale** [cover]. PDF dated June 2024. Anchored in the EU AI Act and "A Europe Fit for the Digital Age" framework.

## Document character

This is the **third major iteration** of Italy's national AI strategy, building on:
- **2018**: AGID analysis of AI impact in society and Public Administration
- **2020**: MISE (Ministry of Economic Development) "Proposals for an Italian Strategy for AI"
- **2021-2024**: "Strategic Program for AI" (MUR + MISE + Minister for Technological Innovation), which has now run its course [Introduction p.4]

The 2024-2026 strategy was triggered by the **generative-AI turning point** (ChatGPT launch + foundation models + new social sensitivity towards AI). EU AI Act + Bletchley Declaration + US Executive Order on AI are explicit external anchors [Risks section p.11].

## Vision and strategic framing

Italy positions itself as a country that must **build AI of its own interest**, not merely import generic solutions:

> "Remaining merely as users of AI would indeed mark, in the long term, not only a strategic dependence on solutions that might not provide adequate guarantees, but would even lead to a homogenization and cultural stereotyping given the impact these technologies have on our society." [p.8]

The strategy frames AI as a tool to **enhance Italy's productive excellences and Made-in-Italy distinctive traits** (manufacturing, agri-food, furniture, clothing, tourism, chemical/pharmaceutical, aerospace, automotive). Anthropocentric, reliable, sustainable, and EU-aligned.

## Three macro-objectives [Introduction p.4-5]

1. **Support creation and adoption of AI applications** for management practices, production models, and innovation projects — with **country-specific data and infrastructure**, avoiding dilution by imported systems.
2. **Promote foundational and applied scientific research**, connecting Italian research units with major international platforms.
3. **Create favorable contextual conditions** — talent through training, PA services through AI, cross-cutting priorities.

## Enabling actions [p.15-16]

### A.1 — National Knowledge Assets: Datasets and Models

**Mandatory registry** of AI datasets and models, built on principles of transparency, fairness, ethical reliability, and reusability. All projects funded under the national strategy (or otherwise receiving public funding) **must** report datasets used and models produced. Three dimensions: methodological (national protocol for trustworthy-by-design datasets), operational (MLops + data preparation platform), applicative (verticalised for Italian application areas).

Uses **Privacy-Enhancing Technologies (PET)** — solutions that allow data collection/processing/sharing while protecting confidentiality. Targets cultural-heritage data plus synthetic data generation for SME contexts.

### A.2 — Network Infrastructures for AI

Intelligent network expansion + public-private partnerships for congestion mitigation. Standardisation of network solutions for AI. Edge computing promotion.

### A.3 — Foundation for Artificial Intelligence ⭐

**Single most distinctive Italian institutional move.** A new public Foundation **under the direct control of the Presidency of the Council of Ministers** [p.15]. Mandate:

- Manage the registry of AI solutions (A.1) with a sustainable business model
- Coordinate strategy implementation across all four macro-areas
- Develop an **annual forecasting document** on AI use in Enterprises and PA
- Coordinate evaluation of projects for SME/startup AI funding
- Support regulatory compliance through guidelines and best practices
- **Monitor the entire strategy** with KPIs + flagship projects + Delphi method expert panel
- Manage the strategy's central fund (sustainable, self-financing through revenues)
- Define an **AI development agenda** in close synergy with all ecosystem actors

This is a **prime-minister's-office-level coordinating body** — comparable institutionally to UK's AI Opportunities Adviser + Spain's AESIA + France's proposed World AI Organization. Portugal has no equivalent.

| R.6 | International Collaborations | Joint research with at least one other European or Extra-European nation; 2-year duration; legal-administrative tools for solution protection |

## Monitoring [p.35-36]

The Foundation produces an **annual public report** with:
- **KPIs** per strategic area (effectiveness metrics linked to main variables)
- **Flagship projects** — a small number of high-visibility projects whose progress proxies overall sector progress (cited example: US space programmes as scientific-technology proxy)
- **Delphi method** by anonymous expert panel — multi-round questionnaire with facilitator-mediated convergence

Specific KPI examples:
- **Education**: number of interdisciplinary degree courses with AI; PhD AI enrollments; AI citizenship awareness via CATI/CAWI sample surveys (n=1000 → 3% margin of error)
- **Research**: scientometric indicators (bibliometrics); FAIR + LMM progress
- **PA**: PA.5 + PA.6 impact via citizen feedback platforms
- **Enterprises**: ISTAT survey on AI in SMEs (extension proposed); startup creation rate

## Regulatory Bodies [p.37]

**Italian Supervisory Agency** to be established under the EU AI Act Article 59 (national supervisory + notification function). Must coordinate with:
- ACN (Cybersecurity Agency)
- AGCM (Competition Authority)
- AGCOM (Communications Authority)
- Garante (Data Protection Authority)
- Bank of Italy + Consob (Banking + Securities)
- ECB (EU Central Bank, for banking)

Italy explicitly worries about **"over-regulation complicated by overlapping disciplines and competencies among Authorities"** — a problem the new Agency must help simplify, in synergy with the Foundation (A.3).

## Quantitative context [Context Analysis p.6-7]

- **Italian academic AI dates to early 1970s**
- 160+ AI-related university curricula across 53 universities
- **National PhD in AI** since 2020/21 — 61 universities/research institutions
- Italy 7th globally in AI publications (2022): **3,261 papers**, behind China, India, USA
- **12% of all European AI projects** involve Italian research units
- Led the ELIAS project (European Lighthouses in AI), 1st of 128 submissions in Horizon CL4 2022
- 1.5% of grads in ICT (one of lowest in EU)
- **45.6% basic digital skills** among citizens
- **15% of Italian SMEs initiated an AI pilot project in 2022** (up from 6% in 2021) [Osservatorio AI Politecnico di Milano]
- **~600 AI patents + ~350 AI startups since 2017** — placing Italy at the European tail end
- **Generative AI could increase Italy's GDP by up to 18.2% annually** [Ambrosetti-Microsoft report]
- **78.2% of Italian companies plan to use generative AI** in short-medium term

## Risk framing [p.11-12]

Italy is the **most explicit comparator in this set about risks of the strategy itself**:

| Risk | Italian framing |
|---|---|
| **Non-action** | Italy's historical IT-resistance + slow approaches risk waste of resources + competitive loss |
| **Homogenization** | **Cultural homogenization** from foreign AI systems — reproducing foreign social values in Italian society. Strong case for sovereign Italian LMM (R.3) |
| **National over-regulation** | "Build further normative superstructures" — explicit anti-gold-plating concern within EU AI Act |
| **World of work** | Reskilling/upskilling urgency; new skills replace old ones |
| **Digital divide** | Italy's territorial heterogeneities (regions/cities). System initiatives, not extemporaneous projects |
| **Ineffectiveness** | AI policies as "moving target"; continuous review process; KPIs + monitoring |

## What Italy does that Portugal's ANIA does NOT

1. **Foundation for AI under PM Office** — dedicated coordinating body responsible for implementation + coordination + monitoring + central fund management + annual public reporting [A.3, p.15]. Portugal's ANIA has no equivalent central coordinating institution.

2. **Mandatory national registry of AI datasets and models** [A.1, p.15] — all publicly-funded AI projects must register their datasets and models. Portugal has no equivalent transparency mechanism.

3. **Italian Sovereign LLM (LMM) as a named strategic action** with ethics committee oversight, cited investment benchmarks (Germany €500M, UK £100M), and phased delivery [R.3, p.19]. Portugal's AMALIA (II.7) exists but is framed as "continuation" rather than a strategic centerpiece with explicit funding benchmarks.

4. **AI Facilitators for SMEs rooted in productive supply chains** [I.1, p.26] — Italian Made-in-Italy verticals (manufacturing, agri-food, furniture, clothing, tourism, chemical/pharmaceutical, aerospace) each get a facilitator under Foundation control, in synergy with FAIR/Chips.IT/AI4Industry. Portugal's II.5 (Sectoral AI Centres) is comparable but **less institutionalised** and without the named supply-chain verticals.

5. **AI Grand Challenge with bottom-up theme selection** [R.5, p.20] — researchers propose themes, then competitive selection within selected themes. Portugal's research initiatives (II.1-II.4) are top-down.

6. **Dedicated AI Department within the National School of Administration** [PA.6, p.24] — institutional anchor for PA AI training, with role-differentiated postgraduate courses. Portugal III.1 (Doutor AP) + INA is comparable but **less institutionally anchored**.

7. **Quantified PhD AI commitment** — 40 scholarships/year × 5 doctorates of national interest [F.4, p.32]. Portugal II.4 (non-academic PhDs in AI) is unquantified.

8. **Industry-specific Academies** with FSC funds [F.5, p.33] — training consortia (training bodies + trade associations + medium-large companies) responsible for delivering reskilling/upskilling for their workforce and SME supply chains. Portugal has no equivalent named institutional structure.

9. **Multi-channel AI awareness campaign** [F.6, p.34] — explicit TV/radio/print/billboard/social media/events/public-debate orchestration, centrally coordinated by a technical-scientific committee. Portugal III.8 (AI literacy) is comparable but **less channel-specific**.

10. **"Risk of homogenization" as explicit strategic risk** [p.11] — cultural homogenization from foreign AI as a strategic concern motivating the LMM investment. **This framing aligns directly with Portugal's interest in Portuguese-language sovereignty and the AMALIA / Lusophone AI project.**

11. **High Apprenticeship (Dual Education and Research)** [F.2, p.31] with ESF tax relief for companies hiring under-30s in training. Portugal has talent visa (III.5) but no equivalent youth-employment tax instrument.

12. **Delphi-method monitoring** with anonymous expert panel and multi-round convergence [p.35]. Portugal's monitoring approach is unspecified.

13. **Flagship-project monitoring approach** complementing KPIs [p.35] — high-visibility projects serve as sector progress proxies. Portugal does not name flagship projects.

14. **Pre-existing infrastructure named** — FAIR Foundation, 8 National Competence Centers + 40 EDIHs under Industry 4.0 Plan, ELIAS European Lighthouse project. ANIA references EuroHPC but doesn't position Portuguese predecessors with the same institutional density.

15. **Quantitative GDP framing** — Italy mentions 18.2% potential GDP increase from generative AI (Ambrosetti-Microsoft study). Portugal's €18-22B/yr is similar in framing but the Italian study is *single-year* increase while Portugal's is annual ongoing GDP add. Similar order of magnitude (both ~8-18% of GDP at peak impact).

## What Portugal does that Italy's strategy does NOT

1. **Per-initiative start dates** — Portugal's 32 initiative cards with semester (2S 2025 → 1S 2027). Italy's actions are within a 2024-2026 horizon without per-action sequencing.

2. **Strategic bets explicitly named** (Cybersecurity, LLMs, AI+Robotics for Industry, Blue Economy, Responsible AI) [Portugal ANIA p.7]. Italy lists Made-in-Italy sectors but not strategic technology bets.

3. **Aggregate quantified macroeconomic case** — Portugal €18-22B/yr GDP add + 2.7pp productivity contribution. Italy cites the 18.2% potential figure but doesn't make it a target.

4. **AI Fast Track talent visa instrument** [Portugal III.5]. Italy's R.2 talks about attracting talent broadly but no specific visa instrument.

5. **Hybrid Development/Application positioning** explicitly framed [Portugal ANIA p.7]. Italy implicitly does both but doesn't articulate the choice.

6. **EU AI Act sandbox as named initiative** [Portugal IV.5]. Italy mentions sandboxes in I.5 but in less operational detail.

7. **Five-pillar regulatory simplification approach** — Portugal anchors to EU AI Act with explicit implementation guide (IV.4 + IV.6). Italy's regulatory framing is broader but less operationally specific.

## Critique-lens conclusion

Italy's 2024-2026 strategy is **the closest peer to Portugal's ANIA** alongside Spain — same EU framework, Southern European Mediterranean economy with strong industrial/Made-in tradition, similar SME-adoption gaps, similar language-sovereignty concerns. Italy's 3-year horizon makes the strategy more execution-focused than Portugal's 5-year framing.

### Top moves Portugal should import

1. **Establish a Portuguese Foundation for AI under the Prime Minister's Office.** Italy's A.3 (Foundation for AI) [p.15] is the single most importable institutional move. A Portuguese equivalent — let's call it **Fundação ANIA** — would: (a) own implementation, coordination, and monitoring across the 32 initiatives, (b) manage the national fund, (c) produce annual public reports with KPIs + flagship projects, (d) coordinate with sectoral ministries. This addresses the largest structural weakness of ANIA: accountability is scattered across 15+ entities without a central PMO. Spain has AESIA, France proposes WAIO, Italy has the Foundation — Portugal needs an equivalent.

2. **Mandate a Portuguese Registry of AI Datasets and Models** [I.A.1 equivalent]. All publicly-funded Portuguese AI projects (PRR, FCT, BfP, IFIC) must register datasets and models, with reuse permissions, ethical reliability documentation, and PET (Privacy-Enhancing Technologies) compliance. Anchored under ANIA IV.4 (EU AI Act implementation) or IV.6 (EU AI Act implementation guide).

3. **Upgrade AMALIA to LMM-scale ambition** [R.3 equivalent]. Italy's R.3 cites Germany €500M and UK £100M as investment benchmarks for sovereign foundation models. Portugal's AMALIA is currently framed as "continuation" [Portugal ANIA p.14] — it deserves an explicit numerical commitment, ethics-committee oversight, and a phased delivery plan. Italy's 18.2% GDP potential framing is the right scale of ambition.

4. **Reframe ANIA Sectoral AI Centres (II.5) as Portuguese AI Facilitators** [I.1 equivalent]. Italy's facilitators are rooted in productive supply chains (manufacturing, agri-food, furniture, clothing, tourism, chemical/pharmaceutical, aerospace) under direct Foundation control. Portugal can adapt: Sectoral AI Facilitators for **Made-in-Portugal supply chains** (Blue Economy + Agri-food + Tourism + Pharmaceuticals + Footwear/Textiles + Aerospace via Edisoft) under Fundação ANIA control.

5. **Establish a Portuguese AI Department within INA** [PA.6 equivalent]. Italy's PA.6 (AI Department within Scuola Nazionale dell'Amministrazione) is a clean institutional anchor for PA AI training. Portugal's Doutor AP programme (III.1) is comparable but **needs the institutional permanence** of a dedicated INA AI Department.

6. **Quantify Portuguese PhD AI commitment** [F.4 equivalent]. Italy commits 40 scholarships/year × 5 doctorates of national interest. Portugal's II.4 (non-academic PhDs in AI) is unquantified. A Portuguese commitment of e.g., 30 scholarships/year × 3 national-interest PhD programmes (perhaps via the AIST consortium) sized to Portuguese demographics would close the gap.

7. **Borrow the Italian Risk Framing.** Italy's explicit "Risk of homogenization" + "Risk of national over-regulation" + "Risk of non-action" framing [p.11-12] is the strongest in this comparator set. Portugal should add an explicit risk section to ANIA with Portuguese specificity: risk of cultural-linguistic homogenization (motivating AMALIA), risk of EU AI Act over-implementation (motivating regulatory simplification), risk of non-action (motivating speed).

8. **Adopt Delphi-method + Flagship-project monitoring** [p.35]. Italy's monitoring approach combines quantitative KPIs with bottom-up Delphi-method expert consensus and a small number of high-visibility flagship projects. Portugal's ANIA does not specify a monitoring methodology — Italy's is directly importable.

9. **High Apprenticeship instrument** [F.2 equivalent]. Italy's Dual Education and Research with ESF tax relief for companies hiring under-30s in AI training. Portugal already has fiscal instruments (SIFIDE, IRC reductions) — a specific AI-talent youth-employment tax credit would be a low-cost addition.

10. **Industry-specific Academies** [F.5 equivalent]. Italian consortia of training bodies + trade associations + medium-large companies for reskilling/upskilling delivery. Portugal could activate similar consortia through ACEPI, APED, AHRESP, etc.

### What Portugal should not copy

- **Italy's 3-year horizon** is too short for some strategic initiatives. Portugal's 5-year horizon (2026-2030) gives more room for delivery.
- **Italian regulatory simplification stance** — Italy explicitly worries about national over-regulation. Portugal correctly anchors to EU AI Act with implementation guidance (IV.6) without that political tension.
- **Italian gold-plating concern** — within Italian political context this is a particular framing. Portugal's EU-aligned posture is more cooperative.

### Iberian + Italian Coordination Potential

The most productive structural move would be a **Mediterranean / Southern European AI Coalition** between Portugal + Spain + Italy:
- **Joint sovereign LLM consortium**: Spain ALIA (Castilian + co-officials including Catalan/Basque/Galician/Valencian) + Italian LMM + Portuguese AMALIA → a **Romance-Languages Foundation Model Network** covering ~700M+ Romance-language speakers globally (Spanish ~500M + Portuguese ~260M + Italian ~85M + French co-fed via ALT-EDIC).
- **Joint Mediterranean AI Compute Compact**: MareNostrum 5 (Spain) + Deucalion (Portugal) + Italian compute via FAIR → shared HPC access with bilateral compact for Iberian-Italian researchers and startups.
- **Coordinated EU AI Act implementation guidance** — Spain (AESIA) + Italian Supervisory Agency + Portuguese Centre for Responsible AI as a triangular cooperation network.
- **Coordinated CPLP+LATAM outreach**: Italy's Mediterranean diplomatic network + Portugal's CPLP + Spain's EU-LAC → joint AI development cooperation programme.

This coalition would be substantially more competitive globally than any of the three countries alone.
