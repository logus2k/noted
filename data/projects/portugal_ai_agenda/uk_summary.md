# United Kingdom — AI Opportunities Action Plan (Jan 2025) + AI Playbook for the UK Government (Feb 2025)

This summary covers **two complementary UK documents**, published within four weeks of each other in early 2025:

| Document | Date | Role | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AI Opportunities Action Plan** (CP 1241) | January 2025 | **Strategic vision and 50 recommendations** | [uk_ai_opportunities_action_plan.pdf](../../domains/national_ai_agendas/sources/uk_ai_opportunities_action_plan.pdf), 24 pages |
| **AI Playbook for the UK Government** | February 2025 | **Operational guide for civil servants** implementing AI | [uk_ai_playbook_for_the_uk_government.pdf](../../domains/national_ai_agendas/sources/uk_ai_playbook_for_the_uk_government.pdf), 118 pages |

**Action Plan:** Presented to Parliament by the Secretary of State for Science, Innovation and Technology, the Rt Hon **Peter Kyle MP**, January 2025 [Action Plan p.4]. Authored by **Matt Clifford CBE** (Chair of ARIA, now also AI Opportunities Adviser to the PM). Government accepted **all 50 recommendations** (per the companion Government Response CP 1242). Published under Open Government Licence v3.0.

**Playbook:** Published by the **Government Digital Service (GDS)** within DSIT, February 2025 [Playbook p.7]. Foreword by **Feryal Clark MP** (Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for AI and Digital Government); Preface by **David Knott** (Government CTO). Replaces the January 2024 *Generative AI Framework for HMG*.

**The two are the strategic and operational halves of the UK's 2025 AI agenda**, complementing the earlier **2021 National AI Strategy** (10-year vision) and **2023 White Paper "A pro-innovation approach to AI regulation"** (5 regulator principles).

## Strategic framing — "AI maker, not AI taker" [Action Plan p.21]

> "If this is to benefit the UK we must be an AI maker, not just an AI taker: we need companies at the frontier that will be our UK national champions." [Action Plan p.21]

The Action Plan's three sections are framed as a coherent strategy:

1. **Lay the foundations** to enable AI — infrastructure, data, talent, regulation
2. **Change lives by embracing AI** — public-sector adoption + cross-economy diffusion
3. **Secure our future with homegrown AI** — UK Sovereign AI unit + national champions at the frontier

Core principles guiding the 50 recommendations [Action Plan p.6]:
- **Be on the side of innovators** — every measure must benefit those doing new and ambitious things in the UK
- **Invest in becoming a great customer** — government purchasing power as market-shaping lever
- **Crowd in capital and talent** — UK is medium-sized + tight fiscal; must attract globally
- **Build on UK strengths and catalytic emerging areas** — AI for science, robotics, embodied AI

Headline framing: **AI adoption could grow the UK economy by an additional £400 billion by 2030** [Action Plan p.19, Public First / Google's Impact in UK 2023]. UK is currently 3rd largest AI market in the world after USA and China.

## Existing UK strengths cited [Action Plan p.5]

- Strong fundamental AI research; world-class universities for AI
- Vibrant startup/scaleup scene; sophisticated capital
- Frontier AI companies in London: **Google DeepMind HQ, OpenAI / Anthropic / Microsoft / Meta AI offices, Wayve** (autonomous vehicles)
- Global leadership on AI safety/governance via the **AI Safety Institute (AISI)** — the first such institute globally to conduct pre-deployment evaluations of frontier models
- Pro-innovation, flexible regulatory approach

## Section 1 — Lay the foundations (Recommendations 1-30)

### 1.1 AI Infrastructure (Recs 1-6)

A three-layer compute portfolio: **Sovereign + Domestic + International**:
- **Sovereign AI compute**: publicly-owned/allocated; smallest component; enables independent allocation to national priorities
- **Domestic compute**: privately owned, UK-based; positions UK as leading AI economy
- **International compute**: reciprocal partnerships with like-minded allies (incl. EuroHPC JU)

Key recommendations:
- **Rec 1**: 10-year AI infrastructure plan within 6 months
- **Rec 2**: **Expand AI Research Resource (AIRR) by 20x by 2030** — starting within 6 months
- **Rec 3**: Mission-focused **AIRR programme directors** with DARPA/ARIA-style autonomy
- **Rec 4**: Establish **AI Growth Zones (AIGZs)** — streamlined planning + clean power; explicit candidates include **Culham Science Centre** (UK Atomic Energy Authority HQ), post-industrial towns, coastal Scotland
- **Rec 5**: Mitigate sustainability and security risks of AI infrastructure
- **Rec 6**: International compute partnerships (incl. EuroHPC JU active role)

### 1.2 Data assets (Recs 7-13)

The **National Data Library (NDL)** is the strategic centerpiece:
- **Rec 7**: Identify ≥5 high-impact public data sets for AI researchers
- **Rec 8**: **Strategically shape what data is collected** (building on UK Biobank model for new domains)
- **Rec 9**: Guidelines/best practices for open government data sets
- **Rec 10**: **Couple compute allocation with proprietary data access** as attractive offer
- **Rec 11**: Build public-sector data collection infrastructure
- **Rec 12**: Incentivise researchers + industry to curate private data sets via Research Excellence Framework
- **Rec 13**: Establish **copyright-cleared British media asset training data set** — partnerships with **National Archives, Natural History Museum, British Library, BBC** — licensable internationally

### 1.3 Talent (Recs 14-22)

Target: **train tens of thousands of AI professionals by 2030**:
- **Rec 14**: Accurately assess the skills gap (current estimates outdated since 2019-2020)
- **Rec 15**: Higher Education Institutions to scale AI graduates (currently 46,000 AI-relevant grads in 2022, highest in Europe but behind Finland per capita); co-design with industry (cite Waterloo, Munich CDTM, French CIFRE PhD)
- **Rec 16**: Increase diversity (only 22% of AI/data science workforce are women; achieving parity = thousands more workers)
- **Rec 17**: Expand education pathways (further education, apprenticeships, employer/self-led upskilling)
- **Rec 18**: **Flagship Rhodes/Marshall/Fulbright-scale AI scholarship** — 100 elite scholars from UK + abroad; "exceptional promise" demonstrated incl. via STEM competitions (IMO, IOI)
- **Rec 19**: Lifelong skills programme ready for AI (cite Singapore + South Korea models)
- **Rec 20**: **Internal headhunting capability on par with top AI firms** — bring small number of elite individuals to UK
- **Rec 21**: Extend **High Potential Individual (HPI) visa** to graduates from leading AI institutions (e.g., **Indian Institutes of Technology, Carnegie Mellon since 2020**) currently excluded
- **Rec 22**: Expand **Turing AI Fellowships** — 15 new Pioneer Fellowships + 25 Acceleration/World-Leading Fellowships over 3 years

### 1.4 Regulation, safety, assurance (Recs 23-30)

- **Rec 23**: Continue + grow the **AI Safety Institute (AISI)** — pre-deployment evaluation of frontier models
- **Rec 24**: **Reform UK text and data mining regime** to be at least as competitive as EU — urgent IP resolution
- **Rec 25**: Fund regulators to scale AI capabilities
- **Rec 26**: Sponsor departments to include safe AI innovation focus in strategic guidance
- **Rec 27**: **Regulatory sandboxes in priority sectors** (autonomous vehicles, drones, robotics)
- **Rec 28**: **All regulators publish annually how they have enabled AI innovation** — transparent metrics (timelines for guidance, licence decisions, AI-focused resource allocation). Includes the option of a central body with "statutory powers to issue pilot sandbox licences for AI products that override sector regulations, taking on liability for all related risks"
- **Rec 29**: Support AI assurance ecosystem via AISI's systemic-AI-safety fast grants programme + government-backed assurance tools
- **Rec 30**: Consider full potential of the **Alan Turing Institute** to drive progress at the cutting edge

## Section 2 — Change lives by embracing AI (Recs 31-49)

**"Scan → Pilot → Scale" approach** in government [Action Plan p.17]:

### SCAN (Recs 31-33)
- **Rec 31**: **AI lead for each mission** — identify AI as solution within mission setting
- **Rec 32**: Cross-government technical horizon scanning + market intelligence capability
- **Rec 33**: Two-way partnerships with AI vendors and startups

### PILOT (Recs 34-38)
- **Rec 34**: Consistent framework for whether to **build / buy / run innovation challenges** — open-source preference; design with startups in mind
- **Rec 35**: Rapid prototyping capability
- **Rec 36**: Hire external AI talent — **AI-related civil service role pay benchmarked to ≥75% of private-sector rate**; technical AI recruitment screening
- **Rec 37**: Data-rich experimentation environment
- **Rec 38**: **Faster, multi-stage gated AI procurement** — "Competitive Flexible Procedures"; **startups compensated for rounds they make it through**

### SCALE (Recs 39-42)
- **Rec 39**: **Scaling service for successful pilots** with senior support + central funding
- **Rec 40**: **Mission-focused national AI tenders** — example: NHS AI Diagnostic Fund £21M to 12 imaging networks covering 66 NHS Trusts
- **Rec 41**: Scalable AI tech stack supporting specialist + foundation models for **tens or hundreds of millions of citizen interactions**
- **Rec 42**: **Mandate infrastructure interoperability, code reusability, and open sourcing**

### Public-private reinforcement (Recs 43-46)
- **Rec 43**: Procure smartly as largest customer AND market shaper; **"contemplation" clauses** in contracts requiring contractors to regularly assess + adopt newer AI technologies
- **Rec 44**: **API mandate (Bezos-style)** — all standard documentation interactions (compliance, planning) accessible via APIs that companies can connect their tools to; mandated e-Invoicing
- **Rec 45**: Single **"AI Knowledge Hub"** for best-practice guidance, results, case studies, open-source solutions
- **Rec 46**: Digital Centre of Government to identify quick wins within 3 months

### Private-sector adoption (Recs 47-49)
- **Rec 47**: Leverage the new Industrial Strategy for AI adoption
- **Rec 48**: **AI Sector Champions** in life sciences, financial services, creative industries
- **Rec 49**: Drive AI adoption across the whole country; **local trusted intermediaries + trade bodies** for SMEs

## Section 3 — Secure our future with homegrown AI (Rec 50)

The most ambitious single recommendation:

> "Generating national champions will require a more activist approach and something more akin to **Japan's MITI or Singapore's Economic Development Board in the 1960s, not the 'invisible hand'**." [Action Plan p.22]

**Rec 50 — Create UK Sovereign AI**: a new unit with the power to partner with the private sector to **maximise the UK's stake in frontier AI**. Functions:
- Public-private joint ventures
- Direct investment in startups + scale-ups
- Spin-out support
- International collaboration
- Concentrated bets in strategically important AI research areas
- Government offer includes: capital + AGZ-located compute + UK-owned data sets + headhunting + national-security collaboration
- In exchange: **economic upside from + governance influence on frontier AI for the UK**

Frontier AI is framed as a **national and economic security pillar by end of decade**. Compute used for frontier models in 2024 is **10,000× more than in 2019**; assume similar growth to 2029. Government cannot wait for the market to underwrite a new challenger — must act as activist industrial-policy state.

## The AI Playbook — operational layer (118 pages)

The Playbook sits **beneath** the Action Plan as the operational guide that civil servants actually use. It answers the "how do you do this?" question for every public-sector AI deployment.

### Authorship and scope [Playbook p.5]

Acknowledgements span:
- **~25 government departments + arm's length bodies + devolved administrations + public bodies**: Cabinet Office (incl. No.10 DS + i.AI teams), DSIT, DBT, DfE, DEFRA, DESNZ, DHSC, DLUHC, DfT, DWP, FCDO, GLD, HMLR, HMRC, HMT, HO, MoD, MoJ, CCS; plus DVLA, GCHQ, GIAA, HMCTS, ICO, Met Office, NHS, ONS, Scottish Government
- **4 industry partners**: AWS, Google, IBM, Microsoft
- **5 academic institutions**: Alan Turing Institute, BCS (Chartered Institute for IT), Oxford Internet Institute, Manchester Metropolitan University, University of Surrey

### The 10 Principles [Playbook p.9-15]

| # | Principle |
|---|---|
| 1 | You know what AI is and what its limitations are |
| 2 | You use AI lawfully, ethically and responsibly |
| 3 | You know how to use AI securely |
| 4 | You have meaningful human control at the right stages |
| 5 | You understand how to manage the full AI life cycle |
| 6 | You use the right tool for the job |
| 7 | You are open and collaborative |
| 8 | You work with commercial colleagues from the start |
| 9 | You have the skills and expertise needed to implement and use AI |
| 10 | You use these principles alongside your organisation's policies and have the right assurance in place |

Each principle has a one-page expansion + a downloadable poster for display in government organisations [p.9].

### Playbook structure

| Section | Pages | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| **Principles** | 9-15 | The 10 cross-cutting principles |
| **Understanding AI** | 16-26 | Definitions, AI fields (NN, ML, DL, SR, CV, NLP, generative AI, agentic AI), applications in government, limitations |
| **Building AI solutions** | 28-45 | Building the team, acquiring skills, defining the goal, user research, identifying use cases, buying AI, procurement routes |
| **Using AI safely and responsibly** | 46-101 | Ethics, legal considerations, data protection and privacy, **security (41 pages of operational depth)**, governance |
| **Appendix: 6 case studies** | 103-116 | GOV.UK Chat, CCS recommendation system, FCDO Cicero, NHS user research finder, NHS.UK reviews moderator |

### Notable Playbook-specific operational mechanisms

1. **Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard (ATRS)** [Principle 7, Playbook p.13] — **mandatory** for central government departments and in-scope arm's length bodies. Requires documenting and publishing algorithmic tools used in decision-making processes. "This response has been written by an automated AI chatbot" disclosure required. **Citizens can see which algorithms are being used by which departments.**

2. **AI/ML Systems Inventory** [Governance section, Playbook p.98] — **department-level inventory** of all deployed AI systems.

3. **AI Community of Practice** [Principle 7, Playbook p.13] — cross-government collaboration mechanism for problem-solving and knowledge sharing.

4. **5-Tier Learner Segmentation** [Playbook p.29] — cross-government user research (CDDO 2023+2024) identified five distinct AI-learner groups:
   1. Beginners — general civil servants new to AI
   2. Technical roles outside government digital and data — operational delivery, policy profession
   3. Data and analytics professionals
   4. Digital and data professionals — developers, data scientists, software engineers
   5. Senior leaders — SROs, policy decision-makers

5. **Documented Case Studies — 6 Real UK Public-Sector AI Implementations** [Appendix, Playbook p.103-116]:

| Project | Technology | Owner | Key result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GOV.UK Chat** | RAG + OpenAI API + Qdrant vector store | Government Digital Service | 80% answer accuracy; 69-70% users found useful; pilot phase |
| **CCS commercial agreement recommendation system** | TensorFlow Recommenders, two-tower NN | Crown Commercial Service | Netflix-style discovery for procurement |
| **FCDO Digital Sensitivity Review (Team Cicero)** | Lifelong ML + ISO 44001 collaboration | FCDO Services + Sheffield/Glasgow/Loughborough Universities | **10% of the manual sensitivity-review effort**; saved 100+ FTEs |
| **NHS User Research Finder** | LLM API + custom database | NHS England UCD team | Private beta; 13-week build with commercial partner |
| **NHS.UK Reviews Automated Moderator** | NLP with BERT + BoW + MNET; synthetic data for low-frequency categories | NHS England Data Science team | Hundreds of thousands of reviews moderated; suicidal/self-harm detection + PII + abuse |
| **GOV.UK Chat user research methodology** | Multi-phase usability + accuracy testing | GDS | Pattern document for evaluating AI products |

This is the **only document in the entire comparator set with documented, sized, named-team case studies of national-scale public-sector AI implementations**.

6. **Security depth — 41 pages** [Playbook p.46-87] — deployment patterns, AI-specific risks (data poisoning, perturbation attacks, prompt injections, hallucinations), attack/defense scenarios. References Government Cyber Security Standard + NCSC cloud security principles + Secure by Design principles (CDDO).

7. **AI Governance Board / Ethics Committee** [Playbook p.97] — each AI project requires AI governance board (or AI representation on existing board). Higher-risk projects require ethics committee including internal stakeholders, cross-government stakeholders, sector experts, and **external stakeholders (Civil Society Organisations)**.

8. **Routes to Market for AI Procurement** [Buying AI, Playbook p.41] — Crown Commercial Service (CCS) frameworks simplify AI procurement.

## What the UK does that Portugal's ANIA does NOT

### Strategic (from Action Plan)

1. **An "AI maker, not AI taker" national positioning** — explicit choice to be **at the frontier**, not merely an importer/applier. Portugal's ANIA correctly positions itself as hybrid Development+Application but stops short of frontier ambition.

2. **UK Sovereign AI unit (Rec 50)** with activist Japan-MITI/Singapore-EDB-style mandate. Portugal has no equivalent national-champions vehicle.

3. **AI Growth Zones (Rec 4)** — streamlined planning + clean-power AI data centre regime. Portugal I.4 (National Data Centre Plan) is comparable in spirit but lacks the AGZ-as-instrument framing + named candidate locations + post-industrial regeneration tie-in.

4. **AIRR 20× expansion by 2030** with **DARPA/ARIA-style programme directors** (Rec 2-3). Portugal I.1 (Advanced Computing/EuroHPC) is incremental; UK is openly aggressive.

5. **National Data Library (NDL)** with **5+ high-impact public data sets identified + copyright-cleared British media asset training set + partnerships with National Archives, Natural History Museum, British Library, BBC** (Recs 7-13). Portugal I.3 (sectoral data spaces) is comparable in spirit but lacks the cultural-heritage-as-training-data infrastructure.

6. **AI Safety Institute (AISI)** as **first globally to conduct pre-deployment evaluations** of frontier models. Portugal has no equivalent frontier-model evaluation institution.

7. **£400 billion AI adoption boost by 2030** as the headline macroeconomic target. Portugal cites €18-22B/yr GDP add + 2.7pp productivity — same order of magnitude but the UK figure is **stock** (cumulative by 2030) rather than **flow** (annual).

8. **AI Sector Champions** in life sciences, financial services, creative industries (Rec 48). Portugal names sectoral AI centres (II.5) but no equivalent industry-anchored champion role.

9. **All regulators publish annual AI-enablement reports with transparent metrics** (Rec 28). Portugal IV.4 (EU AI Act implementation) does not yet specify equivalent regulator transparency.

10. **HPI visa extension to specific global AI institutions** (IIT, Carnegie Mellon) (Rec 21). Portugal III.5 (AI Fast Track) is general; UK targets specific universities by name.

11. **Flagship Rhodes/Marshall/Fulbright-scale AI scholarship** for 100 elite scholars (Rec 18). Portugal has goPortugal (III.4) but no equivalent global-prestige scholarship instrument.

12. **Bezos-style API mandate for government** (Rec 44) — all standard documentation interactions accessible via APIs that private companies can connect to. Portugal has no equivalent.

13. **National AI Tenders model — NHS AI Diagnostic Fund** as concrete template (Rec 40). Portugal II.12 (National AI Calls for PA) is comparable but lacks the named flagship project at NHS scale.

14. **"Scan → Pilot → Scale" framework** (Recs 31-42) as named operational doctrine for public-sector AI. Portugal's ANIA has no equivalent named methodology.

15. **Civil service AI roles paid at ≥75% private-sector rate** (Rec 36). Portugal has no equivalent salary commitment for public-sector AI staff.

### Operational (from Playbook)

16. **Comprehensive operational playbook for civil servants** — Portugal has II.11 (PA Centre of Excellence) + II.12 (PA AI Calls) + II.13 (Procurement Code Guide) + III.1 (Doutor AP training) but **no integrated operational manual**.

17. **Mandatory Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard (ATRS)** — Portugal has no equivalent.

18. **Documented case studies of public-sector AI** — Portugal has zero documented case studies of equivalent depth.

19. **AI/ML systems inventory at department level** — Portugal has no equivalent mandatory inventory.

20. **5-tier civil-servant learner segmentation** — Portugal III.1 + III.2 are at higher abstraction level.

21. **AI Community of Practice** — cross-Ministry / cross-DG collaboration network. Portugal has no equivalent.

22. **41 pages of operational security guidance** specifically for government AI deployment. Portugal lacks an equivalent.

23. **AI governance board / ethics committee** as standard departmental practice. Portugal IV.3 (Centre for Responsible AI) covers this at national level only.

## What Portugal does that the UK documents do NOT

1. **EU AI Act anchoring** — UK is post-Brexit and pursues its own pro-innovation framework. Portugal IV.4 + IV.6 anchor explicitly to EU AI Act + GDPR.

2. **Per-initiative start dates** (Portugal: 2S 2025 → 1S 2027 per initiative card). UK Action Plan has 50 recommendations with "within 6 months" / "by 2030" markers but no per-recommendation timeline matrix.

3. **Strategic technology bets explicitly named** (Cybersecurity, LLMs, AI+Robotics for Industry, Blue Economy, Responsible AI) [Portugal ANIA p.7]. UK's Action Plan covers many of these but doesn't enumerate strategic bets the same way.

4. **Sovereign LLM commitment** — Portugal's AMALIA (II.7). UK Action Plan's UK Sovereign AI (Rec 50) is broader than just LLMs; it addresses frontier AI as a category but doesn't name a specific national-language LLM.

5. **AI literacy citizen campaign** (Portugal III.8) + **AI Generation career campaign** (Portugal III.7). UK Playbook covers civil-servant awareness but lacks broad citizen-facing campaigns.

6. **Regulatory sandbox as a forward initiative** with named Portuguese-flagged scope (Portugal IV.5). UK has regulatory sandboxes in priority sectors (Action Plan Rec 27) but with a more decentralised model.

## Critique-lens conclusion

The UK provides **the most complete strategic-plus-operational stack** in the entire comparator set. Action Plan = vision, 50 recommendations, £400B target, "AI maker" positioning, UK Sovereign AI unit. Playbook = 10 principles, 118 pages of operational guidance, 6 documented case studies, mandatory transparency standard. **Portugal needs both layers**; today ANIA covers the strategic layer (well) but lacks the operational layer entirely.

### Top moves Portugal should import

1. **Publish a Portuguese "Plano de Acção para Oportunidades de IA"** alongside ANIA, with 30-50 specific recommendations (Portugal-proportional to UK's 50), each with Lead Ministry, "within 6 months / by 2027 / by 2030" milestones, and explicit funding source. Anchor to ANIA's 32 initiatives but make the recommendations more **operationally specific** than the cards. Independent commission methodology (à la France's Aghion-Bouverot or UK's Clifford) would strengthen credibility.

2. **Adopt the "Scan → Pilot → Scale" framework** as named operational doctrine within ANIA Pillar II. Currently Portugal's II.11-II.13 cover the Pilot phase but Scan + Scale phases are not articulated as named methodology.

3. **Establish Portuguese Sovereign AI ambition** — Portugal cannot match UK frontier-AI ambition at the same scale, but should articulate **what frontier components Portugal will own** (likely: Portuguese-language LLM via AMALIA at scale; Atlantic-Lusophone data infrastructure; cybersecurity AI; AI+Blue Economy maritime applications). Pick 2-3 frontier domains where Portugal has comparative advantage.

4. **Commission a Portuguese AI Playbook for AP** under the umbrella of ANIA II.11 (PA Centre of Excellence). Lead drafting: AMA + INA + Centro para a IA Responsável. **Modelled on the UK Playbook structure**: 10 principles + understanding AI + building AI solutions + using AI safely and responsibly + Portuguese case studies appendix. Cite Portuguese instruments (CCP, RGPD, EU AI Act, NP 4595, LCN). This is the single most importable UK move for ANIA delivery.

5. **Establish a Portuguese Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard (PT-ATRS)** — mandatory documentation and publication of AI tools used in PA decision-making. Anchored under ANIA IV.4 + IV.6.

6. **Document Portuguese AI case studies in a public registry** — Centro para a IA Responsável maintains a public registry equivalent to UK's 6 case studies, growing as II.12 (National AI Calls for PA) pilots mature.

7. **AI/ML Systems Inventory mandate per Ministry** under ANIA IV.4 EU AI Act implementation.

8. **5-tier civil-servant AI learner segmentation** under III.1 + III.2.

9. **Civil service AI salary benchmarking** — match Portuguese public-sector AI roles to ≥75% of equivalent private-sector pay (or another defensible benchmark), addressing III.5's underlying retention issue.

10. **Portuguese AI Sector Champions** in 3-5 industries — equivalent to UK's named champions in life sciences, financial services, creative industries. For Portugal, natural picks: Health, Pharmaceuticals, Tourism, Blue Economy, Footwear/Textiles, Aerospace (via Edisoft).

11. **AI Knowledge Hub for Portuguese public administration** — single point for case studies, frameworks, open-source solutions. Modelled on UK Rec 45.

12. **API mandate for Portuguese public administration** (Bezos/UK style, Rec 44) — all standard documentation interactions accessible via APIs that companies can connect their tools to. Builds on existing Portal de Serviços + ePortugal but more ambitious.

13. **Portuguese AI Growth Zones** (modelled on UK AIGZs, Rec 4) — co-located near renewable energy, with streamlined planning. Natural candidates: Sines (port + LNG terminal + Vodafone cable), Évora (Sines-adjacent), Açores (low-temperature ambient).

14. **Flagship Portuguese AI Scholarship** — Rhodes/Marshall/Fulbright-scale (à la UK Rec 18), 20-30 scholars/year, FCT-administered, with placements in Portuguese public administration or AI startups.

15. **Headhunting capability** to recruit small numbers of elite AI individuals to Portugal (Rec 20-21 equivalent). Extension of III.5 (AI Fast Track visa) to include named-institution graduates of top global AI universities.

### What Portugal should NOT copy

- **The post-Brexit pro-innovation regulatory stance** — Portugal correctly anchors to EU AI Act; replicating UK's regulatory autonomy would dilute that.
- **UK Sovereign AI at the same financial scale** — UK is 3rd largest AI market globally with frontier-AI companies (DeepMind, Wayve). Portugal's scale is proportionately smaller; activist industrial policy should focus on **narrower frontier wedges** where Portugal can credibly compete (Portuguese LLM, Atlantic data infrastructure, AI+Blue Economy).
- **The HPI visa specificity** — Portugal's AI Fast Track is closer to a normal-skilled-worker visa; replicating UK's named-university approach risks bilateral diplomatic complications.

### Strategic complementarity with other comparators

UK's strategic-plus-operational stack is **the most complete** in this set, but Portugal can benefit from combining UK's operational rigour with **Spain's per-initiative budget transparency** (Tabla 6), **France's per-recommendation Ministry assignment**, and **Italy's Foundation-under-PM coordination model**. The ideal Portuguese ANIA evolution would adopt:
- UK structure (strategic plan + operational playbook + documented case studies)
- Spain budget format (Tabla 6: per-initiative € + funding source)
- France recommendation format (per-recommendation Lead Ministry)
- Italy coordination model (Fundação ANIA under PM Office)
