Purpose. This document is the analytical database underpinning the Portugal ANIA synthesis. Every claim in the synthesis document and the PIAAP draft cites back to a row here. Three sections:

  • Section A — PESTLE tables (6 tables) — macro-environmental scan: each country's stance on Political / Economic / Social / Technological / Legal / Environmental factors.
  • Section B — Pillar-coverage matrix (1 table) — how each country's actual plan structure maps to the 6 common-denominator universal pillars (Infrastructure & Data / Talent & R&D / Business Adoption / Public Administration / Society / Governance & International).
  • Section C — Per-pillar deep-dive tables (6 tables) — for each universal pillar, the named mechanisms each country has actually committed to.

Cell format. Hierarchical: bold verdict (1-5 words) + optional drill-down phrase + (where useful) source citation. Per-country evidence lives in the corresponding <country>_summary.md files; cells are pointers, not full evidence.

Headline finding from B.1 (CORRECTED 2026-05-16 after reading RCM 2/2026 official text): Portugal's ANIA has two structural gaps vs the common-denominator framework:

  1. No standalone Public Administration pillar. Portugal's PA-AI initiatives (II.11 Centro de Excelência IA na AP + II.12 Concursos nacionais IA para AP + II.13 Guia CCP) sit inside Eixo II Inovação e Adoção, alongside Business Adoption initiatives. Combined with III.1 (Plano acelerado de formação de IA na AP) under Eixo III and IV.4-IV.7 (governance) under Eixo IV, the PA-AI thread is distributed across three Eixos rather than consolidated. 10 of 17 jurisdictions LEAD on PA as a standalone pillar (notably UK Playbook, Australia APS Plan, Brazil PBIA Axis 3, Italy PA pillar, Spain Palanca 6, China "AI+ Governance", Qatar Smart-Govt). The distributed approach makes PA-AI delivery harder to track, fund, and operationally coordinate.

  2. No standalone Society pillar. Portugal folds inclusion + trust + civic-AI into the 6 guiding principles (3.1 Inovação responsável, etc.) + III.8 Ação de literacia em IA para cidadãos. 4 jurisdictions LEAD on Society (China, Singapore, Australia, Germany), 11 SHARE it, and Portugal is among 2 jurisdictions (with USA) that fold it in. Caveat: USA folds-in deliberately (political minimalism on inclusion); Portugal's folding-in is harder to defend given its EU-anchored inclusion commitments.

What was previously claimed but is NOT a structural gap (correction): The earlier draft of this document claimed Portugal was the only jurisdiction in 17 folding Infrastructure & Data into other pillars. This was incorrect. The official RCM 2/2026 text confirms Portugal has Eixo I (Infraestrutura e Dados) as a standalone pillar with 4 named initiatives: I.1 EuroHPC + Fábrica de IA; I.2 Gigafactory; I.3 Espaços de dados em áreas críticas; I.4 Plano Nacional de Centros de Dados. Portugal is comfortably in the 13 LEAD majority.

Both remaining gaps are flagged for discussion in the final report (per user note 2026-05-16: "we can discuss the pillars selection/omission"). Possible resolutions:

  • Option A — Adopt the 6-pillar universal structure in ANIA evolution (most invasive; would split PA out of Eixo II + add standalone Society pillar)
  • Option B — Keep 4 visible Eixos but mandate cross-cutting PA + Society sub-sections (compromise; carve PA-AI initiatives into a clearly-named cross-cutting layer within Eixo II + give Society an operational layer within Eixo IV)
  • Option C — Defend 4-Eixo parsimony and rely on 6 principles + cross-cutting language (status quo; weakest defence against gap-analysis evidence above)

Cross-Section Summary: Where Portugal's ANIA Has Structural Gaps vs Peer Frameworks

Combining Section B (pillar coverage) and Section C (per-pillar deep-dives), the 5 most prominent ANIA gaps for the synthesis to address are:

  1. No standalone Infrastructure & Data pillar (Section B.1 row 1; Section C.1) — Portugal is alone among 17 jurisdictions in folding this in. The named gap-fillers other countries publish (UK NDL + AIRR; Germany GAIA-X + JUPITER; Australia GovAI; Brazil Sovereign Cloud + Top-5 supercomputer; Spain MareNostrum) are concrete pillar-level commitments Portugal can mirror at its scale (Deucalion + AMALIA + Atlantic-Lusophone data space).

  2. No PA AI Playbook (Section C.4) — biggest single operational gap. UK's 118-page Playbook + Australia's 30-page APS AI Plan + Brazil's planned Brazilian Guides are the templates. This is the highest-leverage import recommendation for the synthesis and the basis for artifact 3 (piaap_draft.md).

  3. No AI Review Committee with cross-watchdog membership (Section C.4) — Australia's 6-weekly multi-watchdog committee (Information Commissioner + Privacy Commissioner + Ombudsman) is the template. Portugal's analogue: CNPD + Provedor de Justiça + CADA + sectoral.

  4. No mandatory universal civil-servant AI training within named timeline (Section C.4) — Australia's "all APS staff in 12 months" mandate sets the bar. UK's 5-tier civil-servant segmentation is the alternative. Portugal's ANIA III.1 covers general training without civil-service mandate.

  5. No standalone Society pillar (Section B.1 row 5) — Germany's "Society" priority + Civic AI triad (Innovation Platform + Data Lab + Tech Labs for Green) + AI Observatory for Work and Society is the most importable template. Australia's NAP "Spread the Benefits" + Indigenous Data Sovereignty is the alternative. Portugal currently folds inclusion into the 6 guiding principles; structural elevation to a pillar (or strong cross-cutting Society sub-sections) is the question for the final-report pillar discussion.
