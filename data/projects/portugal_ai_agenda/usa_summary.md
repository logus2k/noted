# United States — Winning the Race: America's AI Action Plan (July 2025)

**Source:** [eua_ai_action_plan.pdf](../../domains/national_ai_agendas/sources/eua_ai_action_plan.pdf), 28 pages. The White House, Office of Science and Technology Policy. Signed by Michael J. Kratsios (Assistant to the President for S&T), David O. Sacks (Special Advisor for AI and Crypto), Marco A. Rubio (Assistant to the President for National Security Affairs) [p.2]. Derived from Executive Order 14179, "Removing Barriers to American Leadership in Artificial Intelligence," 23 Jan 2025 [p.1].

## ⚠️ Caveat on framing

This is an explicitly **competitive / national-security framed** document. The opening quote and recurring theme is "global AI dominance" and "Winning the Race" [cover, p.1]. Much of the language ("Counter Chinese Influence," "Build, Baby, Build," "free from ideological bias," "DEI / climate change") is politically charged and is not a frame the EU or Portugal would adopt wholesale. For Portugal-critique purposes, the value is the **mechanisms, institutions, and gaps the plan addresses** — not its geopolitical posture. Strip the partisan language and the document is a very dense operational playbook.

## Vision

"Unquestioned and unchallenged global technological dominance" in AI [p.i]. Three principles cut across all pillars: (1) worker-first AI policy; (2) ideology-free, "objective truth"-oriented systems; (3) prevention of misuse and theft [p.1-2].

## Structure — 3 pillars [Table of Contents, p.ii]

| Pillar | Sub-sections (~ count) | Theme |
|---|---|---|
| I. Accelerate AI Innovation | 15 | Deregulation, adoption, research, science, evaluations, federal adoption, defence |
| II. Build American AI Infrastructure | 7 | Permitting, grid, semiconductors, secure data centres, workforce, cybersecurity, incident response |
| III. Lead in International AI Diplomacy and Security | 7 | Exports, China containment, export controls, frontier-model security, biosecurity |

Every sub-section follows a fixed template: (a) prose framing, (b) "Recommended Policy Actions" — a bulleted list where each action names a **lead Federal agency or office**. Lead agencies named include OSTP, OMB, DOC (incl. NIST and CAISI), DOL, ED, NSF, DOE, DOD, DOJ, DHS, ODNI, FCC, FTC, FDA, SEC, NSC, GSA, Treasury, BLS, Census Bureau, BEA, NIH, USDA.

## Notable mechanisms — and where Portugal has nothing equivalent

The plan introduces or formalises a long list of named federal mechanisms. Each row below maps a US mechanism to the closest thing in Portugal's ANIA and flags the gap.

| US mechanism | Portugal ANIA closest | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| **CAIOC — Chief AI Officer Council** (cross-agency coordination body) [p.11] | None | ANIA has 15+ entities scattered across cards but **no central PMO or coordination council** |
| **NAIRR — National AI Research Resource** (compute/data/model access for academics & startups) [p.5] | I.1 EuroHPC/Deucalion access | ANIA has the supercomputer but no **researcher-portal framing**, no specific access programme |
| **CAISI at NIST — Center for AI Standards and Innovation** [p.4, p.10] | None | ANIA has no dedicated **AI evaluations agency**; II.6 (regulatory sandboxes) and IV.6 (EU AI Act implementation guide) cover compliance not capability eval |
| **AI Workforce Research Hub** under DOL — sustained labor-market measurement [p.6] | III.2 National Smart Skills Framework | ANIA's Framework is a one-shot mapping; **no recurring measurement function** for AI labor impact |
| **AI-ISAC — AI Information Sharing and Analysis Center** under DHS [p.18] | None | ANIA has no **critical-infrastructure AI threat-sharing** body |
| **AI Incident Response** doctrine (CISA playbooks updated for AI) [p.19] | None | ANIA has no **AI incident response track** at all |
| **AI procurement toolbox at GSA** — catalogue of compliant models for any Federal agency [p.11] | II.13 Procurement Code Practical Guide | ANIA's guide is interpretation only; no **pre-vetted model catalogue** |
| **Mandate that all Federal employees who could benefit from frontier LLMs have access** [p.11] | III.1 Doutor AP / Accelerated AI training in PA | ANIA's PA training is much **softer** — training availability, not a deployment mandate |

| **NEPA Categorical Exclusions + FAST-41 + Federal lands for data centres** [p.14-15] | I.4 National Data Centre Plan (pre-defined areas, simplified licensing) | ANIA mentions the idea; US has the **specific statutory hooks** |
| **AI & Autonomous Systems Virtual Proving Ground at DOD** + Senior Military Colleges as AI hubs [p.12] | II.11 mentions defence as one focus of PA Center of Excellence | ANIA has **no standalone defence/IC AI track**; defence is folded into a single bullet |
| **Whole-genome sequencing program for life on Federal lands** (training data for biofoundation models) [p.9] | I.3 Sectoral data spaces | ANIA's data spaces are generic; US gets specific about a **sector-grade national dataset programme** |
| **Open-source / open-weight AI as a strategic priority** with named programmes [p.4-5] | II.7 AMALIA continuation | ANIA continues an existing project; US frames open weights as a **national bet** |
| **AI Evaluations Ecosystem** (NIST AI Consortium + AI testbeds across agriculture/transport/healthcare) [p.10] | II.5 Sectoral AI Centres | ANIA's centres are adoption-focused; US testbeds are **evaluation-focused** |
| **Synthetic media / deepfake legal framework** — TAKE IT DOWN Act + DOJ Federal Rules of Evidence Rule 901(c) for deepfakes [p.12-13] | None | ANIA has **no deepfake / synthetic-media initiative**, despite this being one of the most cited public-trust threats |
| **AI compute export controls + chip location verification** [p.21] | None | Not applicable for Portugal as importer, but parallel: ANIA has no **strategic-tech denial regime** for re-export |
| **Biosecurity — DNA synthesis screening for AI-enabled bioweapons** [p.23] | None | ANIA has no **biosecurity track** despite AI+bio being a top-tier dual-use risk |
| **CBRNE risk evaluation in frontier models** [p.22] | None | ANIA's IV.5 (sandboxes) covers compliance testing, not **catastrophic-risk evaluation** |
| **Federal Rules of Evidence Rule 901(c) for deepfakes** [p.13] | None | ANIA has no **courts/judiciary AI track** |

## What Portugal does that the US Plan does NOT do

To balance the asymmetry, the US Plan is silent or weak on several things Portugal's ANIA gets right:

1. **No explicit quantitative economic case.** US Plan has no equivalent of Portugal's €18-22B/yr GDP add and +2.7pp productivity contribution [Portugal ANIA p.3].
2. **No timeline / start dates per action.** US actions are "Recommended" without sequencing.
3. **No consolidated budget envelope.** (Neither plan publishes a total, but Portugal at least cites specific allocations like the €100M BPF line [Portugal ANIA p.12].)
4. **No K-12 / youth career pipeline campaign** equivalent to Portugal's "AI Generation" (III.7) [Portugal ANIA p.18] — US covers pre-apprenticeships and CTE in passing.
5. **No civic AI-literacy campaign** equivalent to Portugal III.8 [Portugal ANIA p.18].
6. **No alignment with a supranational regulatory framework** (Portugal aligns with EU AI Act and EU AI Continent Action Plan [Portugal ANIA p.2, p.20]; the US Plan is the opposite — counter-EU/OECD coordination).

## Style — what's different

| Dimension | US Action Plan | Portugal ANIA |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 28 pages, dense prose + bullets | 22 pages, slide-deck format |
| Legal hooks | Inline US Code, Federal Register, Executive Orders cited in footnotes | None cited |
| Action ownership | Every bullet names a **lead Federal agency** | Cards list "Relevant Entities" but no single owner |
| Timelines | None — "Recommended Policy Actions" | Each initiative has a start semester (2S 2025 → 1S 2027) |
| Budget | None | Aggregate not stated; specific lines mentioned (€100M BPF, €1.2B PA potential) |
| Quantitative case | None | €18-22B/yr GDP add, +2.7pp productivity, 8% GDP at peak |
| Tone | Adversarial, geopolitical | Cooperative, EU-aligned |

The two are **complementary documents at different altitudes**: Portugal's ANIA is a national operational plan with timeline and KPIs; the US Plan is an executive-branch playbook with named agencies and legal levers but no schedule. Each side could borrow what the other does well.

## Critique-lens conclusion

The US Action Plan is the single richest source of **mechanisms Portugal's ANIA could selectively import**. Top candidates (after stripping the geopolitical framing):

1. **Designate a central AI coordination body** (Portuguese CAIOC-equivalent) to own ANIA delivery across the 15+ entities currently scattered across cards. Without this, "Relevant Entities" is a list, not accountability.
2. **Open an AI Incident Response track** in Pillar IV (Responsibility & Ethics), including a national AI-ISAC for critical infrastructure. Currently ANIA has zero coverage of post-deployment incident handling.
3. **Add a Deepfake / Synthetic Media initiative** under Pillar IV, with a legal hook to the EU AI Act's transparency provisions (Article 50) and to Portuguese evidence law. ANIA has no coverage of one of the most-cited public-trust threats.
4. **Add a Biosecurity / dual-use AI initiative** under Pillar IV — at minimum a DNA-synthesis-screening commitment as part of Responsible AI research.
5. **Add an AI-Energy-Grid initiative** under Pillar I — Portugal claims renewables as a competitive advantage [Portugal ANIA p.4] but ANIA does not tie this to AI compute load. The US grid section is a model for the framing.
6. **Move from "training availability" to a "frontier-AI access mandate" for civil servants** whose work would benefit (sharpens III.1).
7. **Make defence/IC AI its own track**, separate from the generic PA Center of Excellence (II.11), even at small scale.
8. **Add a sectoral national-dataset programme** along the lines of the US whole-genome programme — Portugal could pick maritime (aligned with the Blue Economy strategic bet) or health to start.
9. **Add a federally-mandated AI procurement catalogue** (compliant pre-vetted models) above II.13's procurement-code guide.
10. **Add an AI evaluations agency** (Portuguese CAISI-equivalent), distinct from the regulatory sandboxes (IV.5). Sandboxes test compliance; evaluations test capability and risk.

What Portugal should **NOT** copy:

- The adversarial, China-containment framing — Portugal's geopolitics is EU-multilateral.
- The "remove DEI/climate from AI risk frameworks" stance — directly contradicts Portugal's Pillar IV.
- The deregulation-as-default tone — incompatible with EU AI Act alignment which ANIA correctly anchors to (IV.4) [Portugal ANIA p.20].
