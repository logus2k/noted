# Argentina — Bases para la Agenda Digital Argentina (2018)

**Source:** [argentina_anexo_5628461_1.pdf](../../domains/national_ai_agendas/sources/argentina_anexo_5628461_1.pdf), 11 pages, IF-2018-55052681-APN-SGDEIT#JGM, signed 29 Oct 2018 by María Inés Baqué (Secretaría de Gobierno Digital e Innovación Tecnológica, Jefatura de Gabinete de Ministros) [p.12].

## ⚠️ Important caveat

This is **not an AI-specific national strategy**. It is the **foundational document of Argentina's broader Digital Agenda**, drafted in 2018 — pre-LLM era. AI is mentioned only once, in the intro, alongside IoT, cloud, VR/AR, robotics [p.2]. There is no AI ministry, no AI budget, no AI initiative, and no AI-specific KPI in the document. Treating it as Argentina's AI agenda would be a category error; use it only as a digital-policy baseline against which Portugal's ANIA can be compared on cross-cutting issues (governance, inclusion, sectoral coverage).

## Vision and objectives

Three vision pillars [p.4]:
- Full digital inclusion of all inhabitants
- Productivity and competitiveness of the economy
- A State at the service of its citizens

Eight specific objectives [p.4-5]: legal frameworks for digital information; smart-connectivity infrastructure; digital literacy as a driver of inclusion; efficient citizen-oriented government; digital education for future employability; productive-sector digital adoption (quantitative + qualitative leap); cybersecurity; international protagonism in digital transformation.

## Structure — 5 strategic axes [p.5-11]

| Axis | Focus |
|---|---|
| I. Normative Framework | Telecoms competition, data protection, cybersecurity legislation, international cooperation |
| II. Infrastructure | Connectivity (rural, schools, hospitals, municipalities, agricultural zones, production poles), cybersecurity infrastructure, network management |
| III. Education and Digital Inclusion | Digital inclusion (incl. **gender digital divide**), digital education in curricula, future-of-work skills, innovation ecosystem |
| IV. Digital Economy | Knowledge industry, **agro-tech and biotech**, SME digitalization, ICT hubs, new business ecosystem |
| V. Digital Government | Government-as-Platform, open data and open government, public-sector employment digitalization, federal-scale digital country |

Each axis lists "líneas de acción" numbered like an outline (1.1, 1.2, …). The action lines are imperative verb phrases ("Promote", "Develop", "Foster") with no dates, no responsible entities, no budget, and no KPIs.

## Diagnostic baseline cited [p.2-3]

- Fixed home internet penetration: 59.16%
- Mobile penetration: 136.8% (multiple SIMs)
- Average mobile speed: 13.85 Mbps
- WEF Networked Readiness Index 2017: **rank 89/139**
- IMD Digital Competitiveness 2018: **rank 55/63**

Argentina was framed in 2018 as a digital laggard relative to OECD/G20 peers.

## Governance posture

The document explicitly says the Agenda Digital Argentina "plantea un marco institucional y una estructura de gobernanza, para que los esfuerzos se realicen de manera coordinada" [p.3] — but no specific PMO, council, agency, ministry, or reporting cadence is named in this anexo.

## What stands out (and what does not)

**Notable angles Argentina's document raises that Portugal's ANIA omits or under-treats:**

1. **Gender digital divide as an explicit action line** [p.8, "Inclusión Digital 1.3 — Reducir de la brecha digital, teniendo en especial consideración la brecha de género"]. Portugal's ANIA has no gender lens anywhere in its 32 initiatives.
2. **Rural connectivity for schools, hospitals, agricultural zones, production poles** [p.8, axis II, lines 2.1–3.2]. Portugal's ANIA assumes its national fibre/connectivity backbone as a competitive advantage [Portugal ANIA p.4] and does not address rural/island connectivity for AI deployment.
3. **Agro-tech and biotech as a stand-alone economic-development axis** with four specific lines (precision agriculture, climate prediction, traceability, biotech) [p.10, axis IV, line 2]. Portugal's ANIA names *Blue Economy* as a strategic bet [Portugal ANIA p.7] but has no agriculture/agritech initiative, despite agriculture being a meaningful Portuguese sector.
4. **"Schools as technology nodes"** [p.9, axis III, line 2.2]. Portugal's ANIA has no K-12 strategy; its only youth-facing initiative is the "AI Generation" career-inspiration campaign (III.7) [Portugal ANIA p.18].
5. **"Tell us once" citizen principle** [p.11, axis V, line 1.6 — "Promover que las instituciones públicas soliciten la información sólo una vez a ciudadanos, organizaciones y empresas"]. Portugal's PA Center of Excellence (II.11) and the Public Procurement guide (II.13) are vaguer on the citizen interface.
6. **Federal/territorial scaling** [p.11, axis V, line 4 — "País digital", with explicit support for municipalities]. Portugal's ANIA does not address regional/municipal AI capacity, despite Portugal's known disparities (Lisbon/Porto vs. interior).

**Where Portugal is clearly stronger than this Argentine document (so this is not aspirational on these axes):**

- **AI specificity**: Portugal's ANIA is AI-specific; Argentina's is digital-generic.
- **Quantified economic case**: Portugal cites €18-22B/yr GDP add and +2.7pp productivity [Portugal ANIA p.3]; Argentina cites only ranking deficits.
- **Implementation timing**: Portugal has 32 initiatives each with a start semester (2S 2025 → 1S 2027) and named responsible entities [Portugal ANIA p.11-21]; Argentina's action lines are timeless verbs.
- **Strategic positioning**: Portugal explicitly debates Development vs. Application and chooses a hybrid [Portugal ANIA p.7]; Argentina has no equivalent positioning.
- **Recency**: Portugal is January 2026; Argentina is October 2018 and predates the generative-AI era.

## Critique-lens conclusion

**Argentina is not a credible AI-policy aspiration target for Portugal — it is six AI-generations behind.** But the 2018 document still flags four sectoral and inclusion lenses worth importing into ANIA:

- A **gender lens** somewhere in the Talent or Responsibility pillar (none exists today).
- A **rural/regional/island AI access lens** (Portugal has the connectivity but no plan to deploy AI capacity outside Lisbon-Porto).
- An **agritech initiative** alongside Blue Economy (Portugal names the maritime sector but not the agricultural one).
- A **K-12 schools-as-AI-nodes initiative**, separate from the career-inspiration campaign (III.7).

Argentina would need its own current-decade AI strategy to be a real comparator; the document above predates ChatGPT, GDPR-era data-economy thinking, and the EU AI Act entirely.
