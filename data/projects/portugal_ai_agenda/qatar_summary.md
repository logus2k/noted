# Qatar — Digital Agenda 2030 (DA2030, launched 2024)

**Source:** [qatar_digital_agenda_2030_full_version_english.pdf](../../domains/national_ai_agendas/sources/qatar_digital_agenda_2030_full_version_english.pdf), 69 pages. Issued by the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology, State of Qatar (cover). Forewords by HE Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman bin Jassim Al Thani, Prime Minister and Minister of Foreign Affairs [p.3], and HE Mohammed bin Ali Al Mannai, Minister of Communications and Information Technology [p.4]. Launched 2024 as part of the timeline of Qatar's digital landscape [p.16].

## ⚠️ Caveat on framing

This is a **Digital Agenda**, not an AI-specific strategy. AI is one technology under the umbrella of "HyperAutomation," alongside HyperConnectivity (5G/IoT/cloud) and HyperComputing (HPC/quantum) [p.7-8, p.13-14]. There is a separate **National AI Committee** established 2022 [p.16 timeline] and a "pioneering National AI strategy" referenced [p.20], but **the AI strategy itself is not this document**. DA2030 sits inside a Russian-doll hierarchy: Qatar National Vision 2030 (QNV2030) → Third National Development Strategy NDS3 (2024-2030) → DA2030. For Portugal-critique purposes, treat DA2030 as a **mature peer to Portugal's Digital Strategy 2026-27** (under which ANIA's Action #20 sits [Portugal ANIA p.2]), not as an AI-specific comparator.

## Vision and mission [p.7, p.27]

- **Vision:** "Embrace digital advancement to drive Qatar's competitiveness and prosperity."
- **Mission:** "Establish a leading digital economy powered by an attractive and efficient business environment with high-yield digital investments."

| Pillar | Objective summary | Key strategic programmes |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Cutting-Edge Digital Infrastructure** | World-leading, secure, sustainable digital infrastructure [p.30] | SP01 Commercialise Qatar-based cloud / "data embassies"; SP02 Environmental Technology Programme (carbon footprint of ICT); SP03 National HPC Programme (cloud-native, AI-optimised) [p.33] |
| **2. Booming Digital Economy** | Drive digital adoption, transform Qatar into globally competitive, diversified digital economy [p.35] | SP04 Priority Sectors Digital Transformation Offices; SP05 SME & Enterprise Digital Transformation; SP06 Digital Export Support (incl. digital trade officers, digital export law); SP07 Cross-border Digital Economy Framework [p.38] |
| **3. Thriving Digital Innovation** | Bolster R&D + funding ecosystem | Digital FDI Attraction Programme; National Data & Digital Assets Monetisation Frameworks; Streamline Tech Start-Up Acceleration; National Applied Programmes for Emerging Tech [p.10] |
| **4. Seamless Digital Government** | Top-nation in e-services for citizens, residents, businesses, institutions | Drive Beneficiary-Centric Gov Services Transformation; Refine and Promote Centralised Services [p.10] |
| **5. Nurtured Digital Technologies** | Foster adoption of cutting-edge tech + best-in-class data foundations + appropriate regulation | Refine ICT Regulatory Landscape; National Data Management Frameworks; Technology Foresight Centre; National Data & Analytics Programme; National Emerging Tech Strategic Framework [p.10] |
| **6. Future Leading Digital Society** | Empower Qatar's society to thrive globally — "the HyperGeneration" | Digital Talent Attraction Programme; Promote New Ways of Working; Digital Talent Development & Retention; Expand Digital Inclusion Programme [p.10] |

## Existing baseline (the contrast with Portugal is sharp) [p.16, p.20, p.31]

- **96% 5G coverage**, **99% fibre coverage**
- **145% mobile broadband penetration**
- Median mobile speed: **207 Mbps** (2nd globally, only behind UAE at 269 Mbps) [p.31 chart]
- Personal Data Privacy Protection Law (DPL) since **2017** — first in GCC [p.20]
- Cloud First Framework (2023), National Cyber Security Strategy 2023-2028
- National AI Committee established 2022
- Microsoft Data Center (2022), Google Data Center (2023), Starlink LEO licensed (2022)
- National data-centre capacity 23.5 MW, growing **~45% per year**
- Qatar Computing Research Institute + Qatar Center for Quantum Computing + National Biomedical Informatics Center already operational [p.32]

## Notable mechanisms — and what's distinctive

1. **HyperGeneration framing** — entire strategy is pitched to a target demographic (the next generation of Qataris) as both the audience and the delivery vehicle [p.7, p.22, p.27]. Branding-heavy ("Q marks the spot," "Melting Pot," "Resilience and Rise"), light on detailed implementation.
2. **Data embassies** [p.33, SP01] — explicit policy proposition: regulate Qatar's data-centre operations to host sovereign data for allied countries / regional trade partners. A geopolitical play that exports Qatar's compliance regime.
3. **Digital Export Support Programme** [p.36, p.38, SP06] — treats digital goods and services as an **export sector**, with digital trade officers, a digital export law, partnerships with global marketplaces, and access to international funding. Qatar publishes its digital-services trade balance (4.1bn QAR exports = 3.7% of total services exports, vs. 4.3% of non-hydrocarbon GDP in digital imports) — a level of transparency Portugal does not match.
4. **Cross-border Digital Economy Framework** [p.37, SP07] — explicit plan to negotiate bilateral digital-trade agreements; references ASEAN Digital Economy Framework Agreement as model.
5. **Environmental Technology Programme** [p.32, SP02] — data-centre carbon neutrality by 2030, e-waste regulation, sustainable rules for new data-centre construction. Qatar signed the Climate Data Center Pact.
6. **National HPC Programme** explicitly **cloud-native and AI-optimised** [p.32, SP03] — frames HPC capacity as the platform for "genome sequencing, drug discovery, climate and weather modelling, digital twins."
7. **Technology Foresight Centre** [p.10, P5] — explicit horizon-scanning institution. Portugal's ANIA has no equivalent foresight body.
8. **"Beneficiary-Centric" Government Services** [p.10, P4] — framing is the **citizen as beneficiary**, not as user. Subtly different from Portugal's PA-centric framing (II.11 Center of Excellence, II.12 AI Calls, II.13 Procurement Guide).
9. **Reformed immigration policy** [p.20] — broader than AI-specific visas; attracts "entrepreneurs, freelancers, students, elite talent" generally.
10. **No regulatory sandbox** named — Qatar relies on "appropriate regulation" and DPL/cybersecurity laws, without a formal sandbox framework comparable to Portugal IV.5.

## What Qatar does that Portugal's ANIA does NOT do

1. **Data-embassy / data-as-export concept.** Portugal has the submarine-cable advantage [Portugal ANIA p.4] and renewables — perfect for being Europe's data embassy for Latin America/Africa — but ANIA does not articulate this play.
2. **Digital exports as a measured trade sector.** ANIA mentions exporting AI products in passing [Portugal ANIA p.7] but does not publish a digital-services trade balance, name a digital-export programme, or commit to digital trade officers.
3. **Data-centre carbon neutrality target.** Portugal claims a renewable-energy advantage but ANIA never names a data-centre sustainability commitment.
4. **Talent attraction primary + Talent retention secondary** as a deliberate split [P6 programmes, p.10]. Portugal III.5 (AI Fast Track) and III.4 (goPortugal) [Portugal ANIA p.18] conflate attraction and mobility into the same instrument.
5. **Cross-border digital trade framework** as a strategic programme. Portugal IV.7 (technology diplomacy) [Portugal ANIA p.21] is one sentence.
6. **National AI Committee as a standing governance body**, separate from the broader digital strategy [Qatar p.16 timeline, p.20]. ANIA does not name a National AI Council.
7. **Technology Foresight Centre** [p.10]. No equivalent in ANIA.

## What Portugal does that Qatar's DA2030 does NOT do

1. **Explicit per-initiative start dates and responsible entities.** Portugal's 32 cards each have a semester (2S 2025 → 1S 2027) and entities column [Portugal ANIA p.11-21]. Qatar's 23 strategic programmes are all in a single 2030 horizon with no sequencing visible.
2. **Quantitative economic case in € terms.** Qatar publishes 40bn QAR (a stock target) and 26k jobs; Portugal publishes €18-22B/yr ongoing GDP add and +2.7pp productivity contribution [Portugal ANIA p.3].
3. **Regulatory sandbox framework.** Portugal IV.5 [p.21]; Qatar has nothing equivalent named in DA2030.
4. **EU AI Act alignment.** Portugal IV.4 + IV.6 [Portugal ANIA p.20-21]. Qatar has DPL 2017 and Cybersecurity 2023-2028 but no aligned external regulatory baseline named.
5. **Granular SME-specific tools** like Portugal's II.10 PME.IA low-code/no-code AI platform [Portugal ANIA p.15]. Qatar's SP05 is broader and less specific.
6. **Strategic-bets clarity.** Portugal names 5 strategic bets (Cybersecurity, LLMs, AI+Robotics for Industry, Blue Economy, Responsible AI) [Portugal ANIA p.7]. Qatar lists 23 programmes but no equivalent prioritisation among them.

## Critique-lens conclusion

Qatar's DA2030 is **not a tactical document** in the sense Portugal's ANIA is — it operates one level higher, at digital-strategy altitude, with national-development scaffolding (NDS3 + QNV2030) carrying the operational weight. But three structural moves are directly importable into ANIA:

1. **"Data embassy" positioning for Portugal.** Portugal already has the **submarine-cable bridge to South America** [Portugal ANIA p.4] and renewables baseline — a perfect substrate for hosting Latin-American and Lusophone-African sovereign data and AI workloads under EU regulatory standards. ANIA's I.2 Gigafactory and I.4 National Data Centre Plan could be reframed with this **outward-facing data-hosting** dimension. Qatar's SP01 [p.33] is the template.

2. **Digital-services exports as a measured policy track.** ANIA names AI exports in passing [Portugal ANIA p.7] but has no equivalent of Qatar's SP06 — a programme to grow AI/digital exports, with named instruments (digital trade officers, digital export support law, marketplace partnerships, trade-balance accounting). Portugal already exports AI services (AMALIA, startup ecosystem with 6 unicorns [Portugal ANIA p.4]) and should measure and grow this as a sector.

3. **Data-centre sustainability commitment.** Portugal's renewable-energy advantage [Portugal ANIA p.4] is asserted but not operationalised in any of the 32 initiatives. A Portuguese version of Qatar's SP02 + Climate Data Center Pact membership would (a) align AI compute with the green transition, (b) differentiate Portugal in the EU AI Continent Action Plan context, and (c) make the Gigafactory pitch more competitive.

Secondary borrowable moves: a Technology Foresight Centre under Pillar I or II; an explicit split between Talent **Attraction** (visa, III.5) and Talent **Retention** (separate initiative — currently missing); a Beneficiary-Centric framing layered onto the existing PA initiatives (II.11-13).

What Portugal should **not** copy:
- The HyperGeneration / HyperPowers branding — overfits to Qatar's small-population, top-down narrative
- The unbounded 2030-horizon timing — Portugal's semester-by-semester schedule is operationally tighter
- The absence of a regulatory sandbox — Portugal's IV.5 is strictly better positioned for EU AI Act compliance
