# Egypt — National Artificial Intelligence Strategy, Second Edition (2025–2030)

**Source:** [Egypt AIstrategy English 16-1-2025-1.pdf](../../domains/national_ai_agendas/sources/Egypt%20AIstrategy%20English%2016-1-2025-1.pdf), 95 pages, published January 2025 by the **National Council for Artificial Intelligence (NCAI)** under the **Ministry of Communications and Information Technology (MCIT)**. Launched with a foreword from the **President of the Republic of Egypt**. This is the **Second Edition**, building on the **First Edition launched in May 2021**.

## Strategic framing

The 2021 first edition focused on awareness, education integration, professional development, R&D promotion, entrepreneurship, and international cooperation. The Second Edition explicitly responds to the post-2022 emergence of large generative models and reframes Egypt's positioning accordingly:

> "Inclusive AI to foster Digital Egypt, promoting social and economic development and benefiting all Egyptians. National foundational model as a basis to drive industry development and regional cooperation, making Egypt a leader in AI in Africa and the Arab region and an active international player." [Vision, p.7]

Mission:

> "Create an AI industry supported by governance, technology, data, infrastructure, ecosystem, and talents, to ensure its sustainability and competitiveness for purposes of promoting Egypt's development." [p.7]

## Six strategic objectives [p.7-8]

| # | Objective |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Ensure ethical and responsible AI use** by establishing a comprehensive AI regulatory system, activating the ethical framework, and putting a nucleus for a clear regulatory body |
| 2 | **Enhance quality of life and sectoral efficiency** through AI applications |
| 3 | **Ensure data accessibility and sharing** by developing national data governance frameworks and strengthening lifecycle management |
| 4 | **Build a robust scalable AI infrastructure and cloud services** as the digital foundation for AI |
| 5 | **Create a healthy AI ecosystem** by supporting startups, SMEs, innovation, and VC investment |
| 6 | **Strengthen quantity and quality of local AI talents and experts** |

## Strategic goals — quantitative [p.7-8 and p.90]

| Goal | Target | Year |
|---|---|---|
| ICT sector contribution to Egypt's GDP | **7.7%** | by 2030 |
| Marginal-population workforce benefiting from AI tools | **26%** | by 2030 |
| General public with access to AI / AI-powered products | **36%** | within 5 years |
| AI professionals / experts | **30,000** | by 2030 |
| Successful AI companies in Egypt | **250+** | by 2030 |
| AI publications per year | **6,000** (double current) | by 2030 |
| AI-dedicated strategic funding | **US$430–860M** (0.1–0.15% of GDP) | strategic level |
| Stimulate VC funding in AI | **US$150M** | by 2030 |
| Application of AI in businesses | **>10% of businesses** | by 2030 |
| Public awareness of AI development | **>36% of general public** acknowledge | by 2030 |

The benchmark references for the targets are explicit: Tortoise Media's Global AI Index 2023 for company counts (Austria 195, Ireland 221) and AI experts (Austria 318, Germany 4917), with Egypt currently at **1,401 experts** and ambition to grow to 30,000 by 2030 through industry growth to 250+ companies.

## Capacity Assessment Framework (CAF) — distinctive feature [Section 8]

Egypt's strategy uses a bespoke **Capacity Assessment Framework (CAF)** with **89 indicators** across the 6 pillars, derived from four international indices:

- **OECD Digital Government Index**
- **Stanford University HAI Global AI Index**
- **Government AI Readiness Index** (Oxford Insights)
- **Tortoise Media Global AI Index**

The CAF provides a **single score (out of 100)** plus a **global AI index ranking target**, tracked annually. The expected trajectory:

| Horizon | Initiatives implemented | % of CAF improved | Total Score | AI index ranking |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 year** | 4 | 28% | 55.1 / 100 | **#45** |
| **3 years** | 8 | 32% | 68.4 / 100 | **#32** |
| **5 years** | 8 | 40% | 81.7 / 100 | **#20** |

Explicit ranking targets in a national strategy are uncommon; Egypt's commitment to **reach top-20 globally in the AI Index by 2030** is the most quantitatively specific ranking aspiration in the comparator set.

## Selected initiative detail

### Pillar 1 — Governance

- **Initiative 1: Egypt National AI Regulatory System** — comprehensive regulatory framework
- **Initiative 2: Global and Regional AI Dialogues** — active role in international forums (UN, AU, Arab League, OECD)

### Pillar 2 — Technology

- **Initiative 3: National Foundation Models Development** — Arabic-first foundation model commitment, distinct from the Data pillar's Arabic data emphasis
- **Initiative 4: Abundant R&D Resources** — research funding scale-up
- **Initiative 5: AI Patent Granting System** — IP infrastructure specifically for AI inventions

### Pillar 3 — Data

- **Initiative 6: Comprehensive Data Governance Frameworks and Standards**
- **Initiative 7: High-Quality Sectoral Arabic Data Sets** — explicit sector-specific Arabic data assembly
- **Initiative 8: Open Data and Data Exchange Platform**
- **Initiative 9: Ensure Data Privacy and Security**

### Pillar 4 — ICT and AI Infrastructure

- **Initiative 10: Cutting-edge Domestic Data Center** — domestic-hosting commitment ("keeping data inside its borders")
- **Initiative 11: Intelligent Cloud and Computing Resource** — sovereign cloud emphasis
- **Initiative 12: Ubiquitous 5G and Fiber** — connectivity backbone
- **Initiative 13: Ensure Data Center Sustainability** — green data centre commitment

### Pillar 5 — Ecosystem

- **Initiative 14: Active and Secure Investment Environment** — including the international investment promotion sub-initiative E-I14-A6
- **Initiative 15: Industry-Academia-Research Platforms** — tripartite collaboration
- **Initiative 16: Support for AI Start-ups**
- **Initiative 17: Public Awareness of AI Development**

### Pillar 6 — Talents

- **Initiative 18: Senior AI Experts Cultivation and Attraction** — both grow-domestic and attract-foreign tracks
- **Initiative 19: International Academic Alliances** — university-level collaborations
- **Initiative 20: Cross-Discipline Course Development** — AI literacy in non-technical disciplines
- **Initiative 21: Comprehensive Qualification and Certification System** — national AI professional certification

## Institutional architecture

- **National Council for Artificial Intelligence (NCAI)** — central authority responsible for strategy preparation, approval, governance, and implementation oversight
- **Ministry of Communications and Information Technology (MCIT)** — line ministry
- **Information Technology Institute (ITI)** — referenced training institution
- **AI Working Group** under the AI ecosystem (drafting the ethical principles framework)
- **Egypt Artificial Intelligence Technology Centre** — referenced as one of the benchmarking partners by other African countries (cited by South Africa)

## Benchmark countries

The Second Edition explicitly benchmarks Egypt against six countries judged to be leading AI developers: **China, South Korea, United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Brazil**. Each benchmark country has its own CAF model rating reported in the Appendix. The **China comparison** is particularly extensive, citing China's "2522 framework" (2 foundations, 5 integrations, 2 capabilities, 2 environments) as a top-down strategic template.

## Regulation posture

Egypt has **no AI-specific Act yet** at the time of the Second Edition. The strategy commits to:

- **Egypt National AI Regulatory System (Initiative 1)** — comprehensive AI regulatory framework
- **Ethical AI framework activation** anchored to global standards (OECD, UNESCO, UN)
- **Data Privacy and Security (Initiative 9)** building on existing data protection law
- **AI Patent Granting System (Initiative 5)** — IP infrastructure
- **Local data sovereignty** — "keeping data inside its borders" [Conclusion, p.92]

The First Edition (2021) established the initial regulatory groundwork; the Second Edition operationalises it via Initiative 1.

## Continental and international positioning

Egypt positions itself as:

- **Leader in AI in Africa and the Arab region**
- **Hub for AI development in the Middle East and Africa (MEA)**
- **Active international player** with explicit commitment to UN, AU, Arab League, and bilateral cooperation
- **Regional research cooperation centre** (6,000 publications/year target)
- Active in **global AI competitiveness rankings** with the #20 target by 2030

## What Egypt does that Portugal's ANIA does NOT

1. **Second Edition status with explicit predecessor learning** — Egypt builds on its 2021 first edition, citing what worked and what changed (large generative models in 2022/2023). Portugal's January 2026 ANIA is a first articulation; there is no equivalent reflection-and-iteration cycle yet.

2. **Capacity Assessment Framework (CAF) with single national score and global ranking trajectory** — 89 indicators, scored against four international indices, with explicit 55.1 → 68.4 → 81.7 trajectory and ranking targets #45 → #32 → **#20**. Portugal cites international benchmarks but does not publish a single composite national score or ranking trajectory.

3. **National Foundation Models Development (Initiative 3)** as a standalone initiative in the Technology pillar, alongside (Initiative 7) High-Quality Sectoral Arabic Data Sets in the Data pillar. The pair is the model-plus-substrate commitment Portugal makes for AMALIA — but Egypt makes it explicit at the **sectoral Arabic data** level, not just at the model level.

4. **AI Patent Granting System (Initiative 5)** as a named instrument. Portugal does not name an AI-specific patent infrastructure; this would sit under the EU patent system but Portugal has not flagged AI-specific extensions.

5. **Cross-Discipline Course Development (Initiative 20)** as a named instrument for AI literacy across non-technical disciplines. Portugal has III.1, III.2, III.7, III.8 covering different talent angles but no equivalent named cross-disciplinary instrument.

6. **Comprehensive Qualification and Certification System (Initiative 21)** as a national AI professional certification track. Portugal does not name an equivalent national AI certification system.

7. **Local data sovereignty as an explicit commitment** ("keeping data inside its borders"). Portugal aligns to EU data-space frameworks but does not name an equivalent domestic-data-hosting commitment.

8. **Industry-Academia-Research Platforms (Initiative 15)** as a single named tripartite instrument. Portugal has sectoral AI centres (II.5) but not a single named tripartite platform of this kind.

9. **International Academic Alliances (Initiative 19)** as a standalone Talent-pillar instrument. Portugal has III initiatives covering talent but no equivalent named international-alliances instrument.

10. **NCAI as a single central authority** with strategy preparation + approval + governance + oversight responsibility. Portugal has the Centro para a IA Responsável (IV.3) but no equivalent single coordinating council with central authority over the whole agenda.

11. **Explicit "marginal population" KPI** — 26% of the marginal-population workforce to benefit from AI tools. Portugal's Talent pillar covers vulnerable groups indirectly but no equivalent KPI naming a marginal-population target.

12. **Six benchmark countries with CAF ratings published** (China, South Korea, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Brazil). Portugal's ANIA references EU peers but does not publish CAF-style comparative ratings.

## What Portugal's ANIA does that Egypt's strategy does NOT

1. **Per-initiative budgets** with named funding sources. Egypt's 21 initiatives carry CAF-improvement targets but no per-initiative budget breakdown in the published strategy.

2. **EU AI Act + GDPR as binding regulatory anchors** — Egypt's regulatory framework is being constructed in Initiative 1; it does not yet have an equivalent binding international framework.

3. **AI literacy citizen campaign (III.8)** — Egypt has Public Awareness of AI Development (Initiative 17) and 36% access target, but no equivalent named citizen-facing literacy campaign brand.

4. **Regulatory sandbox as a forward initiative (IV.5)** — Egypt names regulatory system construction (Initiative 1) but no explicit sandbox instrument.

5. **Sovereign-language LLM at named model level (AMALIA)** — Egypt names National Foundation Models Development (Initiative 3) but does not announce a named model brand for Arabic.

6. **Named strategic technology bets** (Cybersecurity, LLMs, AI+Robotics for Industry, Blue Economy, Responsible AI). Egypt names sectors and pillars but no equivalent enumeration of strategic technology bets at the same level of branding.

7. **Per-initiative start semesters (2S 2025 → 1S 2027)** — Egypt has 1-year/3-year/5-year aggregates but not per-initiative start semesters.

## Critique-lens conclusion

Egypt's Second Edition is the **most measurement-disciplined** national AI strategy in the comparator set. Its strongest distinctive features are (a) the **Capacity Assessment Framework with 89 indicators and a single composite score**, (b) the **explicit global-ranking trajectory target (#45 → #32 → #20)**, (c) the **second-edition status with explicit reflection on 2021 first edition learning**, (d) the **Arabic-first cultural and linguistic frame** (sectoral Arabic data sets + national foundation models), and (e) the **NCAI as a single central authority** with cradle-to-implementation responsibility.

Its weakest features for cross-comparison are (a) the **absence of per-initiative budgets**, (b) the **lack of named model-brand for the foundation models commitment**, and (c) the **discursive 95-page length** which makes operational navigation harder than Portugal's per-initiative cards.

### Top moves Portugal could import from Egypt

1. **Capacity Assessment Framework (CAF) with single composite score and global ranking trajectory**. Portugal does not currently publish a composite national AI score. Adopting a CAF-style framework (perhaps based on EU AI Index, OECD AI Observatory metrics, and Stanford HAI) with **target trajectory toward EU top-5 by 2030** would parallel Egypt's #20-global-target discipline.

2. **Second-Edition reflection-and-iteration model**. ANIA Jan 2026 is the first edition; Portugal should plan an explicit **ANIA 2027 Second Edition** that reflects on what worked, what changed (post-AI Act compliance reality, post-AMALIA deployment, post-PRR implementation), and recalibrates.

3. **Sectoral language data sets as a named initiative** parallel to AMALIA. Egypt's Initiative 7 (High-Quality Sectoral Arabic Data Sets) addresses the data substrate that the foundation model needs. Portugal's AMALIA (II.7) addresses the model, but a paired **"High-Quality Sectoral Portuguese Data Sets"** initiative under the Data pillar of a future ANIA would mirror Egypt's pairing and strengthen the AMALIA case.

4. **AI Patent Granting System / IP infrastructure for AI**. Portugal works within the EU patent system but could name an AI-specific IP capability (perhaps via INPI) for the same purpose Egypt's Initiative 5 addresses.

5. **Comprehensive Qualification and Certification System**. Portugal has IEFP and FCT but no named national AI professional certification system. Adopting one would give the Talent pillar (III) a binary accreditation instrument.

6. **Cross-Discipline Course Development as a named initiative**. Portugal has talent initiatives but no equivalent named cross-disciplinary AI-literacy track for non-technical disciplines (law, medicine, business, public administration).

7. **Marginal-population KPI** — 26% of Egypt's marginal workforce as explicit beneficiary target. Portugal does not name an equivalent vulnerable-population KPI. A Portuguese version (e.g., "X% of long-term unemployed receiving AI-enabled re-skilling") would give the Responsibility pillar (IV) a measurable inclusion anchor.

8. **NCAI-style single central coordinating council**. Portugal currently splits coordination across sectoral centres, the Centro para a IA Responsável, and various ministries. An NCAI-style single council under the Prime Minister's Office or DSI would parallel Egypt's clearer accountability chain.

### What Portugal should NOT copy

- **The 95-page length and discursive narrative** — Portugal's compact 22-page format with per-initiative cards is the better operational template.
- **The absence of per-initiative budgets** — Egypt's strategy lacks budget specificity at the initiative level; Portugal's per-initiative funding source is the better template.
- **The president-level launch theatrics** — Egypt's strategy launches with a foreword from the President of the Republic; Portuguese institutional culture handles this through ministerial framing, and importing the head-of-state framing would not fit.

### Strategic complementarity with other comparators

Egypt's measurement discipline and second-edition iteration model combine well with UK operational depth, Spain's per-initiative budget transparency, and Kenya's participatory methodology. An ideal Portuguese ANIA evolution drawing from Egypt specifically would adopt:

- **Egyptian CAF + global ranking trajectory** as a national measurement instrument
- **Egyptian Second-Edition reflection model** for ANIA 2027
- **Egyptian sectoral language data sets initiative** paired with AMALIA
- **Egyptian Comprehensive Qualification and Certification System** in the Talent pillar
- **Egyptian NCAI-style single central council** for clearer accountability
- **Egyptian Cross-Discipline Course Development** initiative
